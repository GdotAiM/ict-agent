# ICT Premium and Discount Zone Identification

For my first two years of trading, I sold tops and bought bottoms by feel. I lost more often than I won. The day I learned to draw a Fibonacci between my swing high and swing low and force myself to sell only above the 50% line and buy only below it, my win rate jumped without changing anything else about my analysis. That single rule is what ICT calls Premium and Discount.

It helps you decide where in a range a trade entry actually belongs — selling only when price is in premium territory, buying only when price is in discount territory.

In this guide, I will show you what premium and discount mean using a simple real-world example, the exact Fibonacci settings used to identify the zones, how to mark a Premium Zone in a bearish trend, how to mark a Discount Zone in a bullish trend, and how to trade them with proper confirmation.

Do not worry — once you know which side of 50% to trade from, this becomes a one-line discipline rule.

## The Meaning of Premium and Discount

Let us understand it with an example.

Suppose you want to buy a car with a market value of $120,000. The basic or fair value of the car is $120,000. **Premium** is the amount paid above the basic or fair value, so anything above $120k is a premium price. **Discount** is the price less than the fair or basic value, so the price of the car below $120k is a discounted price.

Now ask yourself: would you buy the car at premium or at discount? Of course at discount.

The same logic applies to trading. To buy anything, you want to buy it at discount. To sell anything, you want to sell it at premium.

Premium and Discount zones simply project that intuition onto a price chart using Fibonacci.

## Fibonacci Settings for ICT Premium and Discount Zone

Use the following inputs in the Fibonacci retracement tool for identification of ICT Premium and Discount zones:

1| Start  
---|---  
0.5| 50% Retracement  
0| End  
  
The 50% level is the midline. Anything above 50% is the Premium Zone. Anything below 50% is the Discount Zone. The two extreme zones (above 79% and below 21%) are the deepest premium and discount, respectively — the highest-probability entry areas.

## What is the ICT Premium Zone?

As discussed earlier, traders use the Premium Zone to sell. So the ICT Premium Zone is utilised in a bearish trend mostly.

To identify the ICT Premium Zone in a bearish trend, draw the Fibonacci retracement tool from [Swing High](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-high-explained/> "ICT Swing High") to [Swing Low](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-low-formation/> "ICT Swing Low").

The area above the 50% Fib retracement level is the Premium Zone, where you will look for sell trade execution after a confirmation like an [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/> "ICT Market Structure Shift").

![ICT Premium Zone example: Fibonacci drawn from Swing High to Swing Low in a bearish trend with the area above 50% marked as the premium zone for sell entries](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ict-premium-zone.png)

## What is the ICT Discount Zone?

Traders use the Discount Zone to buy. So the ICT Discount Zone is used in a bullish trend primarily.

To identify the ICT Discount Zone in a bullish trend, draw the Fibonacci retracement tool from Swing Low to Swing High.

The area below the 50% Fib retracement level is the Discount Zone, where you will look for buy trade execution after a confirmation like an ICT Market Structure Shift.

![ICT Discount Zone example: Fibonacci drawn from Swing Low to Swing High in a bullish trend with the area below 50% marked as the discount zone for buy entries](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ict-discount-zone.png)

## How to Trade ICT Premium and Discount Zones — Step by Step

The unified trade flow:

  1. **Confirm the higher-timeframe trend.** Bullish for buys, bearish for sells.
  2. **Identify the relevant swing high and swing low** on your bias timeframe.
  3. **Draw the Fibonacci tool** — high to low for a bearish trend, low to high for a bullish trend.
  4. **Mark the zones.** Above 50% = Premium. Below 50% = Discount.
  5. **Wait for price to retrace into the relevant zone** — Premium Zone for sells, Discount Zone for buys.
  6. **Drop to a lower timeframe** and look for an ICT Market Structure Shift in your trade direction as confirmation.
  7. **Enter on the close of the MSS candle** or on a clean retest. Stop loss beyond the swing extreme that produced the zone.
  8. **Target the opposite side of the range** or the next opposing PD Array.

**Pro Tip** — The deepest part of either zone (above 79% in premium, below 21% in discount) usually offers the highest-probability entries. When a higher-timeframe [PD Array](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/> "ICT PD Array") like an order block, fair value gap, or breaker block sits inside the deep zone, the confluence is what makes the reaction sharp. Skip entries in the middle of the range — that is no-man’s-land where neither buyers nor sellers have an edge.

## Bonus Tip: Pairing Premium and Discount With PD Arrays

Premium and Discount zones tell you _which half of the range_ to take entries from. PD Arrays tell you _which exact level_ inside that half to enter at. Combined, they narrow your entry from a general zone to a precise institutional level.

For longs in a bullish trend: find a Discount Zone, then look for an order block, fair value gap, or breaker block inside it. Take entry at the PD Array level after MSS confirmation.

For shorts in a bearish trend: find a Premium Zone, then look for a PD Array inside it. Same flow, opposite direction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I trade solely using ICT Premium and Discount zones?

No. You cannot trade solely using Premium and Discount zones. Premium and Discount zones are part of the ICT trading strategy, so you should use them along with other ICT concepts while keeping the current market structure in view.

### Does the Premium and Discount Zone change after a break of structure?

Yes. After every break of structure, you have to mark a new Premium and Discount zone. In a bearish trend, when price breaks the low and marks a new low, identify the new Premium Zone by drawing Fibonacci from the latest swing high to swing low for fresh sell entries. In a bullish trend, when price breaks the high and makes a higher high, look for a fresh Discount Zone by drawing Fibonacci from the fresh low to fresh high for fresh buy entries.

### Which markets do ICT Premium and Discount zones work in?

ICT Premium and Discount zones work in every market where price is delivered live on a price chart — crypto, Forex, indices, and stocks. The Fibonacci-based logic does not depend on the asset class.

### What is the difference between Premium and Discount?

Premium is any price above the 50% midline of the range — the half of the range where you look for sell entries. Discount is any price below the 50% midline — the half where you look for buy entries. The midline is the fair value reference point.

### What confirmation do I need before entering a Premium or Discount Zone trade?

An ICT Market Structure Shift on a lower timeframe in the direction of your bias. Without that confirmation, the zone is just an area of interest — not a trade.

### Where do I place stop loss on a Premium and Discount Zone trade?

Beyond the swing extreme that produced the zone — above the swing high for a Premium Zone short, below the swing low for a Discount Zone long. The swing extreme is your invalidation: if price closes through it, the trade thesis has failed.

### Can I use Premium and Discount zones on every timeframe?

Yes, but the deeper the timeframe, the more reliable the zone. Daily and 4-hour zones are most reliable for swing trades. For intraday entries, mark zones on the 1-hour or 15-minute, and use 5-minute charts for execution after the MSS confirmation.
