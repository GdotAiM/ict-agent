#### What is Forex?

The word forex is derived from two words — foreign and exchange. The word foreign here refers to **foreign currencies** , so it is basically the exchange of various currencies. You give one currency and take the other one.

In the old days, this exchange of currencies used to be for traveling to other countries or purchasing goods from abroad. All this exchange of currencies used to happen in physical locations like banks or money exchanges.

You must be wondering how you can make money from the exchange of these currencies. Take a deep breath — the world has evolved. Until now you have just understood what forex is. Your money will be generated in the next step of forex trading.

#### What is Forex Trading?

Forex trading is the process of buying a currency with the intent of selling it when its price goes up. It is just like buying any physical asset in the market and then selling it once its price goes up. The hike in the price of any particular asset is dependent on demand and supply, along with other various economic factors.

The same is the case of currencies in forex trading. You buy any particular currency based on your analysis of various economic and technical factors, with the belief that price of this currency will go up in the near future. Once the price goes up, you make the profit.

But forex trading is not all about profits. You can have losses too if the price of your bought currency goes down.

This buying and selling of currencies happens in pairs. Understanding pairs now will get too complicated for you — but I have covered that in the next section on what is traded in forex.

For the purpose of buying and selling these currencies digitally from the comfort of your bed, different mobile and web apps provide services. These digital facilitators of your currency trades are called brokers in the world of forex trading. They receive your orders digitally and provide them to banks and other financial institutions.

More or less, forex trading happening nowadays is just like the exchange of physical currencies that happened in the old days, only at banks and money exchanges.

The only difference is that today exchange happens digitally via brokers, and the major purpose is to earn money on the exchange of currencies — unlike the old days where the purpose was to import goods from a certain country.

You have now understood why it is called **forex trading** — because here exchange of foreign currencies happens just for trading.

#### What is the Forex Market?

The overall ecosystem of buying and selling currencies electronically is called the forex market. You will be surprised to know that this forex market has no physical location, no office, and no central international regulatory authority.

But this does not mean that online forex brokers are not regulated anywhere. Good brokers, which are trustable, are regulated by the relevant finance authorities of the country in which they are operating. To keep your money safe, you have to always go with regulated brokers.

Due to lack of regulations in some countries, some brokers have to go unregulated — but this does not mean that they are a scam.

#### What is Traded in Forex

As explained in the definition of forex trading, different currency pairs are traded in forex. For example, one of the pairs is USD/JPY. When you buy this pair, you have actually bought USD against Japanese Yen.

You must be thinking about the role of the Japanese Yen here. In fact, in the background you bought JPY first, and then with that JPY you bought USD.

If the price of USD goes up and you close that trade, you can sell that USD back to the market and you will get more JPY than you initially invested.

Similarly, you can also sell USD/JPY. This sell actually means you have bought the Japanese Yen with the USD. Once the price of JPY goes up, you can close the trade and earn the profit this way too.

#### What is the Size of the Forex Market?

Forex market is the largest market in the world, with daily trading volume of 6.6 trillion USD according to the Central Bank Survey of FX conducted in 2019. This 6.6 trillion means this amount of currency is bought and sold every day. The number has continued to grow since then.

You have already got the numbers of daily trading volume, so the sky is the limit theoretically. But practically speaking, based on my experience, on average any good forex trader can earn 10% of their invested capital per month. This number can vary, but a professional forex trader earns approximately that much.

That will not be a constant journey. Some months you will be earning 20 to 50%, or even 100% of your invested capital — but for some months you will be losing too at the same pace. It is important to mention that controlling your greed in this market is a very important aspect of learning forex trading.

In sophisticated language: **risk management is the key to success.**

#### Forex Terminology

Your next step in the journey of forex trading should be learning some basic terminology, which I have listed below with compact, easy-to-understand definitions.

**(01) Buy / Ask** — Buy and Ask are similar terms. Buy is the minimum price at which you will be able to purchase any currency pair in your broker account.

**(02) Sell / Bid** — Sell and Bid are similar terms. Sell is the maximum price at which you will be able to sell any currency pair in your broker account.

**(03) Lot** — Lot is the quantity of currency you will be purchasing or selling. It is just like a unit of quantity.

**(04) Leverage** — Leverage is the buying power provided to you by your broker so you can buy or sell more currency than the available amount in your account.

**(05) Pip** — Pip is the smallest change in value of any currency pair.

**(06) Take Profit Limit / TP** — A feature provided by your broker to set limits in your account to close any opened trade once it reaches a certain profit limit.

**(07) Stop Loss / SL** — A feature provided by your broker to limit your loss. Once you set a stop loss limit, your trades are closed automatically once they hit a certain loss limit.

**(08) Spread** — Technically its definition may vary, but simply it is the fee charged to you by the broker for opening your trades.

**(09) Fundamental Analysis** — Analysis and prediction of any currency pair price in the near future, based on the data of economic events worldwide.

**(10) Technical Analysis** — Analysis and prediction of any currency pair price in the near future, based on the historical data of forex charts.

#### Common Beginner Mistakes in Forex

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders are new to forex.

  1. **Trading without a stop loss.** The single fastest way to blow an account. Every trade must have a stop loss before it is opened.
  2. **Over-leveraging.** Leverage amplifies losses just as much as gains. Use the smallest leverage your broker allows for your size.
  3. **Skipping the demo phase.** Trade on a demo account for at least 1–3 months before risking real money.
  4. **Trading without a plan.** A trading plan with entry rules, stop rules, target rules and risk-per-trade rules is mandatory.
  5. **Revenge trading.** Doubling down after a loss to “make it back” is the second-fastest way to blow an account.
  6. **Risking more than 1–2% per trade.** Even good strategies have losing streaks. Risk small per trade so the inevitable streak does not wipe you out.

#### FAQs about Forex Trading

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about forex trading.

**What is forex trading in simple terms?**

Forex trading is buying one currency and selling another in the hope that the price moves in your favor. It is the same idea as exchanging dollars for yen at the airport — but done digitally through a broker for profit.

**What is the forex market?**

The forex market is the worldwide electronic ecosystem of buying and selling currencies. It has no physical location and no central international regulator; brokers are regulated by the finance authority of each country they operate in.

**How big is the forex market?**

Daily trading volume was 6.6 trillion USD per day in the 2019 Central Bank Survey of FX, and has continued to grow. It is the largest financial market in the world.

**How much money can I make from forex trading?**

A professional forex trader averages around 10% of invested capital per month, but it is not consistent — some months 20–50% or more, other months losses at the same pace. Risk management is the key to long-term success.

**Do I need to know finance to start forex trading?**

No — the basics of forex can be learned without any finance background. The hard part is the discipline and risk management, not the technical knowledge.

**What are currency pairs?**

Currencies are traded in pairs — for example USD/JPY. Buying the pair means buying USD against JPY; selling the pair means buying JPY against USD. Every trade involves two currencies.

**What is a pip?**

A pip is the smallest change in the value of a currency pair. For most pairs, one pip is 0.0001 of the price. Profit and loss in forex are measured in pips.

**What is leverage?**

Leverage is the buying power your broker provides — letting you control a larger position than your account size. It amplifies both gains and losses, so use it carefully.

**What is the spread?**

The spread is the fee your broker charges to open a trade — the difference between the buy and sell prices. Smaller spreads are better for active trading.

**How do I start forex trading?**

(1) Open an account with a regulated broker. (2) Practice on a demo account for 1–3 months. (3) Learn a trading framework (I recommend ICT — start with market structure, support/resistance, BOS, CHOCH, FVG). (4) Use strict risk management — 1% to 2% per trade with a stop loss.

**Is forex trading legal?**

In most countries, yes — forex trading through regulated brokers is legal. Some countries impose restrictions or require specific licensing. Check your local regulations before opening an account.

**What is the difference between fundamental and technical analysis?**

Fundamental analysis predicts currency prices from economic data and global events (interest rates, GDP, employment). Technical analysis predicts prices from historical chart patterns and price action. Most professional traders use both.

**How long does it take to become profitable in forex?**

Realistically, 6 to 24 months of focused study and demo trading before consistent profitability. Anyone promising faster results is selling, not teaching.
