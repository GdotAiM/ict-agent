# ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap Projection and Protraction

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-enigma-fair-value-gap/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-enigma-fair-value-gap/)

![ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap diagram showing the origin candle, the volume imbalance, and the projected reaction zones drawn ahead of price](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ict-enigma.png)

The ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap Projection model is one of the most advanced concepts introduced by Inner Circle Trader. Unlike the traditional [Fair Value Gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy/>) strategy, where you wait for an imbalance to form before trading it, the Enigma model tries to project the future institutional reaction zones before price ever gets there.

That is the whole appeal. Instead of reacting after the move has already happened, you anticipate where smart money is likely to become active and have your levels drawn in advance.

There is one honest caveat worth stating up front. ICT explained the ideas of Projection and Protraction publicly, but he never released a complete mechanical model with exact entry, stop loss and take profit rules. So this guide combines his public teaching with proven ICT execution concepts to turn it into a practical, repeatable framework you can actually trade.

#### What is the ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap?

A traditional Fair Value Gap is an imbalance created when a strong displacement candle leaves an inefficient area between three consecutive candles. The Enigma Fair Value Gap takes that idea one step further. Instead of waiting for the gap to form and then trading its retracement, the Enigma model projects where future institutional delivery is expected to occur.

The key difference is what it measures. Rather than the wick to wick gap, the Enigma model focuses on the volume imbalance between the first candle and the displacement candle. Specifically, the imbalance between:

  * The **close of candle 1**
  * The **open of candle 2**

That area is the true institutional inefficiency created when aggressive buying or selling entered the market. Because the imbalance originates from candle 2, ICT calls candle 2 the origin candle of the Enigma model. An example is shown below:

![ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap example marking the volume imbalance between the close of candle 1 and the open of candle 2, the origin candle](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ICT-Enigma-FVG.png)

**Why candle 2 is so important**

Candle 2 serves three purposes:

  * It identifies the beginning of institutional [displacement](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-displacement-move/>)
  * It defines the volume imbalance used in the Enigma calculation
  * It becomes the anchor point for the Projection and the Protraction

Without a valid displacement candle, there is no Enigma setup.

#### Bullish Enigma Fair Value Gap

A bullish Enigma FVG should have the following characteristics:

  * A strong bullish displacement candle (candle 2)
  * A visible volume imbalance between the close of candle 1 and the open of candle 2
  * A traditional bullish Fair Value Gap formed by the three candle sequence
  * A clear [break in market structure](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) or strong directional expansion

The volume imbalance inside candle 2 becomes the measurement used to project future institutional buying zones. An example is shown below:

![Bullish ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap example, a strong bullish displacement candle with a volume imbalance and a bullish FVG](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/BULLISH-ENIGMA-FVG.png)

#### Bearish Enigma Fair Value Gap

A bearish Enigma FVG follows the opposite logic. It should include:

  * A strong bearish displacement candle (candle 2)
  * A volume imbalance between the close of candle 1 and the open of candle 2
  * A bearish Fair Value Gap created by the three candle sequence
  * Strong bearish displacement showing institutional selling

The volume imbalance created by candle 2 becomes the origin for projecting future bearish reaction zones. An example is shown below:

![Bearish ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap example, a strong bearish displacement candle with a volume imbalance and a bearish FVG](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/BEARISH-ENIGMA-FVG.png)

#### ICT Enigma Projection

Projection measures the entire displacement leg that begins with the displacement candle (candle 2). The purpose is to estimate how far institutional price delivery may extend from the origin of the displacement.

#### How to Draw the Bullish Projection

**Step 1: Identify the displacement candle**

Locate a strong bullish displacement candle (candle 2). A valid displacement candle should:

  * Have a large bullish body
  * Create a Fair Value Gap
  * Create a volume imbalance
  * Show aggressive institutional buying

This candle becomes the origin of the projection.

**Step 2: Select the Fibonacci retracement tool**

Set up the Fibonacci retracement tool with the coordinates 0.00, 1.00 and 1.25. The Enigma model uses the Fibonacci differently from a traditional retracement. Instead of measuring an entire market swing, it measures the displacement leg that starts from candle 2.

**Step 3: Draw the projection**

For a bullish Enigma projection, draw the Fibonacci from the open of candle 2 to the low of candle 3, which is the point where the initial displacement leg completes. This measures the full displacement generated by the origin candle. The projected Fibonacci levels then extend beyond the displacement and estimate the future institutional reaction zones.

**Step 4: Read the projection levels**

The most important projected level is the 1.25 Fibonacci level. ICT refers to this area as the Low Hanging Fruit, representing the first anticipated institutional reaction. Higher projected levels create the Extreme Projection, defining the outer boundary of the expected reaction zone. Do not treat these levels as exact reversal prices. Read them as high probability points of interest where you begin looking for confirmation. A bullish Enigma projection example is shown below:

![Bullish ICT Enigma projection example, Fibonacci drawn from the open of candle 2 to the low of candle 3 with the 1.25 Low Hanging Fruit level projected ahead](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/bullish-enigma-projection.png)

#### Bearish Enigma Projection

The bearish model follows the exact opposite procedure. Draw the Fibonacci from the open of candle 2 to the high of candle 3, using the same 0.00, 1.00 and 1.25 coordinates. This measures the bearish displacement leg, and the 1.25 Fibonacci level represents the projected reaction price. A bearish Enigma projection example is shown below:

![Bearish ICT Enigma projection example, Fibonacci drawn from the open of candle 2 to the high of candle 3 with the 1.25 level projected below price](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/bearish-enigma-projection.png)

#### ICT Enigma Protraction

While Projection measures the displacement, Protraction measures the complete institutional imbalance. Instead of beginning at the open of candle 2, Protraction begins at the close of candle 1, which is the start of the volume imbalance. This creates a wider institutional measurement by combining both the volume imbalance and the displacement.

#### How to Draw the Bullish Protraction

Draw the Fibonacci retracement tool from the close of candle 1 to the low of candle 3 of the Fair Value Gap, using the coordinates 0.00, 1.00 and 1.25. This measurement includes:

  * The volume imbalance
  * The complete displacement leg

After drawing the measurement, extend the Fibonacci levels into future price action. These protracted levels stay on the chart until price eventually revisits them. A bullish Enigma protraction example is shown below:

![Bullish ICT Enigma protraction example, Fibonacci drawn from the close of candle 1 to the low of candle 3 combining the volume imbalance and the displacement](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Bullish-enigma-protraction.png)

#### Bearish Enigma Protraction

For the bearish version, draw the Fibonacci from the close of candle 1 to the high of candle 3. This measures the complete bearish volume imbalance together with the displacement. The 1.25 Fibonacci level is then extended into future candles until price revisits the reaction zone. A bearish Enigma protraction example is shown below:

![Bearish ICT Enigma protraction example, Fibonacci drawn from the close of candle 1 to the high of candle 3 with the 1.25 level projected into future candles](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/bearish-enigma-protraction.png)

#### How to Trade the Enigma Projection

The Projection and Protraction levels are not immediate entry signals. They identify the areas where institutional activity is likely to occur. When price revisits a projected reaction zone, you wait for confirmation before entering. Never enter on the level alone.

**Bullish confirmation**

At a bullish reaction zone, look for:

  * A sell side [liquidity](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/liquidity-in-forex-trading/>) sweep
  * A bullish [Market Structure Shift (MSS)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>)
  * Strong bullish displacement
  * A newly formed bullish Fair Value Gap
  * Extra confluence such as an [IFVG](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-inversion-fair-value-gap/>), [SMT divergence](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-smt-divergence-smart-money-technique/>), or a BPR

Enter on the first retracement into the newly formed bullish Fair Value Gap after confirmation. Place the stop loss below the liquidity sweep or the projected reaction zone. Target internal liquidity first, then external buy side liquidity.

**Bearish confirmation**

At a bearish reaction zone, look for:

  * A buy side liquidity sweep
  * A bearish Market Structure Shift (MSS)
  * Strong bearish displacement
  * A newly formed bearish Fair Value Gap
  * Extra confluence such as an IFVG, SMT divergence, or a BPR

Enter on the first retracement into the newly formed bearish Fair Value Gap after confirmation. Place the stop loss above the liquidity sweep or the projected reaction zone. Target internal liquidity first, then external sell side liquidity.

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make with the Enigma Model

The Enigma model is powerful but easy to misuse. These are the errors that ruin it most often.

  1. **Anchoring from the wrong candle.** Projection starts from the open of candle 2, and Protraction starts from the close of candle 1. Mixing the two anchors gives you the wrong levels every time. Know which one you are drawing before you start.
  2. **Trading the 1.25 level as an exact reversal.** The projected levels are points of interest, not automatic entries. Price can push past the Low Hanging Fruit into the Extreme Projection. Always wait for confirmation at the zone.
  3. **Skipping the displacement requirement.** No strong displacement candle means no valid origin candle, which means no Enigma setup. A weak, overlapping candle does not qualify no matter how clean the Fibonacci looks.
  4. **Entering without a lower timeframe confirmation.** The reaction zone only tells you where to look. The trade is triggered by a liquidity sweep, an MSS, and a fresh Fair Value Gap, not by price simply tagging the level.
  5. **Forcing the model on a quiet market.** The Enigma projection works best after genuine institutional displacement. In slow, ranging conditions the projected levels rarely deliver, so stand aside until real displacement appears.

#### Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the ICT Enigma Fair Value Gap?**

It is an advanced ICT concept that projects future institutional reaction zones ahead of price. Instead of trading a Fair Value Gap after it forms, the Enigma model measures the volume imbalance around the displacement candle and projects where smart money is likely to become active next.

**What is the difference between Projection and Protraction?**

Projection measures only the displacement leg and starts from the open of candle 2. Protraction measures the complete institutional imbalance and starts from the close of candle 1, so it includes both the volume imbalance and the displacement. Protraction produces the wider measurement.

**What Fibonacci settings does the Enigma model use?**

Use the retracement tool with the coordinates 0.00, 1.00 and 1.25. The 1.25 level is the key projected reaction area, which ICT calls the Low Hanging Fruit. Levels beyond it form the Extreme Projection.

**What is the Low Hanging Fruit level?**

The 1.25 Fibonacci projection is the Low Hanging Fruit, the first anticipated institutional reaction zone. It is a high probability point of interest, not a guaranteed reversal, so you still wait for confirmation before entering.

**How do I enter a trade using the Enigma projection?**

Wait for price to revisit the projected zone, then look for a liquidity sweep, a Market Structure Shift, strong displacement, and a newly formed Fair Value Gap. Enter on the first retracement into that fresh FVG, with the stop beyond the sweep and targets at internal then external liquidity.

**Is the Enigma model an official ICT strategy?**

The Projection and Protraction concepts come from ICT’s public teaching, but he never released a complete mechanical model with fixed entry, stop and target rules. The execution framework in this guide combines those concepts with broader ICT principles to make the idea tradable.
