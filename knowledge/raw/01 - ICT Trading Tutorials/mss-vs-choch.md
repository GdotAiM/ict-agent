# MSS vs CHOCH — Smart Money Trading Differences, Bullish/Bearish Variants & Trade Flow

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/mss-vs-choch/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/mss-vs-choch/)

![MSS vs CHOCH — Market Structure Shift breaks a swing high or swing low for an initial reversal signal, while CHOCH breaks the previous market structure to confirm a long-term trend change](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/MSS-vs-CHOCH.png)

MSS vs CHOCH is one of the most common confusion points in smart money trading — both involve a structural break, both signal a shift in price delivery, but they sit at different layers of the market structure and they call for different trade decisions.

In this guide I walk you through the MSS vs CHOCH comparison — the definition of each, the bullish and bearish variants, the key differences, where each one fits in the trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ. Real market examples are included throughout.

#### What is Market Structure Shift (MSS)?

To understand the ICT-MSS you must know about the [ICT Swing Low](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-low-formation/>) and [ICT Swing High](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-high-explained/>).

Market Structure Shift is an ICT concept which indicates the short-term change in the delivery of price, indicated by the break of an [ICT Swing Point](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-high-explained/>).

It is the initial signal of a changing market direction, which may turn into a long-term change in the trend of price.

Most traders confuse it with the change of character (CHOCH), and that confusion may result in losing trades.

**(I) Bullish Market Structure Shift** happens when price breaks a [Swing High Point](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-high-explained/>). It indicates that price delivery for the short term has changed to buy-side.

![Bullish ICT Market Structure Shift example — price breaks the most recent swing high signaling the initial bullish shift in short-term delivery](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/ict-Market-sTRUCTUREsHIFT.png)

This may be due to price going to take inducement or to reach a higher-timeframe [PD Array](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/>), and it may result in a Change of Character as well.

**(II) Bearish Market Structure Shift** happens when price breaks a [Swing Low Point](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-swing-low-formation/>). It indicates that price delivery for the short term has changed to sell-side.

![Bearish ICT Market Structure Shift example — price breaks the most recent swing low signaling the initial bearish shift in short-term delivery](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/ICT-MSS.png)

This may be due to price going to take inducement or to reach a higher-timeframe [PD Array](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/>), and it may result in a Change of Character as well.

#### What is Change of Character (CHOCH)?

To understand the Change of Character (CHOCH) you must know about market structure and the valid highs and lows of price, for which I will refer you to my [CHOCH guide](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/change-of-character-choch-in-trading/>).

Change of Character (CHOCH) is a smart money concept which indicates the long-term change in the delivery of price — the change of market trend itself.

It is indicated by the break of previous market structure in the opposite direction — bullish to bearish or bearish to bullish.

![ICT CHOCH example — break of the previous higher low after a bullish leg, signaling the long-term change of character from bullish to bearish](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/choch.png)

**(I) In a bullish market structure** , Change of Character (CHOCH) is indicated by the break of the previous higher low resulting in a new lower low.

It indicates that the trend of price has changed from bullish to bearish.

**(II) In a bearish market structure** , Change of Character (CHOCH) is indicated by the break of the previous lower high resulting in a new higher high.

It indicates that the trend of price has changed from bearish to bullish.

#### MSS vs CHOCH — The Key Differences

Both MSS and CHOCH involve a structural break, but they sit at different layers and tell you different things.

**(1) The level that gets broken.** MSS breaks a swing high or swing low — a recent intraday extreme. CHOCH breaks the most recent higher low (in a bullish trend) or lower high (in a bearish trend) — a structural extreme.

**(2) The signal strength.** MSS is the INITIAL signal that delivery has shifted in the short term. CHOCH is the CONFIRMATION that the long-term trend has changed.

**(3) The order of appearance.** MSS often comes first inside a leg. CHOCH typically requires the MSS to be followed by additional structure that breaks the higher low or lower high.

**(4) The trade decision.** MSS is used as the lower-timeframe entry trigger inside a higher-timeframe PD array. CHOCH is used as the higher-timeframe bias flip — the moment to start looking for setups in the new direction.

**(5) The reliability.** MSS can fail and resolve back into the prior trend. CHOCH has a higher base rate of marking a real trend change because it requires the structural extreme to break.

#### Where Each One Fits in the Trade Flow

I use both MSS and CHOCH together — they are not competing concepts, they are different layers of the same structure.

**Higher-timeframe CHOCH** tells me the bias has flipped — bullish to bearish or vice versa.

**Higher-timeframe BOS** tells me the existing bias is continuing.

**Lower-timeframe MSS** tells me the entry trigger has fired inside a higher-timeframe PD array.

In practice — wait for the higher-timeframe CHOCH or BOS, identify the higher-timeframe PD array, drop to the lower timeframe, wait for the MSS at the PD array, then enter on the MSS retest.

#### Step-by-Step Trade Flow Using MSS and CHOCH

This is the exact sequence I run when using both concepts together.

  1. **Set the higher-timeframe bias.** 1-day and 4-hour structure — has there been a recent CHOCH? If yes, the bias has flipped. If no, the existing trend is intact.
  2. **Mark the higher-timeframe PD array.** Order block, FVG or breaker that price is drawn to inside the bias-aligned zone.
  3. **Wait for price to reach the PD array.** Confirmation that the algorithm is delivering toward the level.
  4. **Drop to the lower timeframe (5-minute or 15-minute).** For the MSS read.
  5. **Wait for the lower-timeframe MSS.** A clean break of the most recent counter-trend swing point in the bias direction.
  6. **Mark the post-MSS PD array.** The fair value gap or order block left behind by the displacement after the MSS.
  7. **Enter on the retest of the post-MSS PD array.** In the higher-timeframe direction.
  8. **Set the stop.** Above or below the MSS swing high or low (depending on direction).
  9. **Take profit at the next draw on liquidity.** Old high or low, relative equal level, or higher-timeframe FVG.

#### Does CHOCH Always Result in a Change of Trend?

Trading is the game of probabilities — nothing is certain here. Every concept including CHOCH gives a high probability of happening, but it may fail too.

That does not hurt — because the success ratio of CHOCH is greater than its failure ratio, and at the end it results in profits.

When CHOCH does fail, it usually fails by being a deep retracement that gets reclaimed before the structural extreme. The fix is to wait for the post-CHOCH MSS retest before committing — that lets the failed CHOCHs filter themselves out before you enter.

#### Best Pairs for Trading MSS and CHOCH

Both MSS and CHOCH work on every major pair — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD. The cleanest prints come from instruments that produce strong displacement legs.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, both frameworks map cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for MSS triggers because the algorithmic delivery is most concentrated there.

#### Common Mistakes Around MSS and CHOCH

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start working with both concepts.

  1. **Trading the MSS as if it were a CHOCH.** MSS is a short-term shift. Treating it as a full trend reversal flips the bias too early and produces counter-trend trades.
  2. **Waiting for CHOCH before entering on every trade.** CHOCH is the bias flip — not the entry. The entry is the lower-timeframe MSS at a bias-aligned PD array.
  3. **Confusing MSS with BOS.** MSS breaks a swing high or low. BOS breaks the previous structure AFTER taking inducement. See my [BOS guide](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/break-of-structure-bos/>) for the BOS-vs-MSS comparison.
  4. **Treating every wick as a structural break.** Both MSS and CHOCH require a candle to close beyond the structure — a wick alone is a stop hunt, not a confirmed break.
  5. **Skipping the higher-timeframe context.** An MSS aligned with the higher-timeframe trend is a high-probability trigger. An MSS against the higher-timeframe trend is unreliable.
  6. **Treating CHOCH and MSS as competing concepts.** They are different layers of the same structure. Use CHOCH for the bias and MSS for the trigger — not one or the other.

#### FAQs about MSS vs CHOCH

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about MSS vs CHOCH.

**What is the difference between MSS and CHOCH?**

MSS breaks a swing high or swing low — short-term delivery shift. CHOCH breaks the most recent higher low (bullish trend) or lower high (bearish trend) — long-term trend change. MSS is the initial signal; CHOCH is the confirmation.

**Is MSS the same as BOS?**

No. MSS breaks a swing point. BOS breaks the previous structure AFTER taking inducement. MSS is the initial reversal signal; BOS is the continuation confirmation.

**Can MSS happen without CHOCH?**

Yes — MSS can happen and resolve back into the prior trend without ever producing a CHOCH. That is exactly when MSS becomes a stop-hunt trap rather than a real reversal.

**Can CHOCH happen without MSS?**

In practice no — CHOCH typically follows an MSS by definition, because the move that breaks the higher low or lower high will first break a swing point along the way.

**Which one do I trade — MSS or CHOCH?**

Both, but for different reasons. Use CHOCH to set the higher-timeframe bias. Use MSS as the lower-timeframe entry trigger inside a bias-aligned PD array.

**Where do I place the stop loss when trading the MSS?**

Above the MSS swing high (for sell trades) or below the MSS swing low (for buy trades), with a small buffer.

**What timeframe is best for spotting MSS?**

5-minute and 15-minute for the MSS trigger. 1-day and 4-hour for the CHOCH-based bias.

**Does CHOCH always result in a change of trend?**

No — trading is the game of probabilities. CHOCH gives a high probability of trend change but may fail. Its success ratio is greater than its failure ratio.

**What if MSS fires but CHOCH never confirms?**

Then the move was a short-term shift that did not reach the structural extreme. The original trend is still in place, and the next setup should align with it.

**Do MSS and CHOCH work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook MSS and CHOCH prints, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**How does CHOCH relate to BOS?**

CHOCH and BOS are opposites in direction. CHOCH = trend reversal. BOS = trend continuation. In a bullish leg, CHOCH is the break of the most recent higher low; BOS is the break of the most recent higher high.
