2. Foundational Principle: Time-Based Candlesticks Only
ICT strongly criticizes non-time-based bars (range bars, volume bars, tick charts, etc.) because they distort the
natural order of price delivery and reduce predictive power. The algorithm operates on fixed daily ranges and
the OHLC data of each individual candlestick. Every single daily candle is a specific data array that the
algorithm can reference when deciding where to deliver price next.
When analyzing any market, the first action is always to establish the relevant look-back range on the daily
chart using pure time-based candles. Lower timeframes (5-minute, 15-minute, etc.) are then used for precise
entry timing once the higher-timeframe PD arrays and bias are identified.
3. The 20-Day Look-Back Range \227 The Core Framing Tool
For day trading and short-term trading, the 
20-day look-back
 is the primary (and often only necessary) data
range ICT uses. Longer look-backs (40-day or 60-day) exist in the broader IPDA framework but are secondary
for most active trading. The process is:
Step-by-Step Construction
1.
 On the daily chart, count back exactly 20 trading days from the current (or most recent completed) day.
2.
 Identify the absolute highest high and lowest low within those 20 days. This forms the dealing range / data
range.
3.
 Draw a box or use horizontal lines from the high to the low of that range for visual reference (optional but
helpful while learning).
4.
 Grade the entire range into equal quadrants using Fibonacci or simple percentage levels: 0% (low), 25%,
50% (equilibrium / midpoint), 75%, and 100% (high).
5.
 The upper quadrant (75\226100%), equilibrium (around 50%), and lower quadrant (0\22625%) become the
primary focus zones. The \223heart\224 of most actionable price delivery usually resides between the 25% and 75%
levels.
ICT states that this 20-day graded range is the algorithm\222s current reference frame. You do not need to concern
yourself with PD arrays outside this active range until price closes beyond the extremes on a closing basis. This
dramatically reduces noise and focuses attention on the highest-probability levels.
Important Insight: When the market has been trending higher within the 20-day range, focus
primarily on PD arrays in the upper half / premium side for potential shorts or on lower-quadrant
arrays for continuation longs after retracements. The reverse applies in downtrends. Extremes (very
top or bottom quadrants) are only prioritized when a full reversal or major liquidity run is expected.
4. Quadrant Grading of Ranges and Individual Arrays
Every significant range and every individual PD array (especially wicks) can itself be graded into quadrants.
This provides multi-layered precision:
\177 
Range Quadrants
 (from the 20-day high\226low): Lower quadrant, Equilibrium, Upper quadrant.
\177 
Wick / Array Quadrants
: For any candle wick or inefficiency, project its own 0/25/50/75/100% levels forward
in time. Price frequently reacts precisely at these projected quadrant levels of prior premium or discount wicks.
Example from the session (Dollar Index): A premium wick on a prior daily candle is graded; its upper quadrant,
equilibrium, and lower quadrant levels later act as precise reaction points on the 5-minute chart (e.g., price
stopping at 98.499, then 98.402 which was the upper quadrant of that wick, then equilibrium at 98.306).
This nested grading is how the algorithm \223knows\224 where to stop or reverse on a dime. ICT demonstrates
repeated precise reactions at these levels across timeframes as evidence of algorithmic delivery rather than
random price action.

6. Practical Instructions: How to Apply the PD Array Matrix
Daily Workflow (Recommended Sequence)
1. Establish Daily Bias & Risk Context
 \227 Check the Dollar Index (DXY). Dollar rallying = Risk-Off (favor
shorts in indices/commodities if technicals align). Dollar declining = Risk-On (favor longs). This is a
barometer, not a guarantee.
2. Frame the 20-Day Look-Back
 \227 Mark the high and low of the last 20 trading days on the daily chart of
your instrument (NQ, ES, EURUSD, GBPUSD, Gold, etc.). Grade into 0/25/50/75/100%.
3. Identify Active Quadrants
 \227 Determine where current price sits relative to equilibrium. Focus attention
on PD arrays inside the upper or lower quadrants that are relevant to the expected direction.
4. Catalogue PD Arrays
 \227 List every relevant FVG, Order Block, Breaker, Mitigation Block, wick (with its
own quadrant grades), NWOG/NDOG, etc., that sit inside the active 20-day range. Rank them by location
(premium vs discount) and confluence.
5. Wait for Price to Enter the Correct Zone
 \227 For a bullish bias, wait for price to trade into discount. For
bearish bias, wait for premium.
6. Drop to Lower Timeframe for Entry
 \227 On 15-min, 5-min, or even lower, look for a Market Structure Shift
(MSS / break of a swing high or low) in the direction of bias, then enter on the retest of the strongest PD
array at that level.
7. Risk Management
 \227 Place stop beyond the structural extreme that preceded the MSS. Target the
opposite side of the dealing range, the next significant liquidity pool (equal highs/lows), or a higher-timeframe
PD array. Scale out partially.
8. Time of Day Awareness
 \227 Certain windows (e.g., late New York session macros around 2:50\2263:10 PM
ICT stresses: do not chase perfect highs or lows. Trade the high-probability arrays that appear inside the
graded range. Build confidence gradually through back-testing and forward observation.

8. Market Structure Shifts & Liquidity Draws
Within the graded 20-day range, shifts in market structure are confirmed by price closing above or below key
swing highs/lows and interacting with quadrant levels. Liquidity draws frequently occur at:
\177 Relative equal highs or equal lows (classic buy-side or sell-side liquidity pools).
\177 The extremes of the 20-day range (or its quadrant boundaries).
\177 Unfilled fair value gaps or order blocks that sit at those levels.
The algorithm systematically \223orders\224 price to these levels. By knowing the active 20-day range and its graded
PD arrays, the trader can anticipate the most likely next draw on liquidity rather than reacting after the fact.

11. Common Pitfalls to Avoid (Synthesized from Session Logic)
\177 Trading PD arrays in the wrong zone relative to bias (e.g., buying a bullish FVG that sits in premium while
bias is bearish).
\177 Ignoring the 20-day look-back and marking random historical arrays that are no longer relevant to the
algorithm\222s current data set.
\177 Failing to grade the range or the individual arrays, thereby missing the precise reaction levels.
\177 Using non-time-based charts that destroy the algorithmic reference points.
\177 Over-complicating with external indicators instead of mastering pure price and time delivery.
\177 Chasing extremes instead of trading the high-probability arrays inside the active quadrants.
\177 Skipping intermarket context (DXY risk-on/off) when trading correlated markets.
