# ICT Liquidity Pool — Buy-Side & Sell-Side Liquidity Trading Guide + Free PDF

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-pool/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-pool/)

![ICT Liquidity Pool — buy-side liquidity above old highs and sell-side liquidity below old lows where smart money targets retail stops](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ICT-Liquidity-Pool-3.png)

A liquidity pool is the cluster of buy stops resting above old highs and the cluster of sell stops resting below old lows. Smart money — and ICT traders — target these pools because that is where the most willing buyers and sellers sit.

In this guide I walk you through the ICT liquidity pool concept in depth — the definition, buy-side vs sell-side liquidity, the bullish and bearish models, the entry and stop placement, the grey pool concept and the common mistakes traders make.

#### What is an ICT Liquidity Pool?

To understand the ICT liquidity pool you have to learn about [liquidity](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/liquidity-in-forex-trading/> "Liquidity") itself. Liquidity means the willing buyers and sellers who want to sell or buy at the market price.

Mostly retail traders buy or sell at the market price while smart money buys below the market price and sells above the market price.

You have to look at where most retail traders are willing to buy or sell. Mostly they buy above an old high after a breakout and sell below an old low as a breakout trade.

Alternatively, if they have already bought they place their sell stops below an old low to mitigate the loss, and if they have already sold they place their buy stops above an old high.

So old highs have a pool of liquidity resting above them in the form of buy stops and willing buyers waiting for the breakout. Old lows have a pool of liquidity resting below them in the form of sell stops or willing sellers ready to enter at market after the breakout.

![ICT Liquidity Pool diagram — buy-side liquidity above old highs, sell-side liquidity below old lows on a price chart](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ICT-liquidity-Pool.png)

That is why the area above an old high and the area below an old low are termed an ICT liquidity pool. Smart money and ICT traders look to trade these liquidity pools against retail traders.

#### Buy-Side vs Sell-Side Liquidity Pool

A liquidity pool is named after the type of orders that sit inside it.

  * **Buy-side liquidity** — buy stops resting above old highs and the breakout-buy entries that get triggered when price runs through the high. The pool fills willing sellers when smart money sells into it.
  * **Sell-side liquidity** — sell stops resting below old lows and the breakout-sell entries that get triggered when price runs through the low. The pool fills willing buyers when smart money buys into it.

In an uptrend smart money targets sell-side liquidity (below an old low) to buy at a discount. In a downtrend smart money targets buy-side liquidity (above an old high) to sell at a premium.

#### Bullish ICT Liquidity Pool

Bullish liquidity pools are areas on the price chart where smart money traders look to buy.

As discussed earlier, below old lows sell-side liquidity rests in the form of sell stops.

So in a bullish trend you look for the [discount zone](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-premium-and-discount-zone-identification/> "Discount Zone") to buy when price goes below an old low and clears the sell-side liquidity.

In this case you are buying against the sell orders of retail traders and price will most likely go higher to target buy-side liquidity.

![Bullish ICT Liquidity Pool — sweep below an old low into the discount zone followed by a rally targeting buy-side liquidity above](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bullish-liquidity-pool.png)

Your stop loss in this scenario will not be more than 40 to 50 pips as price should not go much lower below the old low.

For take profit you target the old highs as buy-side liquidity.

#### Bearish ICT Liquidity Pool

Bearish liquidity pools are areas on the price chart where smart money traders look to sell.

As discussed earlier, above old highs buy-side liquidity rests in the form of buy stops.

So in a bearish trend you look for the [premium zone](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-premium-and-discount-zone-identification/> "Premium Zone") to sell when price goes above an old high and clears the buy-side liquidity.

In this case you are selling against the buy orders of retail traders and price will most likely go lower to target sell-side liquidity.

![Bearish ICT Liquidity Pool — sweep above an old high into the premium zone followed by a drop targeting sell-side liquidity below](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bearish-liquidity-pool.png)

Your stop loss in this scenario will not be more than 40 to 50 pips as price should not go much higher above the old high.

For take profit you target the old lows as sell-side liquidity.

#### What is the ICT Grey Pool?

The ICT grey pool is a specific category of liquidity pool that sits at a level which is not yet confirmed as either a high or a low — typically a relative equal high or relative equal low that has formed but has not been swept yet.

It is “grey” because the resolution is uncertain: when price arrives, the move may be a [liquidity sweep](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) (taking the level and reversing) or a [low-resistance liquidity run](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-hrlr-lrlr/>) (taking the level and continuing).

Treat a grey pool as a draw-on-liquidity, not as a stand-alone trade signal. The classification only resolves once price prints a structure response after touching the level.

#### Step-by-Step ICT Liquidity Pool Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when targeting a liquidity pool.

  1. **Read the daily and H4 bias.** Bullish, bearish or neutral.
  2. **Mark the old high and old low.** Long-term highs and lows that have stood for several days or sessions.
  3. **Identify the matching pool.** Buy-side above the old high (for bearish setups), sell-side below the old low (for bullish setups).
  4. **Wait for the sweep.** Price must wick through the level and close back inside the prior range.
  5. **Drop to the lower timeframe.** 5-minute or 1-minute for the entry trigger.
  6. **Look for confirmation.** A clean lower-timeframe Market Structure Shift after the sweep is the simplest filter; the original framework treats the sweep itself as enough, but a confirmation entry consistently improves the result.
  7. **Set the stop.** 40 to 50 pips beyond the swept level.
  8. **Take profit at the opposite pool.** Old highs for buy trades, old lows for sell trades.

#### Best Markets for Trading ICT Liquidity Pools

Liquidity pools form on every market that the ICT methodology is applied to.

  * **NQ (NASDAQ futures)** and **ES (S &P 500 futures)** — the daily highs and lows produce some of the cleanest liquidity-pool sweeps because of the depth of US institutional flow.
  * **GBP/USD** and **EUR/USD** — relative equal highs and lows form constantly during the London-NY overlap, especially around the killzones.
  * **XAU/USD (Gold)** — pool sweeps at 08:30 ET news releases are some of the most reliable on the chart.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, NQ and ES on the daily and H4 produce textbook liquidity-pool setups. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to look for sweeps because the algorithmic delivery is concentrated there.

#### Do We Need Confirmation to Trade an ICT Liquidity Pool?

In the original framework, no — you do not need confirmation when executing a trade using the ICT liquidity pool. Setting a 40 to 50 pip stop loss is enough because price seeks liquidity and returns after targeting the highs or lows.

That said, on smaller accounts I prefer to wait for a Market Structure Shift on the lower timeframe after the sweep. The MSS confirms that price has rejected the level and improves the entry timing without changing the underlying setup.

#### How Much Should I Risk per Trade?

You should risk only the amount you can afford to lose.

The standard rule is to risk only 1% of your capital per trade.

#### Common Mistakes Around ICT Liquidity Pools

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading liquidity pools.

  1. **Trading every old high or low.** Not every old level is a meaningful pool. The cleanest pools are those that have stood for multiple sessions or multiple days.
  2. **Wrong side of the bias.** Bullish setups buy below old lows; bearish setups sell above old highs. Confusing the side inverts the entire trade.
  3. **Stop too tight.** Stops parked exactly at the swept level often get hunted on the second test. The 40 to 50 pip buffer is part of the model.
  4. **Holding past the opposite pool.** Take profit is the opposite-side pool. Holding for a “bigger” target often gives back the gains when price reaches the opposite pool and reverses.
  5. **Ignoring news.** A red-folder news release inside the sweep window distorts price action and routinely turns a clean setup into a stop-out.
  6. **Confusing a pool with a sweep.** The pool is the resting liquidity. The sweep is the action that takes it. Both terms are related but not identical.

#### Is ICT Liquidity Pool a Reliable Model?

Yes — it is one of the more reliable trading models from ICT teachings. But to get better results you should have a correct direction and bias of the market.

The model works best when paired with a higher-timeframe PD array — entering at a discount when sweeping sell-side, or at a premium when sweeping buy-side.

#### FAQs about ICT Liquidity Pool

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about ICT liquidity pools.

**What is an ICT liquidity pool?**

An ICT liquidity pool is the cluster of stop orders and pending breakout entries resting above an old high (buy-side) or below an old low (sell-side). Smart money targets these pools to fill orders.

**What is buy-side liquidity in ICT?**

Buy-side liquidity is the cluster of buy stops and breakout-buy entries that sit above an old high or relative equal high. When price runs through the high, that pool of liquidity is filled.

**What is sell-side liquidity in ICT?**

Sell-side liquidity is the cluster of sell stops and breakout-sell entries that sit below an old low or relative equal low. When price runs through the low, that pool of liquidity is filled.

**How is the ICT liquidity pool different from a liquidity sweep?**

The pool is the resting liquidity at a level. The sweep is the action of price taking that liquidity. The two terms describe related but distinct concepts.

**What is the ICT grey pool?**

The ICT grey pool is a level (typically a relative equal high or low) that has formed but has not yet been swept. It is “grey” because the resolution — sweep or run — is not yet known when price arrives.

**Where should I place the stop loss?**

40 to 50 pips beyond the swept level — below the swept low for buy trades, above the swept high for sell trades.

**Where should I take profit?**

The opposite-side pool — old highs for buy trades and old lows for sell trades.

**Do I need confirmation to trade a liquidity pool?**

The original framework does not require confirmation; setting a 40 to 50 pip stop is enough. On smaller accounts, an MSS on the lower timeframe after the sweep is a useful additional filter.

**What is forex liquidity pool?**

In forex, a liquidity pool refers to the same concept — buy stops above old highs and sell stops below old lows on currency pairs. The model is identical to the ICT liquidity pool framework on indices and metals.

**How much should I risk per trade?**

The standard rule is 1% of capital per trade — a number small enough to survive a streak of stops and large enough to compound meaningfully on winners.
