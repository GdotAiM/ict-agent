# How to Trade an RSI Divergence — Bullish/Bearish Reversal Setup with Confirmation

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/how-to-trade-an-rsi-divergence/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/how-to-trade-an-rsi-divergence/)

![RSI Divergence trading strategy — regular bullish and bearish divergence with price-RSI disconnect at support and resistance levels for trend reversal trades](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/RSI-divergence.png)

RSI Divergence is one of the most reliable momentum-based reversal signals in technical analysis — when price makes a new low but RSI makes a higher low (or price makes a new high but RSI makes a lower high), the momentum behind the trend is fading and a reversal is often near. Master the regular bullish and bearish divergence at strong support and resistance levels, and you have a high-conviction reversal filter.

This guide walks you through the identification and use of RSI divergence with real market examples. By thoroughly studying it and dedicating yourself to practice, you will gain the ability to identify and execute RSI divergence trading opportunities with the skill of a professional trader.

In this guide I walk you through RSI divergence — the indicator definition, regular vs hidden divergence, bullish and bearish identification, trade execution, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is an RSI Divergence?

**The Relative Strength Index (RSI)** , created by J. Welles Wilder, serves as a momentum oscillator that gauges the velocity and direction of price changes.

RSI values fluctuate between zero and 100, tracking price movements and signaling instances of overbought and oversold conditions in the market.

But sometimes the price movement and the movement of RSI may diverge from each other — price moves in one direction while RSI moves in the other direction — and this is termed an **RSI divergence**.

RSI divergence is divided into two types:

**(I) Regular RSI Divergence**

**(II) Hidden RSI Divergence**

This guide primarily focuses on Regular RSI divergence — the type of divergence most commonly utilized in trading practices.

#### Regular RSI Divergence

Regular RSI signals a trend reversal in the market. It has two types based on the trend of the market.

**(I) Bullish RSI Divergence** is mostly seen and reliable at the end of a bearish trend.

In a bearish trend, when price makes lower lows but the RSI diverges from price making higher lows, this indicates the losing momentum of sellers in the market and a possible change in the direction of market.

In the picture below you can see an example of bullish RSI divergence on a chart.

![Bullish RSI divergence example — price makes a new lower low while RSI prints a higher low, signaling weakening bearish momentum and potential reversal](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Bullish-RSI-divergence-1.png)

**How to Trade Bullish RSI Divergence**

To trade using bullish RSI divergence, you have to identify the trend of the market — and it should be bearish.

Price should be seen making lower lows and is going to test a strong support area where you can expect a reversal of price or a deeper retracement.

When price tests the support area, you will be looking for a buy trade — and here the RSI divergence will act as confirmation of your buy trade.

At the support area, if you see RSI diverging from price, you can expect a price reversal.

But to execute a trade, the RSI divergence alone is not reliable — you have to look for other bullish evidences like a bullish candlestick pattern, [Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>), or bullish chart pattern.

![Bullish RSI divergence trade example on XAU/USD 4H — 1932 support line tested with bullish RSI divergence, bullish engulfing candle confirmation and market structure shift](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/rsi-bullish-divergence.png)

As you can see in the picture above — XAU/USD 4-hourly chart where price was in a bearish trend and tested the 1932 support line.

When it tested the support line, RSI indicated a bullish divergence — and as a confirmation, the bullish engulfing candlestick pattern was also formed. Plus price shifted its structure too, so there were three confirmations of price reversal from here.

A buy trade is executed at support using RSI bullish divergence.

In this scenario, your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips below the recent low or support zone, whichever is lower. For take profit, target the next resistance zone.

**(II) Bearish RSI Divergence** is typically observed and considered reliable towards the conclusion of a bullish trend.

During a bullish trend, when price makes higher highs but the RSI demonstrates lower highs — showing a divergence from price — it signifies diminishing momentum among buyers in the market, hinting at a potential reversal in market direction.

The provided image below illustrates an example of bearish RSI divergence on a chart.

![Bearish RSI divergence example — price makes a new higher high while RSI prints a lower high, signaling weakening bullish momentum and potential reversal](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/RSI-bearish-divergence.png)

**How to Trade Bearish RSI Divergence**

To execute trades based on bearish RSI divergence, first identify a bullish trend in the market.

During this bullish trend, monitor the price action as it forms higher highs — indicating a potential test of a significant resistance level where a reversal or deeper retracement could occur.

As price approaches the resistance area, prepare to enter a sell trade. The bearish RSI divergence will serve as confirmation for your sell trade. Keep an eye on the RSI indicator as it diverges from price action at the resistance area.

This divergence suggests a weakening bullish momentum and hints at an impending price reversal. Therefore, when you observe the RSI diverging from price at the resistance level, anticipate a reversal — confirming your decision to enter a sell trade.

But to execute a trade, the RSI divergence alone is not reliable — you have to look for other bearish evidences like a bearish candlestick pattern, [Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>), or bearish chart pattern.

![Bearish RSI divergence trade example on XAU/USD 4H — 2088 resistance area tested with bearish RSI divergence, evening star candle confirmation triggering the sell entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/rsi-bearish-divergence-trading.png)

In the picture above you can see the XAU/USD 4-hourly chart where price approaches the 2088 strong resistance area.

When it tests the resistance, you can see RSI indicating bearish divergence and a formation of the Evening Star candlestick pattern for confirmation.

A sell trade is executed on the test of the evening star pattern using RSI bearish divergence.

In this case, your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips above the recent high or resistance level, whichever is higher. For take profit, target the next support area.

#### RSI Divergence in the ICT Framework

RSI divergence is a momentum filter that ICT traders can layer on top of the structural framework — not a replacement for it.

The cleanest setups occur when RSI divergence aligns with three structural conditions: (1) price at a higher-timeframe PD array, (2) an [inducement sweep](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) at the level, and (3) a lower-timeframe MSS in the reversal direction.

Use RSI divergence to filter out structural reversal signals — if MSS prints but RSI agrees with the prior trend, the reversal is weaker. If MSS prints AND RSI diverges, the reversal is high-conviction.

#### Step-by-Step RSI Divergence Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading an RSI divergence reversal.

  1. **Identify the trend.** Bearish for bullish divergence setup, bullish for bearish divergence setup.
  2. **Mark the key level.** Strong support (bullish setup) or strong resistance (bearish setup) — ideally aligned with a higher-timeframe PD array.
  3. **Wait for price to reach the level.** Patience — do not pre-position.
  4. **Check RSI for divergence.** Bullish — price makes a new low but RSI prints a higher low. Bearish — price makes a new high but RSI prints a lower high.
  5. **Wait for confirmation.** A bullish candlestick pattern (hammer, engulfing, morning star) at support — or bearish candle (evening star, shooting star, bearish engulfing) at resistance. Optional: lower-timeframe MSS in the reversal direction.
  6. **Enter on the confirmation candle close.**
  7. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips below the recent low / support (buy) or above the recent high / resistance (sell), whichever is the more extreme level.
  8. **Set the target.** Next opposite-direction level — next resistance for a buy, next support for a sell.
  9. **Manage the trade.** Book partials at 1:1 or at the first opposite-direction PD array, trail the runner.

#### Common Mistakes Around RSI Divergence

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading RSI divergence.

  1. **Trading divergence in the middle of a range.** Divergence only matters at strong support / resistance. In a range, every minor reversal produces a divergence.
  2. **Entering on divergence alone.** RSI divergence is the FILTER — not the trigger. You need a candlestick pattern, MSS or structural confirmation to enter.
  3. **Confusing regular and hidden divergence.** Regular divergence signals reversal. Hidden divergence signals continuation. Mixing them flips the direction of the trade.
  4. **Tight stops at the recent extreme.** The 10–20 pip buffer below the support or above the resistance is mandatory — wicks hunt tight stops at the level.
  5. **Trading every divergence.** Some divergences fail. Stack the filters — divergence + level + candle pattern + MSS — for the highest hit rate.
  6. **Wrong RSI period.** Default RSI(14) works on every timeframe. Changing the period for “better signals” usually over-fits and produces fakes.

#### FAQs about RSI Divergence

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about RSI divergence.

**What is an RSI divergence?**

When price and the RSI indicator move in opposite directions — for example, price makes a new high while RSI makes a lower high (bearish divergence), or price makes a new low while RSI makes a higher low (bullish divergence). It signals fading momentum behind the trend.

**What is regular RSI divergence?**

Regular RSI divergence signals trend reversal. Bullish regular divergence (in a downtrend) precedes a reversal up; bearish regular divergence (in an uptrend) precedes a reversal down.

**What is hidden RSI divergence?**

Hidden divergence signals trend continuation, not reversal. It is the opposite of regular divergence — bullish hidden divergence in an uptrend predicts continuation higher.

**How do I identify bullish RSI divergence?**

In a bearish trend, watch for price to make a new lower low while RSI prints a higher low. The disagreement signals fading sell-side momentum.

**How do I identify bearish RSI divergence?**

In a bullish trend, watch for price to make a new higher high while RSI prints a lower high. The disagreement signals fading buy-side momentum.

**Can I trade RSI divergence alone?**

No — RSI divergence alone is not reliable. Pair it with a strong support / resistance level, a candlestick pattern confirmation, and ideally a market structure shift.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

After price reaches the support / resistance level, RSI diverges, and a confirmation candle prints (bullish at support, bearish at resistance). Enter on the candle close.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

10 to 20 pips below the recent low or support (buy trade), 10 to 20 pips above the recent high or resistance (sell trade) — whichever is the more extreme level.

**Where do I take profit?**

The next opposite-direction level — next resistance for a buy trade, next support for a sell trade.

**What RSI period works best?**

The default RSI(14) is the standard and works on every timeframe. Avoid changing the period — shorter periods produce too many false signals, longer periods miss the divergence entirely.

**What timeframe is best for RSI divergence?**

RSI divergence works on every timeframe but is most reliable on the 4-hour and daily. Lower timeframes (15-minute, 5-minute) produce more false divergences.

**Does RSI divergence work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook RSI divergence reversals at higher-timeframe key levels.

**How does RSI divergence fit in the ICT framework?**

Use it as a momentum filter on top of the structural framework — RSI divergence at a higher-timeframe PD array combined with an inducement sweep and a lower-timeframe MSS is the highest-conviction reversal setup.
