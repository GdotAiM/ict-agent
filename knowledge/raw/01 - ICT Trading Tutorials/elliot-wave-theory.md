# Elliott Wave Theory — 5-Wave Motive + 3-Wave Corrective Sequence with Trade Rules

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/elliot-wave-theory/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/elliot-wave-theory/)

![Elliott Wave Theory — 5-wave motive sequence followed by 3-wave corrective sequence with impulse and diagonal motive variants and the three unbreakable rules](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Elliot-wave.png)

In 1938, after 9 years of research, American financier Ralph Nelson Elliott formulated the Elliott Wave Theory. He stated that financial markets follow some random and disorderly but repetitive patterns — which he named waves. He observed the fractal nature of markets: price patterns repeat themselves over and over again on all timeframes.

In this guide I walk you through Elliott Wave Theory — the motive and corrective waves, the impulse vs diagonal motive variants, the 3 unbreakable rules, the 5-wave and ABC sequences, advantages and disadvantages, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is Elliott Wave Theory?

The Elliott Wave Theory is a type of technical analysis that seeks out repeating long-term price patterns associated with continuing shifts in investor sentiment and psychology.

This theory states that there are two types of price movements in the market.

**(I) Motive Waves:** Aligned with the large trend.

**(II) Corrective Waves:** Contrary to the large trend.

![Elliott Wave Theory illustration — motive 5-wave sequence \(1,2,3,4,5\) aligned with the trend, followed by 3-wave corrective sequence \(A,B,C\) against the trend](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/ELLIOT-WAVE.png)

The Elliott wave sequence contains 5 waves (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) that make up the motive wave, and 3 waves (A, B, C) that make up the corrective wave.

After the completion of wave 5, the market will develop into a 3-wave (A, B, C) retracement move contrary to the motive wave.

#### Motive Wave

Motive wave is further classified into two waves depending on the form and location within the overall market structure.

**(I) Impulse Waves** are the most common form of motive wave and are easiest to spot in the market.

These waves look flat-vertical or zigzag in shape and are quite heavy movements in one direction. In this wave, price travels a great distance in a short period of time.

**(II) Diagonal Waves** look like a wedge — either expanding or contracting.

This form of price movement is less energetic than an impulse wave, and hence it moves in one direction taking retracements, creating a wedge pattern.

![Elliott motive wave types — impulse wave \(flat vertical or zigzag with strong directional move\) vs diagonal wave \(expanding or contracting wedge shape\)](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/impulse-wave.png)

#### Corrective Wave

Corrective waves are basically the retracement moves which are contrary to the large trend of market, and hence are less energetic than the motive waves.

Corrective waves can be in the form of triangles, flag or a zigzag move.

![Elliott corrective wave variants — triangle, flag and zigzag corrective patterns retracing against the motive wave direction](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Corrective-Wave.png)

#### The 3 Unbreakable Rules of Elliott Wave

To identify an Elliott wave structure, you should first know about the 3 unbreakable rules of Elliott Wave Theory.

If one of these rules is violated, the structure is not an Elliott wave.

**(I)** Wave 2 cannot retrace more than 100% of wave 1.

**(II)** Wave 4 cannot move into the territory of wave 1.

**(III)** Wave 3 cannot be the shortest wave.

#### How to Identify Wave 1 in Bullish Market

Wave 1 begins the impulse sequence. During this wave, price begins to bounce higher after a long sustained downtrend.

The sentiment in the market is very bearish at the start of wave 1.

It is generally the least predictive wave within the entire Elliott wave structure — there is really no way to know how long the wave will last.

However, at some point it will clearly become apparent that wave 1 has ended as a significant swing high is breached and price begins to move lower in a bearish direction.

#### How to Identify Wave 2 in Bullish Market

Wave 2 is a corrective wave — it will move counter to wave 1 and will retrace a larger portion of wave 1.

Often it will retrace wave 1 by either 50%, 61% or even 78%.

Remember the unbreakable rule that wave 2 cannot go beyond the low of wave 1.

Informed traders will start to recognize this pattern and will start to position on the long side.

![Elliott Wave 1-5 motive sequence example — wave 1 initial bounce, wave 2 retracement \(50-78% of wave 1\), wave 3 strongest leg, wave 4 shallow correction, wave 5 final push](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Elliot-wave-12345.png)

#### How to Identify Wave 3 in Bullish Market

Wave 3 offers the largest potential for profit, as it is usually the largest and most powerful wave in the Elliott wave structure.

After the break of the swing high of wave 1, price will begin to accelerate upward as more and more traders recognize the bullish sentiment and start taking long positions.

Many sellers will have their stops triggered at the wave 1 high, which adds fuel to the upside price movement.

If you identify the 3rd wave, you need to join the trend as quickly as possible in order to take advantage of the explosive price movement.

Those waiting for a pullback will miss the entire move as wave 3 offers very little pullback.

#### How to Identify Wave 4 in Bullish Market

Wave 4 is also a corrective wave — it will retrace a portion of wave 3.

Typically wave 4 will be a shallower retracement and will often terminate at 38% or 50% of wave 3.

Wave 4 will be less aggressive in comparison to wave 2.

Keep in mind the core rule of Elliott Wave Theory: wave 4 should not overlap the territory of wave 1.

Wave 4 is also seen as a profit-taking wave for traders who bought early in wave 3 — and it creates sideways movement, so wave 4 often takes the form of a triangle.

#### How to Identify Wave 5 in Bullish Market

Wave 5 is the final leg within the Elliott wave impulse sequence. It moves in the direction of the overall trend just like wave 1 and wave 3.

Wave 5 is often considered the retail wave because during its progression almost everyone is bullish — and mostly retail traders drive prices higher.

The momentum behind the bullish activity in wave 5 will be less dynamic than wave 3 and wave 1.

During the last stages of wave 5, you will also see technical divergence between price and momentum indicators like [RSI (Relative Strength Index)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/how-to-trade-an-rsi-divergence/>), MACD and Stochastic.

These divergences signal that the velocity of price movement higher is diminishing — and you should expect the upside trend to reverse.

Along with momentum divergences, wave 5 will often register lower volume compared to wave 3. This is another indication of trend weakness — profits should be locked in as a potential reversal is imminent.

The end of wave 5 brings the completion of the impulse sequence — so you can expect the corrective 3-wave pattern A, B, C.

#### How to Identify Wave A in Bullish Market

Wave A starts the corrective sequence. During this wave, most traders are still very bullish and consider wave A just a pullback — a minor retracement.

However, some experienced traders begin to recognize the overbought market conditions during wave 5.

Wave A is often a sharp move and may subdivide as either a 5-wave pattern or 3-wave pattern.

#### How to Identify Wave B in Bullish Market

Wave B is the second leg of the corrective sequence structure. It follows wave A in the contrary direction, retracing either 50%, 61% or 78%.

Wave B will be short-lived and will fail to surpass the wave 5 swing high.

Wave B will often create a double-top formation as it tries to test the wave 5 swing high.

Wave B is mostly seen as a zigzag pattern but sometimes forms as a triangle.

![Elliott Wave ABC corrective sequence example — wave A sharp drop, wave B counter-retracement \(50-78% retrace, fails to surpass wave 5 high\), wave C powerful final leg](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Elliot-wave-ABC.png)

#### How to Identify Wave C in Bullish Market

Wave C marks the final leg within the corrective structure.

When the swing low of wave B is broken, it marks the turning point where more and more traders will be convinced that the uptrend has ended and the market is poised for lower prices.

Wave C has the same characteristics as wave 3. Both tend to be relatively long waves and can be quite powerful from a momentum point of view.

#### Elliott Wave in the ICT Framework

Elliott Wave and ICT are not competing frameworks — they read the same chart from different angles.

Wave 1 is the displacement leg that produces the first [BOS](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/break-of-structure-bos/>). Wave 2 is the retracement that sweeps the inducement. Wave 3 is the high-conviction continuation after the inducement sweep. Wave 4 is a smaller retracement that respects the wave 1 territory. Wave 5 is the final extension with weakening momentum (often with RSI divergence and a [CISD](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-change-in-the-state-of-delivery/>) at the top).

The ABC correction maps onto the bearish reversal sequence — wave A breaks the wave-5 high, wave B is the retracement to a premium PD array, wave C is the post-[CHOCH](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/change-of-character-choch-in-trading/>) bearish leg targeting the wave-4 low or below.

Use Elliott Wave for the macro narrative; use ICT for the entry trigger inside each wave.

#### Advantages of Elliott Wave

**Understanding Market Cycles:** The Elliott Wave Theory offers a structured approach to comprehending market cycles, illustrating how they fluctuate in waves of sentiment. Through the examination of wave patterns, traders can identify potential trends and reversals within the market.

**Entry and Exit:** By pinpointing crucial levels of support and resistance, the Elliott Wave Theory can offer precise entry and exit points for trades. This approach aids in risk management and the optimization of profits by strategically timing trades.

**Conjunction with Other Tools:** The Elliott Wave Theory can be integrated with other technical and fundamental analysis tools to validate trading decisions. This collaborative approach enhances the accuracy of assessments and ensures a more comprehensive understanding of market dynamics.

#### Disadvantages of Elliott Wave

**Complexity:** Mastering the Elliott Wave Theory needs a substantial investment of time and effort due to its complexity, requiring dedicated learning and practical application to utilize effectively.

**Subjective Interpretation:** The interpretation of wave patterns within the Elliott Wave Theory can be subjective, as different analysts may perceive and interpret these patterns differently based on their individual perspectives and analytical techniques.

**Not a 100% Accurate Approach:** Although the Elliott Wave Theory can be valuable for detecting potential trends and reversals, its accuracy is not guaranteed, and it can sometimes produce false signals.

#### Step-by-Step Elliott Wave Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading using Elliott Wave.

  1. **Identify the macro trend.** Higher highs and higher lows (bullish) or lower highs and lower lows (bearish).
  2. **Mark wave 1.** The first impulsive leg in the new direction after a sustained counter-trend.
  3. **Wait for wave 2.** Corrective retracement (50%–78% of wave 1) that does not exceed the wave 1 start.
  4. **Identify wave 3.** The strongest extension — enter on the BOS of the wave 1 swing high (bullish) or low (bearish).
  5. **Wait for wave 4.** Shallow retracement (38%–50% of wave 3) — never into wave 1 territory.
  6. **Trade wave 5 cautiously.** Look for RSI divergence and weakening volume — this is the last leg before reversal.
  7. **Watch for wave A.** First sharp counter-trend leg after wave 5 completes.
  8. **Identify wave B.** Failed retracement that double-tops near the wave 5 high.
  9. **Trade wave C aggressively.** Same characteristics as wave 3 — powerful, sustained, low-risk entry after wave B failure.
  10. **Apply the 3 unbreakable rules at every step.** Wave 2 < 100% of wave 1, wave 4 outside wave 1 territory, wave 3 never the shortest.

#### Best Pairs for Elliott Wave

Elliott Wave works on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the Elliott Wave framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. Higher-timeframe Elliott Wave (daily, 4-hour) is more reliable than lower-timeframe wave counts.

#### Common Mistakes Around Elliott Wave

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start with Elliott Wave.

  1. **Forcing wave counts on every chart.** Not every move is an Elliott Wave. If the structure does not respect the three rules, do not label it.
  2. **Trading wave 1 too aggressively.** Wave 1 is the least predictable. Wait for wave 2 retracement to confirm before committing capital.
  3. **Missing wave 3 by waiting for a pullback.** Wave 3 offers very little pullback. Aggressive entry on the wave-1-high break is often the only realistic option.
  4. **Violating the wave-4 / wave-1 territory rule.** If wave 4 overlaps wave 1, the count is invalid. Re-label and start again.
  5. **Trading wave 5 without divergence confirmation.** Wave 5 is the retail wave — without RSI / MACD divergence, you may catch a sustained continuation instead of the topping move.
  6. **Treating wave B as a new trend.** Wave B is a retracement that fails to break wave 5. Trading it as a new bullish leg flips the direction of the ABC correction.

#### FAQs about Elliott Wave Theory

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about Elliott Wave Theory.

**What is Elliott Wave Theory?**

A technical analysis framework formulated by Ralph Nelson Elliott in 1938. It states that financial markets move in repetitive wave patterns — 5 motive waves aligned with the trend, followed by 3 corrective waves against the trend (the 5-3 sequence).

**What are the two types of waves?**

(1) Motive waves — aligned with the large trend, 5 waves labeled 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. (2) Corrective waves — against the trend, 3 waves labeled A, B, C.

**What are the three unbreakable rules of Elliott Wave?**

(1) Wave 2 cannot retrace more than 100% of wave 1. (2) Wave 4 cannot move into the territory of wave 1. (3) Wave 3 cannot be the shortest wave.

**What is an impulse wave?**

The most common motive wave — flat-vertical or zigzag in shape, with strong directional movement and minimal retracement.

**What is a diagonal wave?**

A motive wave shaped like an expanding or contracting wedge — less energetic than an impulse wave, with directional retracements that form the wedge.

**Which wave is the most profitable?**

Wave 3 — usually the largest and most powerful wave in the motive sequence. It offers the most pip potential and the cleanest momentum.

**How do I identify the end of wave 5?**

Look for RSI / MACD / Stochastic divergence between price and the momentum indicator, plus reduced volume compared to wave 3. Both signal that the trend is exhausting.

**How does Elliott Wave relate to ICT?**

Wave 1 = displacement BOS. Wave 2 = inducement sweep. Wave 3 = high-conviction continuation. Wave 4 = shallow retracement. Wave 5 = exhaustion with divergence and CISD. The ABC correction maps onto the post-CHOCH bearish leg.

**What timeframe works best for Elliott Wave?**

Higher timeframes (daily, weekly) produce the most reliable wave counts. Lower timeframes are subject to noise and false wave-count interpretations.

**Can I trade Elliott Wave alone?**

You can — but combining it with ICT structure (BOS, MSS, inducement sweep) and momentum filters (RSI divergence) significantly improves the hit rate.

**Does Elliott Wave work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all respect Elliott Wave structures on the higher timeframes (daily and weekly).

**What are the disadvantages of Elliott Wave?**

(1) Complexity — requires significant study and practice. (2) Subjectivity — different analysts may label the same chart differently. (3) Not 100% accurate — false signals do occur, especially in choppy markets.
