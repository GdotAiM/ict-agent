#### What are ICT Killzones?

ICT killzones — also known as ICT session times or forex killzone times — are time frames with high trading volume and volatility in the market, resulting in good trading opportunities you can use to maximise profits.

These killzones are based on the four primary sessions of the international forex market: Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York. The overlap of these sessions and the institutional flow inside them is what makes the killzone windows volatile.

Another major factor contributing to the surge in trading volume during these sessions is heavy trade activity by various institutional traders, which Michael Huddleston refers to as ICT killzones.

These zones are divided into four time frames named: ICT Asian killzone, ICT London killzone, ICT New York killzone and ICT London close killzone. The timing for all four is tabulated below.

These ICT killzone times are referenced in both GMT and EST.

#### ICT Asian Killzone Time

The ICT Asian killzone lies between 7:00 PM and 10:00 PM (New York local time).

It is based on the ICT Asian range — the first strategic period in the forex market — with the ideal pairs being the Australian Dollar, New Zealand Dollar and Japanese Yen.

These are the most active pairs in ICT Asian time and the US Dollar mostly tends to consolidate during the Asian session.

**Characteristics of the Asian Killzone**

  * Asian killzone time lies between 07:00 PM and 10:00 PM.
  * Higher timeframe bias is helpful during this period, but a short-term retracement (bullish or bearish) can offer a similar optimal trade entry.
  * The Asian session typically displays the lowest volatility and the tightest ranges, making it less attractive for traders who need movement.
  * The Asian open often sets up an optimal trade entry pattern that can offer a 15 to 20 pip scalp trade.
  * Most trade setups during the Asian session happen on cross pairs because of US Dollar consolidation in this timeframe.

#### ICT London Killzone Time

The ICT London killzone time lies between 02:00 AM and 05:00 AM (New York local time).

It falls inside the ICT London session and witnesses the highest trading volume compared with the Asian session.

The Euro (EUR) and Britain Pound (GBP) are the hot pairs during this period. The London session possesses the highest probability of a large directional move in 24 hours.

**Characteristics of the London Killzone**

  * ICT London killzone lies between 02:00 AM and 05:00 AM EST.
  * Higher timeframe bias is helpful during this period.
  * The London open can set up an optimal trade entry pattern that can offer more than 30 pips of directional trade.
  * On a bullish day during the London killzone, price mostly sets the high of the day.
  * On a bearish day during the London killzone, price often sets the low of the day.

#### ICT New York Killzone Time

The ICT New York killzone lies between 07:00 AM and 09:00 AM (New York local time).

It is highly volatile due to the overlap of the London and New York sessions. During this session, pairs coupled with the US Dollar are important to watch and trade.

**Characteristics of the New York Killzone**

  * ICT New York killzone lies between 07:00 AM and 09:00 AM EST.
  * Higher timeframe bias is helpful during this period, but a short-term retracement either bullish or bearish can offer a similar optimal trade entry.
  * The New York session mostly retraces back to the London trading range and sets up an optimal trade entry pattern that can offer 30 to 40 pips of trade.
  * The New York session is volatile with high trading volume because of the overlap between the London and New York sessions.
  * The 09:50 NY-AM [ICT macro time](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) sits inside this killzone and is one of the highest-probability windows of the entire trading day.

#### ICT London Close Killzone Time

The ICT London close killzone lies between 10:00 AM and 12:00 PM (New York local time).

It carries significant importance due to its timing — most traders secure their positions before the close of the European day, which often results in a retracement of price back to the daily range.

Price mostly retraces back to its daily range offering a good optimal trade entry setup, and major pairs coupled with the US Dollar are preferable to trade during this time.

**Characteristics of the London Close Killzone**

  * ICT London close killzone lies between 10:00 AM and 12:00 PM EST.
  * London close killzone can offer a 5-minute OTE setup with a 15 to 20 pip scalp trade.
  * On a bullish day, after making the high of the day, price retraces back to the daily range during the London close killzone.
  * On a bearish day, after making the low of the day, price retraces back to the daily range during the London close killzone.

#### Step-by-Step Killzone Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run before any killzone window opens — from pre-market preparation to entry and management.

  1. **Set the daily bias.** Read the higher-timeframe [daily bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-explained/>) the night before — bullish, bearish or neutral.
  2. **Mark the draw on liquidity.** Highlight relative equal highs/lows, prior day high/low, prior session high/low and any unfilled fair value gaps.
  3. **Wait for the killzone to open.** Do not enter before the window. The institutional flow that creates the volatility starts only when the killzone opens.
  4. **Watch for the sweep.** The first move of the killzone is usually a liquidity sweep — Asian high/low for the London killzone, London high/low for the New York killzone, and so on.
  5. **Wait for an MSS.** A clean lower-timeframe market structure shift confirms intent.
  6. **Enter on the retracement.** Into the fair value gap, order block or OTE 0.62 to 0.79 zone left behind by displacement.
  7. **Set the stop.** Above the swept high (for shorts) or below the swept low (for longs), with a small buffer.
  8. **Take profit at the next external pool.** The opposite side of the prior session, relative equal highs/lows or the next day’s projected high/low.
  9. **Manage at first liquidity.** Move stop to break-even once the first internal pool is reached, then trail.

#### Best Pairs for Each ICT Killzone

The pair you trade should match the killzone you are inside. These are the pair preferences I personally apply, broken down by window.

  * **Asian killzone** — AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, AUD/USD and cross pairs. The US Dollar consolidates so most setups print on crosses.
  * **London killzone** — GBP/USD, EUR/USD and EUR/GBP. London session is the largest directional mover of the day on these pairs.
  * **New York killzone** — USD majors (GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, USD/JPY) plus US indices (NQ, ES) and XAU/USD.
  * **London close killzone** — USD majors and indices that retrace into the daily range as European positions close.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the New York killzone (07:00 to 10:00 AM ET) is the most natural fit — it overlaps with the open of the New York Stock Exchange at 09:30 ET and contains the 09:50 NY-AM macro window. NQ and ES futures (CME Group) along with major USD pairs deliver the cleanest setups inside this killzone.

#### How does Daylight Saving Time Affect ICT Killzone Times?

Daylight Saving Time (DST) in the US is a practice where clocks are moved forward one hour in the spring, giving more daylight hours in the evening during the summer months, and then moved back one hour in the fall to return to standard time.

Essentially “springing forward” in March and “falling back” in November, with the time change occurring on the second Sunday of March and the first Sunday of November respectively, at 2:00 AM local time.

If your country does not follow daylight saving time then it can affect the ICT killzone times relative to your local time with a shift of an hour.

But if your country follows daylight saving time then you do not need to worry about it.

To avoid any confusion ICT himself recommends following the New York local time as the base time. It does not matter where you live — just follow the NY local clock.

The session and killzone windows remain the same irrespective of daylight saving; only the label changes from EST (Eastern Standard Time) to EDT (Eastern Daylight Saving Time).

#### Common Mistakes When Trading ICT Killzones

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading the killzone windows.

  1. **Pre-positioning before the window opens.** The killzone is the catalyst. An entry placed before the window opens removes the entire reason for using the strategy.
  2. **Trading every killzone.** Not every window has a clean draw on liquidity or a clean bias. If the higher-timeframe context is murky, sit out and wait for the next one.
  3. **Wrong pair for the wrong killzone.** Trying to trade GBP majors during the Asian killzone or AUD pairs during the New York killzone produces choppy, unreliable price action.
  4. **Ignoring the sweep.** The first leg of the killzone is usually a sweep, not the trade direction. Entering on the sweep itself almost always leads to a stop-out before the real move.
  5. **No MSS confirmation.** A market structure shift on the lower timeframe is the trigger. Without it the entry is guesswork.
  6. **Forgetting daylight saving.** Twice a year the EST/EDT label changes. If you trade from a fixed local clock, your entries will be off by an hour for two weeks until your charting platform syncs.
  7. **News overlap.** A red-folder release inside the killzone distorts price action. I avoid the window when high-impact news is scheduled inside it.
