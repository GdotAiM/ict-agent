# Gravestone Doji Candlestick — Bearish Reversal at Resistance with Long Upper Wick

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/gravestone-doji-candlestick/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/gravestone-doji-candlestick/)

![Gravestone Doji Candlestick — bearish reversal pattern with long upper wick and almost no real body, open low and close at the same price level](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Gravestone-Doji-Candlestick.png)

Gravestone Doji is the bearish mirror of the [Dragonfly Doji](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/dragonfly-doji-candlestick/>) — a single candle with a long upper wick and virtually no real body, signaling that buyers pushed price up but were forcefully overwhelmed by sellers who drove it back to the open. When it prints at a strong resistance area, it is one of the highest-conviction bearish reversal triggers in candlestick analysis.

In this guide I walk you through the Gravestone Doji — the definition, how to use it, stop and target rules, the limitations, comparisons with the shooting star and dragonfly doji, other bearish reversal candles, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is Gravestone Doji?

A doji candlestick is one which has a small body range — almost 5% to 10% of its wick.

A Gravestone Doji candlestick has its open, low and close price at the same level, with a long wick at the top.

It signals potential reversal of price to the downside. As the candlestick opens, price initially goes up — but when sellers enter the market, they push price down, resulting in the close of the candlestick near its open price.

![Gravestone doji candlestick anatomy — open, low and close at the same level, with a long upper wick showing seller rejection of the highs](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/gravestone-doji.png)

It is named because its shape resembles a gravestone — having a long upper wick and a small body at the bottom.

Most traders wait for the follow-up candlestick to close below the low of the gravestone candlestick as a confirmation of the gravestone doji.

#### How to Use Gravestone Doji

To trade using gravestone doji, look for a bullish market trend approaching a strong resistance area.

When price approaches the resistance zone, it gets strong rejection as sellers enter the market. Wait for the candlestick to close.

When you see a gravestone doji candlestick forming at the resistance zone, wait for the next candlestick to close below the low of the gravestone doji — it will confirm the reversal of price from the resistance zone.

After the confirmation of the gravestone doji, wait for price to retrace back and test the gravestone doji candlestick’s body or wick.

When price approaches the gravestone doji candlestick, you can execute a sell trade.

![Gravestone doji trade example — bullish trend tests resistance, gravestone doji forms with confirmation candle, then retraces back to the doji body for the sell entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/gravestone-trading.png)

#### Stop Loss and Take Profit

When you sell at the resistance using a gravestone doji, your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips above the high of the gravestone doji.

For profit target, set your take profit at the next support level — keeping in view your risk-reward ratio.

#### What Does Gravestone Doji Tell Us?

Gravestone doji informs about the losing control of buyers and the entrance of sellers in the market — which indicates the potential reversal of price.

#### Limitations of Gravestone Doji

Gravestone doji sometimes may fail as an indicator of reversal — price may go up above the high of the gravestone doji.

Sometimes the follow-up candlestick may close much lower and price may not retrace back, so traders may not get into a sell position because of the larger stop loss disturbing the risk-reward ratio.

Gravestone doji candlestick does not define the profit target — so you have to use other strategies to find a safe exit.

#### Gravestone Doji vs Shooting Star Candlestick

Both gravestone doji and shooting star are bearish reversal candlesticks formed at the end of an uptrend.

But gravestone doji has no real body — its open, low and close price are almost the same. The shooting star candlestick has a small real body, either bullish or bearish.

#### Can We Use Gravestone Doji for Scalping?

Yes — gravestone doji can be used for scalping on lower timeframes like 15-minute or 5-minute.

But keep in mind that lower timeframes have less reliability as compared to higher timeframes, and they offer low risk-to-reward as well.

#### Gravestone Doji vs Dragonfly Doji

Both are doji candlesticks with almost no real bodies, and both candlesticks indicate reversal of price.

The main difference between gravestone doji and [dragonfly doji](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/dragonfly-doji-candlestick/>) is the direction of price.

Gravestone doji has a long UPPER wick — and its formation at a resistance level indicates lower prices in the future. Dragonfly doji has a long LOWER wick — and its formation at a support level indicates bullish reversal.

#### Gravestone Doji in the ICT Framework

In ICT terms, the gravestone doji is the single-candle signature of an [inducement sweep](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) above resistance followed by a strong rejection.

The long upper wick is the sweep — buyers grab the liquidity above resistance. The close at the open is the candle-level [change in state of delivery (CISD)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-change-in-the-state-of-delivery/>) — buyers exhausted, sellers in control.

For the highest-conviction setup, look for gravestone doji at a higher-timeframe supply zone or bearish PD array, then drop to the 15-minute for the lower-timeframe MSS confirmation before entering.

#### Step-by-Step Gravestone Doji Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run for a gravestone doji trade.

  1. **Identify the trend.** Bullish — making higher highs and higher lows.
  2. **Mark the resistance zone.** Strong horizontal resistance, ideally aligned with a higher-timeframe PD array.
  3. **Wait for price to test resistance.** Patience is mandatory.
  4. **Watch for the gravestone doji to print.** Open, low and close at the same level with a long upper wick.
  5. **Wait for the candle to close.** Only the closed candle counts.
  6. **Wait for the confirmation candle.** Next candle must close below the gravestone’s low.
  7. **Wait for the retest.** Price retraces back to test the gravestone’s body or wick.
  8. **Enter the sell.** On the retest, with bearish confirmation or lower-timeframe MSS.
  9. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips above the high of the gravestone doji.
  10. **Set the target.** Next support level, keeping the risk-reward ratio in view.

#### Best Pairs for Gravestone Doji

Gravestone doji works on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the gravestone doji framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for gravestone reversals.

#### Common Mistakes Around Gravestone Doji

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading the gravestone doji.

  1. **Trading every gravestone doji.** Without a resistance level above the wick, the gravestone is just noise. The level is mandatory.
  2. **Skipping the confirmation candle.** The follow-up candle must close below the gravestone’s low. Without it, the doji may fail.
  3. **Trading gravestone doji on low timeframes.** 5-minute and 15-minute produce many fake gravestones. Higher timeframes (4-hour, daily) are far more reliable.
  4. **Stops too tight.** The 10–20 pip buffer above the high is mandatory — wicks often spike just past the doji high on the second test.
  5. **Confusing gravestone with shooting star.** Shooting star has a small real body; gravestone has no body. Treating them as identical is wrong — the no-body version is stronger.
  6. **Entering before retest.** The clean entry is on the retest of the doji body or wick, not on the confirmation candle close.

#### FAQs about Gravestone Doji Candlestick

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the gravestone doji.

**What is a gravestone doji?**

A single candlestick where open, low and close are at the same level, with a long upper wick. It signals strong bearish rejection from a high and potential reversal downward.

**What does gravestone doji tell us?**

It signals the losing control of buyers and the entrance of sellers, indicating potential reversal of price from an uptrend to a downtrend.

**Where does a gravestone doji form?**

Most reliably at strong horizontal resistance after a bullish trend. Gravestones in the middle of a range or against the trend have a much lower hit rate.

**How do I confirm a gravestone doji?**

Wait for the next candle to close below the low of the gravestone doji. That close confirms sellers have taken control.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

After the confirmation candle, wait for price to retrace back to the gravestone’s body or wick. Enter on the retest with bearish confirmation or lower-timeframe MSS.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

10 to 20 pips above the high of the gravestone doji.

**Where do I take profit?**

Next support level, with risk-reward ratio kept in mind. For extended targets, use the next higher-timeframe PD array.

**What is the difference between gravestone doji and shooting star?**

Both are bearish reversal candles with long upper wicks. Shooting star has a small real body; gravestone has virtually no real body (open and close at the same level). Gravestone is the stronger signal.

**What is the difference between gravestone doji and dragonfly doji?**

Gravestone doji has a long UPPER wick and signals bearish reversal at resistance. Dragonfly doji has a long LOWER wick and signals bullish reversal at support. They are direct opposites.

**Can I use gravestone doji for scalping?**

Yes — on 15-minute or 5-minute timeframes. But lower timeframes are less reliable and offer lower risk-to-reward ratios than 4-hour or daily gravestones.

**Can a gravestone doji fail?**

Yes — sometimes price continues higher above the gravestone high. That is why confirmation candle, resistance level alignment, and trend context are all required filters.

**How does gravestone doji fit in the ICT framework?**

It is a single-candle signature of an inducement sweep at a bearish PD array, with the close at the open functioning as a candle-level CISD. The strongest setup is gravestone doji at a higher-timeframe supply zone with lower-timeframe MSS confirmation.

**Does gravestone doji work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook gravestone dojis at higher-timeframe resistance and supply zones, especially during the New York AM session.
