# Supply and Demand in Forex Trading — Zone Identification, BOS/CHOCH Context & SMC Trade Setup

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/supply-and-demand-in-forex-trading/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/supply-and-demand-in-forex-trading/)

![Supply and Demand in Forex Trading — supply zone above price and demand zone below price marked with break of structure and change of character](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Supply-and-Demand-trading-strategy.png)

Supply and Demand in Forex Trading is the foundational SMC framework that explains every reversal, continuation and balance area on the chart — supply is the area where institutional sellers stack orders and price drops, demand is the area where institutional buyers stack orders and price rises. Once you can identify these zones, every BOS, CHOCH and PD-array entry becomes a refinement of supply or demand.

Price of any asset depends on the supply and demand of that asset in the market.

Supply means availability of the asset against demand. When the supply of an asset is more than the demand, prices may fall.

Demand means willingness of buyers to buy an asset at the market price. When the demand of an asset is more than the supply, prices may rise.

In trading, a supply zone is an area on the price chart where many sell orders are placed — due to which the supply of the asset increases and the price falls. A demand zone is an area on the price chart where many buy orders are placed — due to which the demand increases and the price rises.

In this guide I walk you through the supply and demand trading strategy — the meaning, the BOS / CHOCH context, the supply and demand zone identification, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### Purpose of Supply and Demand Zones

Supply and demand zones are key areas on the price chart that traders pay attention to in order to execute a buy or sell trade. Supply and demand zones can also help capture reversal trades as well as continuation trades in a trending market.

These areas are important because the market often reacts quickly from them — in Smart Money Concepts it is believed that institutional traders enter in this area with huge volume, leading to high supply or demand.

That is why retail traders wait for the market to test these supply or demand zones to execute their sell or buy trades and follow the footprints of institutional traders.

#### How to Identify a Supply and Demand Zone

To identify supply and demand zones in the market, we first have to understand basic market structure.

**(I)[Break of Structure (BOS)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/break-of-structure-bos/>)**

When the market is in a trend, it breaks the previous structure in the direction of the trend and continues — which is called break of structure (BOS).

In a bullish trend the market breaks the previous high and makes a higher high. In a bearish trend the market breaks the previous low and makes a lower low. An illustration is shown below.

![Break of Structure example — bullish trend producing a higher high above the previous swing high to confirm continuation](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/break-of-structure.png)

**(II)[Change of Character (CHOCH)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/change-of-character-choch-in-trading/>)**

When the market changes trend it breaks structure in the opposite direction and starts a new trend — which is called reversal or change of character (CHOCH).

When the market reverses from bearish to bullish, it breaks the previous lower high and makes a new higher high. When it reverses from bullish to bearish, it breaks the previous higher low and makes a new lower low. An illustration is shown below.

![Change of Character example — bullish trend ending with a break below the previous higher low confirming the bearish reversal](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/change-of-character.png)

Once you have understood the structural context, the supply and demand zones can be marked.

#### Supply Zone Identification

A supply zone is the area in the market from which prices fall quickly — so a supply zone is found and is useful in a bearish trend.

Before a change of character from bullish to bearish, and before a break of structure in a bearish trend, a retracement after which prices fall quickly and strongly is called a supply zone. The retracement can be a single bullish candle or a number of bullish candles.

![Supply zone identification example — bullish retracement candle at the top of the leg before a strong bearish move marked as the supply zone](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Supply-zone.png)

#### Demand Zone Identification

A demand zone is the area in the market from which prices rise quickly — so a demand zone is found and is useful in a bullish trend.

Before a change of character from bearish to bullish, and before a break of structure in a bullish trend, a retracement after which prices rise quickly and strongly is called a demand zone. The retracement can be a single bearish candle or a number of bearish candles.

![Demand zone identification example — bearish retracement candle at the bottom of the leg before a strong bullish move marked as the demand zone](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Demand-zone.png)

#### How to Trade Using Supply and Demand Strategy

To trade using supply and demand zones we need to identify the market trend first.

In a bullish trend, after a bullish CHOCH or BOS, we have to identify the demand zone. When the market retraces back and approaches the demand zone, we can buy at that test.

If the demand zone is a large area, we should wait for the market to test at least 50% of the demand zone — in conjunction with other confirmations like a market structure shift on a lower timeframe.

When executing a buy trade at the demand zone, the stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips below the low of the demand zone.

In a bearish trend, after a bearish CHOCH or BOS, we have to identify the supply zone. When the market retraces back and approaches the supply zone, we can sell at that test.

If the supply zone is a large area, we should wait for the market to test at least 50% of the supply zone — in conjunction with other confirmations like a market structure shift on a lower timeframe.

When executing a sell trade at the supply zone, the stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips above the high of the supply zone.

#### Supply and Demand vs ICT Order Block

Supply and demand zones and the [ICT Order Block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-order-block/>) overlap heavily but they are not the same thing.

The order block is the SPECIFIC last opposing candle before the displacement that produced the structural break — a refined, single-candle subset of the broader supply or demand zone.

The supply or demand zone is the WIDER area that includes the order block plus any nearby pullback candles.

Trading with the order block gives a tighter stop and higher reward-to-risk. Trading with the broader S&D zone gives a higher hit rate at the cost of stop width. ICT-style traders often layer them — using the supply / demand zone as the area, then refining to the order block inside it for the entry.

#### Step-by-Step Supply and Demand Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading from a supply or demand zone.

  1. **Read the higher-timeframe context.** 1-day and 4-hour structure — bullish, bearish or neutral.
  2. **Wait for the structural confirmation.** BOS in the trend direction (continuation play) or CHOCH at the higher-timeframe extreme (reversal play).
  3. **Identify the supply or demand zone.** Bullish — the bearish retracement candle (or cluster) before the bullish move that produced the BOS / CHOCH. Bearish — the bullish retracement candle (or cluster) before the bearish move.
  4. **Mark the zone boundary.** High and low of the retracement candles.
  5. **Wait for price to retrace and tap the zone.** If the zone is large, require at least 50% retracement into the zone.
  6. **Drop to the lower timeframe (5-minute or 15-minute).** For the entry trigger.
  7. **Wait for the lower-timeframe MSS at the zone.** A clean break in the bias direction.
  8. **Enter on the post-MSS retest.** At the post-MSS PD array (FVG, OB or breaker).
  9. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips below the demand low (or above the supply high), with a small buffer.
  10. **Take profit at the next draw on liquidity.** Old high or low, relative equal level, or higher-timeframe FVG.

#### Best Pairs for Supply and Demand Trading

Supply and demand works on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the supply and demand framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for supply and demand zone reactions because the algorithmic delivery is most concentrated there.

#### Common Mistakes Around Supply and Demand

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading supply and demand.

  1. **Marking S &D zones without structural context.** Every retracement candle is not a zone. Only the retracement BEFORE a BOS or CHOCH qualifies.
  2. **Trading every zone touch as an entry.** The zone is the area — the entry trigger is the lower-timeframe MSS or order block formed inside the zone.
  3. **Tight stops at the zone boundary.** Stops parked at the zone high / low get hunted on the second test. Always use the 10–20 pip buffer.
  4. **Ignoring zone size.** A 5-pip zone is high-conviction; a 50-pip zone needs the 50% rule before entering.
  5. **Trading S &D against the higher-timeframe trend.** Counter-trend zones have a much lower hit rate than bias-aligned zones.
  6. **Confusing supply / demand with order block.** The OB is the refined single-candle subset of the S&D zone. Trade the OB for tighter stops and higher reward; trade the broader S&D for a higher hit rate.

#### FAQs about Supply and Demand in Forex Trading

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about supply and demand.

**What is supply and demand in forex trading?**

Supply is the availability of an asset against demand — when supply exceeds demand, prices fall. Demand is the willingness of buyers to buy at the market price — when demand exceeds supply, prices rise. Supply and demand zones are price areas where institutional orders create these imbalances.

**What is a supply zone?**

A supply zone is the area on the price chart from which prices fall quickly. It is identified as the bullish retracement candle (or cluster) before the bearish move that produced a BOS or CHOCH.

**What is a demand zone?**

A demand zone is the area on the price chart from which prices rise quickly. It is identified as the bearish retracement candle (or cluster) before the bullish move that produced a BOS or CHOCH.

**How is supply and demand different from ICT order block?**

The ICT order block is the specific last opposing candle before the displacement — a refined, single-candle subset of the broader supply or demand zone. The S&D zone is the wider area; the OB is the refinement inside it.

**How do I trade a demand zone?**

In a bullish trend, after a bullish BOS or CHOCH, mark the demand zone. When price retraces and tests the zone, wait for a lower-timeframe MSS, then buy on the retest. Stop 10–20 pips below the demand low.

**How do I trade a supply zone?**

In a bearish trend, after a bearish BOS or CHOCH, mark the supply zone. When price retraces and tests the zone, wait for a lower-timeframe MSS, then sell on the retest. Stop 10–20 pips above the supply high.

**What if the zone is too large?**

If the zone is a large area, wait for at least 50% of the zone to be tested before considering an entry — combined with a lower-timeframe MSS confirmation.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

10 to 20 pips beyond the zone extreme — below the demand low for buy trades, above the supply high for sell trades.

**Where do I take profit?**

The next draw on liquidity — old high or low, relative equal level, or the next higher-timeframe PD array.

**What timeframe is best for supply and demand?**

Mark the zone on the 1-hour or 4-hour for the higher-timeframe area. Use the 5-minute or 15-minute for the entry trigger.

**Does supply and demand work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook supply and demand reactions, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**Is supply and demand the same as support and resistance?**

Related but not identical. Support and resistance are horizontal lines drawn at price extremes. Supply and demand are zones that include the structural context (BOS or CHOCH) of why those areas matter. S&D adds the institutional-volume layer to traditional S&R.
