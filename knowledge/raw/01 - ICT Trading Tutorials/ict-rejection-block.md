# ICT Rejection Block — Wick Rejection Reversal Setup (Free PDF)

An ICT Rejection Block is the wick of a long-tailed candle that swept liquidity at a swing extreme, where the rejection itself becomes the reversal zone. It is one of the three “block trio” PD Arrays in the ICT toolkit, alongside the Breaker Block and the Mitigation Block, and it has a distinct role: the Rejection focuses on the wick, not the body.

The Rejection Block is what I look for after a clean wick rejection at a swept high or swept low. The candle body did not close past the extreme, but the wick speared into liquidity and reversed sharply. That wick — and the unfilled portion of it — is the entry zone for the reversal.

This guide is the full breakdown of the ICT Rejection Block — what it is, the bullish and bearish variants, the trade flow, the difference from a regular order block and from the other block types, the common mistakes, and the answers to the questions I get most often.

#### What is an ICT Rejection Block?

An ICT Rejection Block is a wick rejection at a swing extreme. The candle reaches into liquidity above an old high or below an old low, the wick gets rejected, and the candle closes back inside the prior range. The wick — specifically the area between the candle’s open/close range and the extreme of the wick — becomes the Rejection Block.

The mechanism is simple: smart money used the wick to trigger retail stops sitting beyond the extreme, then snapped price back. The wick is the footprint of that liquidity raid. When price returns to the wick zone later, the same institutional level is in play, and the reversal often plays out a second time.

#### (I) Bullish ICT Rejection Block

A bullish ICT Rejection Block forms at a swing low after a long-tailed bearish wick has swept liquidity below the low and reversed back inside the prior range.

The wick — the area between the body close of that candle and the low of the wick — is the Rejection Block. When price returns to that zone later, I expect a bullish reaction.

![Bullish ICT Rejection Block example — long bearish wick sweeps swing low, body closes back inside range, wick zone becomes bullish entry](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Bullish-rejection-block.png)

**How to Trade the Bullish Rejection Block**

To trade a bullish Rejection Block, I follow this sequence:

  1. **Set a bullish daily bias** using [ICT Daily Bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-trick/>). Rejection Block trades only work in the direction of the higher-timeframe bias.
  2. **Identify the swing low** that has been swept by a long-tailed bearish candle. The wick must extend clearly below the prior swing low and the candle must close back inside the range.
  3. **Mark the Rejection Block zone** — the wick area between the candle close and the candle low.
  4. **Wait for price to retrace** back to the wick zone. Do not chase the initial reversal leg.
  5. **Drop to a lower timeframe** (5-minute or 3-minute) at the wick tap and watch for a Market Structure Shift to the upside.
  6. **Execute the buy trade** on the wick tap with stop loss below the candle low (the deepest point of the wick), with a small buffer.
  7. **Take profit** at the next significant draw on liquidity — typically the recent swing high or the next old high.

#### (II) Bearish ICT Rejection Block

A bearish ICT Rejection Block forms at a swing high after a long-tailed bullish wick has swept liquidity above the high and reversed back inside the prior range.

The wick — the area between the body close of that candle and the high of the wick — is the Rejection Block. When price returns to that zone later, I expect a bearish reaction.

**How to Trade the Bearish Rejection Block**

The flow mirrors the bullish version:

  1. **Set a bearish daily bias.**
  2. **Identify the swing high** that has been swept by a long-tailed bullish candle. Wick clearly above the prior high, body closes back inside.
  3. **Mark the Rejection Block zone** — the wick area between the candle close and the candle high.
  4. **Wait for price to retrace** back to the wick zone.
  5. **Drop to a lower timeframe** at the wick tap and watch for a Market Structure Shift to the downside.
  6. **Execute the sell trade** on the wick tap with stop loss above the candle high.
  7. **Take profit** at the next significant draw on liquidity — recent swing low or the next old low.

#### ICT Rejection Block vs Order Block

The Rejection Block and the Order Block are related but distinct PD Arrays.

A regular [Order Block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-order-block/>) is the body range of a candle that preceded a strong displacement leg. The trade is on the body of the OB candle.

A Rejection Block is the wick range of a candle that swept liquidity and reversed. The trade is on the wick, not the body.

Both are reversal-zone tools, but the Rejection Block is sharper because the wick gives a tighter, more precise entry zone than the OB body.

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make on the Rejection Block

Five mistakes show up in nearly every Rejection Block comment thread.

  1. **Trading every long-tailed wick as a Rejection Block.** Not every wick is a Rejection. The wick must sweep a clear swing extreme — not just any random spike. Without the swept extreme, the Rejection has no institutional context.
  2. **Stop loss too tight inside the wick.** The stop must sit beyond the deepest point of the wick (the candle high for bearish, the candle low for bullish), with a small buffer. Stops inside the wick get tagged routinely.
  3. **Trading Rejections against the daily bias.** Rejection Block trades work in the direction of the daily bias. Counter-bias Rejections fail far more often than aligned ones.
  4. **Confusing the Rejection with the Breaker.** A Rejection is a wick reversal — the body did not break the OB. A Breaker is a failed OB — the body closed past the extreme. They look similar at a glance but represent opposite trade ideas.
  5. **Forcing the trade in low-volatility windows.** Rejection setups in Asia lunch or NY lunch produce weaker reactions than in London open or NY AM. Pair the Rejection with a kill-zone time for the cleanest entries.

#### Is the ICT Rejection Block Reliable for Trading?

The Rejection Block is one of the most reliable single-candle reversal tools in the ICT method when used inside a clear daily bias and at a swept higher-timeframe extreme. The win rate I see on strict execution sits in the 55-65% range with a 1:2 to 1:3 risk-to-reward target.

The reliability degrades sharply outside those conditions: against the daily bias, on random wicks that did not sweep a real extreme, or in low-volatility sessions. The setup is high quality in the right context and a coin flip outside it.
