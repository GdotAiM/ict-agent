# Bear Flag Pattern Explained — Pole, Flag, Breakout-Retest & Measured-Move Target

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/bear-flag-pattern-explained/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/bear-flag-pattern-explained/)

Bear Flag is one of the most reliable bearish continuation patterns — an energetic vertical “pole” (a strong sell-off) followed by a slower retracement “flag” that, when broken to the downside, signals the next leg lower. #### What is Bear Flag Pattern?

The bear flag pattern is one of the most reliable bearish continuation patterns. Often seen in downtrends, it is formed when there is a strong sell-off followed by a period of consolidation.

The objective of trading this pattern is to catch the next leg down in the trend.

#### Bear Flag Pattern Formation

Bear flag pattern is formed when price has moved down significantly with an energetic move, and at some point it slows down seeking retracement.

The retracement can be due to profit taking of initial sellers.

During the retracement, price may move in the form of a rectangle sloping upward — or it may consolidate between two price levels, making a horizontal rectangle.

So the initial bearish move looks like a vertical pole, and the retracement in the form of a rectangle joined with the pole looks like a flag — which is termed the bear flag pattern.

![Bear flag pattern formation example — energetic vertical bearish pole followed by an upward-sloping flag rectangle, marking the pre-breakout consolidation higher](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bearish-flag-pattern.png)

#### How to Identify Bear Flag Pattern

**(I)** To identify a bear flag pattern, first look for a bearish trend in the market.

**(II)** Now identify the energetic bearish move (the pole).

**(III)** After identifying the trend and a strong bearish move, look for a less energetic retracement move (the flag).

During the retracement, price may make higher highs and higher lows — or equal highs and equal lows by consolidating between two price levels.

Draw a trendline by joining the highs and another trendline by joining the lows. The retracement will form a rising rectangle / wedge shape or a horizontal rectangle shape.

Now join the rectangle with the energetic bearish move by drawing a vertical line. The vertical line indicates the pole and the rectangle indicates the flag — you have a complete bear flag pattern.

#### How to Trade Bear Flag Pattern

To trade a bear flag pattern, after identifying the bear flag, wait for price to break the lower trendline of the bear flag.

When price breaks the lower trendline, wait for a retest of the trendline.

![Bear flag breakout example — bearish candle breaks below the lower trendline of the flag rectangle, signaling the continuation of the original bearish trend](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bear-flag.png)

When price retraces back and retests the lower trendline of the bear flag after breakout, look for some bearish signs — for example a bearish candlestick pattern formation or strong price rejection — as a confirmation.

After the confirmation, you can execute a sell trade.

#### Stop Loss and Take Profit

While trading a bear flag, your stop loss will be above the lower trendline of the bear flag — or as a safety measure, you can set the stop above the upper trendline of the flag, depending on your risk-to-reward ratio.

![Bear flag breakout-and-retest trade example — price breaks the lower trendline, retraces to retest it, and confirms with a bearish rejection candle for the sell entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bear-flag-trading.png)

For take profit, measure the pole length of the bear flag and set your target at the length of the pole, measured from the lower trendline of the flag.

#### Bear Flag in the ICT Framework

Although the bear flag is a classic technical analysis pattern, it sits perfectly inside the ICT framework as a visualization of a bearish [break of structure](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/break-of-structure-bos/>) followed by a [valid pullback](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/3-step-guide-to-master-valid-pullback/>).

The pole is the displacement leg that produced the bearish BOS. The flag is the valid pullback inside that leg. The first valid pullback inside the flag becomes the [inducement](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/what-is-inducement-in-forex/>). The breakout-and-retest entry is the bearish continuation play after the inducement is taken.

If you trade with ICT, treat the bear flag as a visual shortcut for the BOS-plus-inducement setup and add a lower-timeframe [MSS](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) confirmation to filter false breakouts.

#### Step-by-Step Bear Flag Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading a bear flag.

  1. **Confirm bearish trend.** Higher-timeframe structure must be bearish — lower highs and lower lows.
  2. **Identify the pole.** An energetic bearish move with large-body candles and clear displacement.
  3. **Mark the flag.** Draw two trendlines connecting the highs and lows of the retracement — either upward-sloping or horizontal rectangle.
  4. **Wait for the breakout.** Price must close below the lower trendline of the flag.
  5. **Wait for the retest.** Price retraces back to the lower trendline after the breakout.
  6. **Wait for confirmation.** Bearish candlestick pattern, strong rejection wick, or lower-timeframe MSS at the retested trendline.
  7. **Enter the sell.** On the confirmation candle close.
  8. **Set the stop.** Above the lower trendline (tighter) or above the upper trendline (safer).
  9. **Set the target.** Measure the pole length and project it from the breakout point.
  10. **Manage the trade.** Book partials at 1:1 risk-reward, trail to the full pole-projection target.

#### Best Pairs for Bear Flag Trading

The bear flag works on every major instrument that produces trending moves — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the bear flag framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for bear flag breakouts.

#### Common Mistakes Around Bear Flag

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading bear flags.

  1. **Trading a bear flag against the trend.** The pattern only works in a bearish trend. A bear flag inside a bullish trend is just a bull retracement waiting to fail.
  2. **Entering at the breakout without retest.** Many breakouts are fakeouts. Wait for the retest of the lower trendline plus a confirmation candle before entering.
  3. **Marking the flag too early.** The flag needs at least 3 to 5 candles of retracement. Treating a single pullback candle as a flag produces noise.
  4. **Wide flags (50%+ retracement of the pole).** A valid flag retraces 30%–50% of the pole. Beyond 50%, the pattern is no longer a flag — it is a reversal candidate.
  5. **Tight stops below the lower trendline.** Stops at the boundary get hunted on the second test. Place the stop above the lower trendline with a buffer, or above the upper trendline for safer trades.
  6. **Ignoring the pole-height target.** The measured-move target is the disciplined way to take profit. Holding for longer than the pole projection breaks the model.

#### FAQs about Bear Flag Pattern

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the bear flag pattern.

**What is a bear flag pattern?**

A bearish continuation pattern formed by an energetic vertical “pole” (initial bearish move) followed by a slower retracement “flag” (upward-sloping or horizontal rectangle). Breakout below the flag’s lower trendline signals continuation lower.

**How do I identify a bear flag?**

Find a bearish trend, identify the energetic bearish move (pole), then draw two trendlines joining the highs and lows of the retracement that follows. The retracement should form a rising rectangle or horizontal rectangle shape (flag).

**What is the difference between a bear flag and a bear pennant?**

Bear flag has parallel trendlines (rectangle). Bear pennant has converging trendlines (triangle). Both are bearish continuation patterns with the same trade rules — measure the pole and project from the breakout.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

After price breaks below the lower trendline and retests it, enter on the bearish confirmation candle (rejection wick, bearish engulfing, or lower-timeframe MSS).

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

Above the lower trendline (tighter) or above the upper trendline (safer) of the flag, with a small buffer.

**How do I set the take profit?**

Measure the pole height and project it from the breakout point of the lower trendline. This is the measured-move target.

**What timeframe works best for bear flag?**

Bear flag appears on every timeframe. For day trading use 15-minute and 1-hour. For swing trading use 4-hour and daily. Higher timeframes produce more reliable bear flags.

**How does bear flag fit in the ICT framework?**

The pole is the bearish BOS displacement leg. The flag is the valid pullback inside that leg. The breakout-and-retest entry is the post-inducement continuation play. Add a lower-timeframe MSS at the retest for filter.

**Does the bear flag work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook bear flags, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**What if the breakout fails?**

If price breaks above the upper trendline of the flag instead of breaking down, the pattern is invalidated. The trend may be reversing — switch to looking for long setups.

**What is the success rate of the bear flag?**

In a confirmed bearish trend with a clean pole and tight flag, the bear flag historically has a 65–75% breakout-success rate. The success rate drops significantly when traded against the higher-timeframe trend.

**Do bear flag patterns fail?**

Yes — bear flag patterns can fail too. That is why you should look for confirmation like a clear breakout and retest before taking a trade.

**Can I trade the bear flag with no other indicators?**

Yes — the bear flag is self-contained. But pairing it with the higher-timeframe ICT bias and a lower-timeframe MSS at the retest produces a much higher hit rate than the pattern alone.
