# Bull Flag Pattern Trading Strategy — Pole, Flag, Breakout-Retest & Measured-Move Target

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/bull-flag-pattern-trading-strategy/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/bull-flag-pattern-trading-strategy/)

Bull Flag Pattern is one of the cleanest bullish continuation patterns on the chart — an energetic vertical “pole” followed by a slower retracement “flag” that, when broken to the upside, signals the next leg higher. Master the formation and breakout-retest entry and you have a reliable trend-following setup.

This guide walks you through the bull flag pattern from identification to execution with real market examples. By thoroughly studying it and dedicating yourself to practice, you will gain the ability to identify a bull flag pattern and execute trading opportunities with the skill of a professional trader.

#### What is Bull Flag Pattern?

Bull Flag — also known as Bullish Flag — is a bullish chart pattern that indicates higher prices for an asset in the coming days.

Bull Flag is a bullish continuation pattern formed in between a bullish trend, and it signals the continuation of the trend.

#### Bull Flag Pattern Formation

Bull flag is formed when price has moved up significantly with an energetic move, and at some point it slows down seeking retracement.

The retracement can be due to profit taking of initial buyers.

During the retracement, price may move in the form of a rectangle sloping downward — or it may consolidate between two price levels, making a horizontal rectangle.

So the initial bullish move looks like a vertical pole, and the retracement in the form of a rectangle joined with the pole looks like a flag — which is termed the bull flag pattern.

![Bull flag pattern formation example — energetic vertical bullish pole followed by a downward-sloping flag rectangle, marking the pre-breakout consolidation](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/BULL-FLAG-PATTERN.png)

#### How to Identify Bull Flag Pattern

**(I)** To identify a bull flag pattern, first look for a bullish trend in the market.

**(II)** Now identify the energetic bullish move (the pole).

**(III)** After identifying the trend and a strong bullish move, look for a less energetic retracement move (the flag).

During the retracement, price may make lower highs and lower lows — or equal highs and equal lows by consolidating between two price levels.

Draw a trendline by joining the highs and another trendline by joining the lows. The retracement will form a falling rectangle / wedge shape or a horizontal rectangle shape.

Now join the rectangle with the energetic bullish move by drawing a vertical line. The vertical line indicates the pole and the rectangle indicates the flag — you have a complete bull flag pattern.

![Bull flag identification example — vertical pole drawn from start of bullish move, two trendlines joining highs and lows of the retracement flag rectangle](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/BULLISH-FLAG-PATTERN.png)

#### How to Trade Bull Flag Pattern

To trade a bull flag pattern, after identifying the pattern you have to wait for price to break the upper trendline of the flag.

![Bull flag breakout example — bullish candle breaks above the upper trendline of the flag rectangle, signaling the continuation of the original bullish trend](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/breakout-of-flag.png)

When price breaks the upper trendline, wait for a retest of the trendline.

When price retraces back and retests the upper trendline of the bull flag after breakout, look for some bullish signs — for example a bullish candlestick pattern formation or strong price rejection — as a confirmation.

![Bull flag breakout-and-retest trade example — price breaks the upper trendline, retraces to retest it, and confirms with a bullish rejection candle for the buy entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bullish-flag-trading.png)

After the confirmation, you can execute a buy trade.

#### Stop Loss and Take Profit

While trading a bull flag, your stop loss will be below the upper trendline of the bull flag — or as a safety measure you can set the stop below the lower trendline of the flag, depending on your risk-to-reward ratio.

For take profit, measure the pole length of the bull flag and set your target at the height of the pole, measured from the upper trendline of the flag.

![Bull flag pattern target measurement example — pole height measured from the start of the bullish move to the high of the pole, then projected from the breakout point](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/bull-flag-pattern-strategy-1.png)

#### Bull Flag in the ICT Framework

Although the bull flag is a classic technical analysis pattern, it sits perfectly inside the ICT framework as a visualization of a [break of structure](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/break-of-structure-bos/>) followed by a [valid pullback](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/3-step-guide-to-master-valid-pullback/>).

The pole is the displacement leg that produced the BOS. The flag is the valid pullback inside that leg. The first valid pullback inside the flag becomes the [inducement](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/what-is-inducement-in-forex/>). The breakout-and-retest entry is the bullish continuation play after the inducement is taken.

If you trade with ICT, treat the bull flag as a visual shortcut for the BOS-plus-inducement setup and add a lower-timeframe [MSS](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) confirmation to filter false breakouts.

#### Step-by-Step Bull Flag Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading a bull flag.

  1. **Confirm bullish trend.** Higher-timeframe structure must be bullish — higher highs and higher lows.
  2. **Identify the pole.** An energetic bullish move with large-body candles and clear displacement.
  3. **Mark the flag.** Draw two trendlines connecting the highs and lows of the retracement — either downward-sloping or horizontal rectangle.
  4. **Wait for the breakout.** Price must close above the upper trendline of the flag.
  5. **Wait for the retest.** Price retraces back to the upper trendline after the breakout.
  6. **Wait for confirmation.** Bullish candlestick pattern, strong rejection wick, or lower-timeframe MSS at the retested trendline.
  7. **Enter the buy.** On the confirmation candle close.
  8. **Set the stop.** Below the upper trendline (tighter) or below the lower trendline (safer).
  9. **Set the target.** Measure the pole height and project it from the breakout point.
  10. **Manage the trade.** Book partials at 1:1 risk-reward, trail to the full pole-projection target.

#### Best Pairs for Bull Flag Trading

The bull flag works on every major instrument that produces trending moves — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the bull flag framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for bull flag breakouts.

#### Common Mistakes Around Bull Flag

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading bull flags.

  1. **Trading a bull flag against the trend.** The pattern only works in a bullish trend. A bull flag inside a bearish trend is just a bear retracement waiting to fail.
  2. **Entering at the breakout without retest.** Many breakouts are fakeouts. Wait for the retest of the upper trendline plus a confirmation candle before entering.
  3. **Marking the flag too early.** The flag needs at least 3 to 5 candles of retracement. Treating a single pullback candle as a flag produces noise.
  4. **Wide flags (50%+ retracement of the pole).** A valid flag retraces 30%–50% of the pole. Beyond 50%, the pattern is no longer a flag — it is a reversal candidate.
  5. **Tight stops above the upper trendline.** Stops at the boundary get hunted on the second test. Place the stop below the upper trendline with a buffer, or below the lower trendline for safer trades.
  6. **Ignoring the pole-height target.** The measured-move target is the disciplined way to take profit. Holding for longer than the pole projection breaks the model.

#### FAQs about Bull Flag Pattern

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the bull flag pattern.

**What is a bull flag pattern?**

A bullish continuation pattern formed by an energetic vertical “pole” (initial bullish move) followed by a slower retracement “flag” (downward-sloping or horizontal rectangle). Breakout above the flag’s upper trendline signals continuation higher.

**How do I identify a bull flag?**

Find a bullish trend, identify the energetic bullish move (pole), then draw two trendlines joining the highs and lows of the retracement that follows. The retracement should form a falling rectangle or horizontal rectangle shape (flag).

**What is the difference between a bull flag and a bull pennant?**

Bull flag has parallel trendlines (rectangle). Bull pennant has converging trendlines (triangle). Both are bullish continuation patterns with the same trade rules — measure the pole and project from the breakout.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

After price breaks above the upper trendline and retests it, enter on the bullish confirmation candle (rejection wick, bullish engulfing, or lower-timeframe MSS).

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

Below the upper trendline (tighter) or below the lower trendline (safer) of the flag, with a small buffer.

**How do I set the take profit?**

Measure the pole height and project it from the breakout point of the upper trendline. This is the measured-move target.

**What timeframe works best for bull flag?**

Bull flag appears on every timeframe. For day trading use 15-minute and 1-hour. For swing trading use 4-hour and daily. Higher timeframes produce more reliable bull flags.

**How does bull flag fit in the ICT framework?**

The pole is the BOS displacement leg. The flag is the valid pullback inside that leg. The breakout-and-retest entry is the post-inducement continuation play. Add a lower-timeframe MSS at the retest for filter.

**Does the bull flag work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook bull flags, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**What if the breakout fails?**

If price breaks below the lower trendline of the flag instead of breaking up, the pattern is invalidated. The trend may be reversing — switch to looking for short setups.

**What is the success rate of the bull flag?**

In a confirmed bullish trend with a clean pole and tight flag, the bull flag historically has a 65–75% breakout-success rate. The success rate drops significantly when traded against the higher-timeframe trend.

**Can I trade the bull flag with no other indicators?**

Yes — the bull flag is self-contained. But pairing it with the higher-timeframe ICT bias and a lower-timeframe MSS at the retest produces a much higher hit rate than the pattern alone.
