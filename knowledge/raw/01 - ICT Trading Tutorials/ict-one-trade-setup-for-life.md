Before going forward, you need to understand what draw on liquidity means.

#### What is Draw on Liquidity?

[Liquidity](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/liquidity-in-forex-trading/>) means willing buyers and sellers at the current market price.

In trading terms, liquidity means the liquidation price of active traders — either sellers or buyers.

Draw on liquidity means hunting the liquidity of active traders — hitting their stop losses or activating their pending orders such as sell stops or buy stops.

Draw on liquidity is the sole purpose of price delivery.

#### How to Trade Using ICT One Trade Setup for Life

To trade using the ICT One Trade Setup for Life, follow the steps below as told by ICT himself, and set your TradingView time window to New York local time.

#### (I) PM Session Range

PM session range is the previous day’s price delivery. For the PM session range, look at the 01:30 PM to 04:00 PM (New York local time) range.

Mark the highest high and lowest low of the PM session range. Above the highest high rests the buy-side liquidity; below the lowest low rests the sell-side liquidity.

If your [Daily Bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-explained/>) is bullish, anticipate price to run below the PM session range low to raid the sell-side liquidity and create an [ICT Judas Swing](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-judas-swing-complete-guide/>).

If you are bearish, look for price to run above the PM session high for the liquidity raid and the [ICT Judas Swing](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-judas-swing-complete-guide/>).

![PM session range example for ICT One Trade Setup — 1:30 PM to 4:00 PM NY range marked, with sell-side liquidity below the low and buy-side liquidity above the high](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/PM-session-ranges.png)

After the liquidity raid, wait for an [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) in the direction of your daily bias. Then execute a trade at the retest in the direction of your daily bias.

#### (II) London Session Raid

If the PM session is not a factor or price is far away from the PM session range, look for the London session liquidity raid for trade execution.

Mark the London session range 02:00 AM to 05:00 AM — highest high and lowest low.

On the New York opening, if you are bullish, anticipate a run below the low of the London session range for the sell-side liquidity raid.

If your daily bias is bearish, anticipate a run above the high of the London session range for the buy-side liquidity hunt.

![London session range example — 2:00 AM to 5:00 AM NY range marked, with buy-side liquidity above the London high targeted by a bearish bias](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/london-session-raid.png)

The process after the liquidity hunt is the same for every session — look for a market structure shift in the direction of your daily bias and then execute a trade on the retracement.

#### (III) Opening Range Gaps

The opening gap is the event that takes place when trading stops and the market reopens at 08:30 AM for forex and 09:30 AM (New York local time) for indices like Nasdaq and S&P500.

If your daily bias is bearish and price opens with a gap, wait until New York lunch hour — the algorithm engineers liquidity in the morning and during lunch hour at 01:30 PM NY local time.

After lunch hour, anticipate price to approach the opening gap and then sell off to take the liquidity engineered during the AM session and lunch hour.

#### (IV) New York Lunch Raid

If price has not done anything yet — no PM session raid, no London session raid, no opening gap range — look for the New York lunch raid.

Mark the New York lunch range (highest high and lowest low) from 12:00 PM to 01:30 PM (New York local time).

If you are bearish, look for the New York lunch range high to be raided for buy-side liquidity, and then a market structure shift to the downside to execute a sell trade.

![New York lunch raid example — 12:00 PM to 1:30 PM NY range marked, with the high being raided for buy-side liquidity before a bearish MSS confirms the sell entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/NY-Lunch-Riad.png)

If your daily bias is bullish, look for the New York lunch range low to be raided for sell-side liquidity, and then a market structure shift to the upside to execute a buy trade.

#### (V) AM Session Liquidity Raid (Target)

The previous day’s AM session (09:30 AM to Noon) liquidity is the target.

If you are bullish and have the trade setup, your final target is the previous day’s AM session high.

If you are bearish and have the trade opportunity, your final profit target is the previous day’s AM session low.

#### Step-by-Step One Trade Setup Flow

This is the exact sequence I run every morning to find the One Trade Setup.

  1. **Set your chart to New York local time.** Every session range in the model is defined in NY time.
  2. **Set the daily bias.** 1-day and 4-hour structure plus the higher-timeframe PD array — bullish, bearish or neutral.
  3. **Mark the previous day’s AM session high and low.** 09:30 AM to Noon — this is the final target zone.
  4. **Mark the PM session range.** 01:30 PM to 04:00 PM of the previous day.
  5. **Mark the London session range.** 02:00 AM to 05:00 AM NY time of the current day.
  6. **Mark the NY lunch range.** 12:00 PM to 01:30 PM NY time once the period closes.
  7. **Identify the opening range gap.** Forex 08:30 AM, indices 09:30 AM — note the gap if present.
  8. **Wait for the first session raid against your bias.** Bullish bias — wait for a sell-side raid below one of the session lows. Bearish bias — wait for a buy-side raid above one of the session highs.
  9. **Wait for the lower-timeframe MSS.** 5-minute or 1-minute MSS in the direction of your daily bias after the liquidity raid.
  10. **Enter on the retest.** Take the trade in the bias direction at the post-MSS PD array.
  11. **Set the stop.** Beyond the swept session extreme, with a small buffer.
  12. **Take profit at the AM session target.** Bullish — previous AM session high. Bearish — previous AM session low. Use partial profits at the relative equal level along the way.

#### Common Mistakes Around the One Trade Setup

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading the One Trade Setup.

  1. **Wrong timezone.** Every range is defined in New York local time. Using broker-server time or local time produces the wrong session ranges.
  2. **Trading the first raid that prints, regardless of bias.** The raid must align with your daily bias. A buy-side raid against a bullish bias is a stop hunt, not the setup trigger.
  3. **Skipping the MSS confirmation.** The session raid is the trigger zone — the entry is the post-raid lower-timeframe MSS. Do not enter on the touch of the session level alone.
  4. **Marking the lunch range too early.** The 12:00 PM to 01:30 PM range is only valid AFTER 01:30 PM. Marking it at 12:30 PM produces a half-formed range.
  5. **Targeting the same-day AM session.** The AM session target is the PREVIOUS day’s AM range, not today’s.
  6. **Trading every session raid every day.** The model is a “first opportunity wins” framework. Once one session triggers a clean trade, the rest of the day is monitoring — not new entries.

#### FAQs about ICT One Trade Setup for Life

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the One Trade Setup.

**What is the ICT One Trade Setup for Life?**

A complete daily trading model based on five session-based liquidity raids — PM session, London session, opening range gap, NY lunch raid and the previous AM session as the target — anchored to the daily bias.

**What time zone do I use?**

New York local time. Every range in the model is defined in NY time.

**What is the PM session range?**

01:30 PM to 04:00 PM NY time of the previous day. Mark the highest high and lowest low — buy-side liquidity sits above the high, sell-side liquidity sits below the low.

**What is the London session range?**

02:00 AM to 05:00 AM NY time. Mark the highest high and lowest low for the New York open liquidity raid.

**What is the NY lunch range?**

12:00 PM to 01:30 PM NY time. Mark the highest high and lowest low. Used as the trigger range when no earlier session has produced a setup.

**What is the AM session target?**

The previous day’s 09:30 AM to Noon session high (bullish target) or low (bearish target). This is the final profit target of the model.

**How does the daily bias fit in?**

The daily bias decides which side of every session range you target. Bullish bias — sell-side raids below session lows. Bearish bias — buy-side raids above session highs.

**Where do I enter?**

After the session raid in the direction of your bias, wait for the lower-timeframe MSS, then enter on the retest of the post-MSS PD array. Not on the touch of the session level alone.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

Beyond the swept session extreme, with a small buffer.

**What timeframe is best for the entry trigger?**

5-minute or 1-minute for the MSS. 15-minute for the post-MSS PD array.

**Does this setup work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all respect the same session ranges, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**Why is it called One Trade Setup for Life?**

Because the model repeats every day and covers every session — if you master it, you do not need a second strategy. It scales from scalping to day trading without changing the framework.
