The ICT Order Block is one of the most important PD Arrays in the entire ICT method. It is the area on the price chart where institutions executed a large block of orders before a strong impulse move — and that area becomes a magnet for price every time it returns. Used correctly, the Order Block tells me where institutional buyers or sellers are positioned. Retail traders follow the institutional footprint by waiting for price to retest these zones, then entering alongside the institutions instead of fighting them.

#### What is an ICT Order Block?

An ICT Order Block is the price area where a large number of orders were executed by institutional traders before the market made a sudden, strong move away from that level. It is the visible footprint of institutional positioning on the chart.

The mechanic is simple: institutions cannot fill all of their large orders at one price — that would move the market against them. Instead, they accumulate gradually over a small price range. When their position is fully built, they push price out of that range with force, leaving a clear order block behind.

Retail traders watch for these zones because price tends to return to them. When price comes back to test an Order Block, the unfilled portion of the institutional order gets mitigated, and price reacts sharply — usually continuing in the original direction of the impulse move.

#### Types of ICT Order Block

By market direction, the Order Block splits into two clean types — bullish and bearish — each with its own validation criteria and trading mechanics.

**(I) Bullish Order Block** — the last bearish candle before a strong bullish impulse move.

**(II) Bearish Order Block** — the last bullish candle before a strong bearish impulse move.

Both variants are 2-candle formations at minimum, with the first candle in the opposite direction of the move that follows.

#### Bullish Order Block

A bullish Order Block is the last bearish candle before a bullish impulse move. It typically consists of two candles — the first being bearish (the OB itself) and the second being bullish (the impulse candle that engulfs the OB).

**How to Identify a Bullish Order Block**

To validate a bullish Order Block, four conditions must be true:

  1. The second candle (bullish) must grab the low of the previous bearish candle — price goes below the low of the bearish OB before reversing.
  2. The second candle must close above the high of the previous bearish candle — a clean engulf.
  3. An imbalance (fair value gap) prints on the lower timeframe inside or just above the Order Block zone.
  4. A market structure shift to the upside on the lower timeframe confirms the bullish intent.

In short: the second candle should completely engulf the first — body to body and wick to wick — and that engulf should occur at a place where the lower timeframe shows imbalance and structural shift.

![Bullish ICT Order Block example — last bearish candle before bullish impulse, with bullish engulfing candle taking out the OB low and closing above its high](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/bullish-order-block.png)

#### Bearish Order Block

A bearish Order Block is the last bullish candle before a bearish impulse move. It typically consists of two candles — the first being bullish (the OB itself) and the second being bearish (the impulse candle that engulfs the OB).

**How to Identify a Bearish Order Block**

To validate a bearish Order Block, four conditions must be true:

  1. The second candle (bearish) must grab the high of the previous bullish candle — price goes above the high of the bullish OB before reversing.
  2. The second candle must close below the low of the previous bullish candle — a clean engulf to the downside.
  3. An imbalance (fair value gap) prints on the lower timeframe inside or just below the Order Block zone.
  4. An [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) to the downside on the lower timeframe confirms the bearish intent.

Same rule: the second candle completely engulfs the first — body to body and wick to wick — at a level showing lower-timeframe imbalance and structural shift.

#### Step-by-Step Order Block Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run on every Order Block setup. Save it, print it, do not skip a step.

  1. **Set the daily bias** using [ICT Daily Bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-explained/>). Order Block trades work cleanest in the direction of the higher-timeframe bias.
  2. **Mark the higher-timeframe[PD Array](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/>)** on the daily, 4-hour, or 1-hour chart — the level where institutional positioning is expected.
  3. **Wait for price to approach** the higher-timeframe PD Array. Do not pre-position.
  4. **Drop to the 5-minute or 3-minute chart** at the moment of the tap.
  5. **Watch for the impulse move** in the direction of your bias — large bodies, short wicks, ideally a fair value gap left behind.
  6. **Identify the last opposing candle before the impulse** — the bearish candle before a bullish impulse, or the bullish candle before a bearish impulse. That candle is the Order Block.
  7. **Validate the four conditions** — engulf body and wick, lower-timeframe imbalance, market structure shift in the direction of the impulse.
  8. **Wait for the retest** back to the Order Block zone. Do not chase the impulse.
  9. **Execute the trade** on the OB tap, with stop loss 10–20 pips beyond the OB extreme — below the OB low for bullish, above the OB high for bearish.
  10. **Take profit** at the next significant draw on liquidity in the trade direction.

#### Bullish Order Block Trading Strategy

For a bullish Order Block trade, I look for the shift of price delivery from bearish to bullish. When the prevailing trend is bearish and price approaches a higher-timeframe demand zone (a discount PD Array), I drop to the lower timeframe and watch for the structure to shift to the upside.

The shift creates the impulse move. The last bearish candle before that impulse — the one that just got engulfed by the bullish push — is the bullish Order Block.

I wait for price to retrace back and tap the OB zone. The retest is the buy entry.

![Bullish Order Block trade strategy chart — discount PD Array tap, MSS to upside, bullish OB identified at start of impulse leg, retest as buy entry](https://www.innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/bullish-order-block-trading-strategy.png)

Stop loss on a bullish Order Block trade sits 10–20 pips below the low of the Order Block zone. Take profit targets the next buy-side liquidity above — equal highs, the previous swing high, or the higher-timeframe premium PD Array.

The shift creates the impulse move. The last bullish candle before that impulse — the one that just got engulfed by the bearish push — is the bearish Order Block.

#### Order Blocks in Trends — Continuation Setups

Order Blocks are not only reversal tools. They also form during trend continuation, and these continuation Order Blocks are some of the highest-probability entries in the ICT method.

In a bearish trend, after a bullish pullback, a bearish Order Block may print to confirm the strength of the dominant downtrend. I can use that OB to add a new sell order alongside the existing trend.

Likewise, in a bullish trend, after a bearish pullback, a bullish Order Block may print to confirm the strength of the dominant uptrend. That OB is a clean entry to add a new buy order in the trend’s direction.

The validation rules are identical to a reversal Order Block — engulf body and wick, lower-timeframe imbalance, MSS in the trend direction. What differs is the context: continuation OBs sit in the middle of an existing move, not at the higher-timeframe PD Array reversal point.

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make on Order Blocks

Five mistakes account for the majority of failed Order Block trades I see in the comments. Avoid these and the model converts at a much higher rate.

  1. **Treating any opposing candle as an Order Block.** Not every bearish candle before a bullish move is a valid OB. The four conditions must be true — engulf body and wick, lower-timeframe imbalance, market structure shift, and clean impulse move. Without all four, it is just a candle.
  2. **Trading Order Blocks against the daily bias.** Counter-bias OBs fail far more often than aligned OBs. Filter every setup through the daily bias before acting.
  3. **Stop loss too tight on the OB extreme.** Place the stop 10–20 pips beyond the OB low (bullish) or OB high (bearish), with a small buffer. Stops one pip past the OB get tagged routinely on the typical re-test spike.
  4. **Missing the imbalance / FVG requirement.** An Order Block without an associated fair value gap on the lower timeframe is much weaker. The FVG confirms the impulse was strong enough to qualify the candle as a real OB.
  5. **Confusing an Order Block with a Breaker Block.** An OB is a fresh institutional level — price retraces and continues in the OB direction. A Breaker Block is a failed OB — price closed past it, swept liquidity, and now trades the opposite direction. Same level on the chart, opposite trade idea. #### FAQs About the ICT Order Block

**What is an ICT Order Block?**

An ICT Order Block is the price area where institutional traders executed a large block of orders before a strong impulse move. The OB is the visible footprint of institutional positioning, and price tends to return to retest the level where unfilled orders mitigate.

**What is a bullish Order Block?**

A bullish Order Block is the last bearish candle before a bullish impulse move. It is validated when the next bullish candle takes out the OB low and closes above the OB high, with imbalance and structural shift on the lower timeframe.

**What is a bearish Order Block?**

A bearish Order Block is the last bullish candle before a bearish impulse move. It is validated when the next bearish candle takes out the OB high and closes below the OB low, with imbalance and structural shift on the lower timeframe.

**How do I identify a valid Order Block?**

Four conditions: the impulse candle engulfs the OB body and wick, the lower timeframe shows a fair value gap inside or adjacent to the OB zone, a Market Structure Shift confirms the impulse direction, and the move follows from a higher-timeframe PD Array tap.

**Where do I place stop loss on an Order Block trade?**

10–20 pips beyond the OB extreme — below the OB low for bullish trades, above the OB high for bearish trades. The buffer absorbs the typical re-test spike before the move resolves.

**What is the difference between an Order Block and a Breaker Block?**

An Order Block is a fresh institutional zone — price retraces to it and continues in the OB direction. A Breaker Block is a failed Order Block — price closed past the OB extreme, swept liquidity, and now trades in the opposite direction. Same level on the chart, opposite trade.

**Can Order Blocks be used in trending markets?**

Yes. Continuation Order Blocks form during trends — after a pullback, the OB at the start of the next leg confirms trend strength and gives a clean entry to add to the existing trend.

**What is the best timeframe for Order Blocks?**

Daily and 4-hour for the higher-timeframe Order Blocks that produce the biggest moves. 15-minute and 5-minute for entry-level Order Blocks inside a higher-timeframe PD Array tap. Use both — the higher TF for context, the lower TF for execution.

**What instruments work best for Order Blocks?**

US index futures (NASDAQ 100 / NQ and E-mini S&P 500 / ES) produce the cleanest Order Blocks because the CME session structure delivers tight impulse moves. Major forex pairs (GBP/USD, EUR/USD) and Gold (XAU/USD) also respect OB mechanics.
