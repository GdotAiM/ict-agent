# Pin Bar Trading Strategy — Bullish/Bearish Reversal at S&R with ICT Mapping

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/pin-bar-trading-strategy/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/pin-bar-trading-strategy/)

![Pin Bar Trading Strategy — single-candlestick rejection pattern with long wick and small body at support and resistance for bullish and bearish reversal trades](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PIN-BAR-TRADING-STRATEGY.png)

Pin Bar Trading Strategy is one of the cleanest single-candle reversal signals in price action — a long rejection wick combined with a small body shows that price tried to move past a key level and was forcibly rejected back, signaling that the level has held and a reversal is likely. Mastering pin bar entry at strong support and resistance is one of the highest hit-rate price-action setups in technical trading.

A pin bar pattern consists of a single candlestick price bar that represents a sharp reversal and rejection of price from a key level (support or resistance).

Before going in-depth on pin bar trading strategy, you should learn about [Support and Resistance](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/support-resistance-trading/>) — because a pin bar is mostly reliable at strong support or resistance levels.

In this guide I walk you through the pin bar concept — the candlestick definition, the bullish and bearish variants, the trade execution at support and resistance, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is a Pin Bar Candlestick?

It is a single candlestick pattern that has a long tail (known as shadow or wick) and a small real body as compared to the tail.

Due to the long pin-shaped wick and small bar-shaped body, it is called a Pin Bar candlestick.

The long wick of a pin bar candlestick shows a strong price rejection and signals the reversal of price from a certain price level.

Pin bar candlestick is of two types.

**(I) Bullish Pin Bar** has a long shadow at the bottom and a small body at the top — so it looks like a hammer.

**(II) Bearish Pin Bar** has a long shadow at the top and a small body at the bottom — also known as a [shooting star candlestick](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/shooting-star-candlestick/>).

Below is the illustration explaining the bullish and bearish pin bars.

![Bearish pin bar candlestick example — long upper wick \(rejection from above\) with small body near the bottom, also known as a shooting star pattern](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/bearish-pin-bar.png)

Pin bars are highly reliable on higher timeframes like the week, day or 4-hour.

#### What is Pin Bar Trading Strategy?

Pin Bar Trading Strategy is a combination of the pin bar candlestick pattern and support / resistance strategy.

**Bullish Pin Bar** is mostly traded in a bullish trend, when price moves higher making higher highs and higher lows.

In a bullish trend, mark the strong support zone and wait for price to test that zone.

When price approaches the support zone, wait for the closing of the candlestick and the formation of a Bullish Pin Bar candlestick.

After the formation of a bullish pin bar at the support zone, you may execute a buy trade directly — or wait for price to retrace at least 50% of the pin bar and then execute a buy trade.

In the picture below you can see the XAU/USD daily-timeframe chart where price approached a strong support zone and a pin bar formed there. After the formation of the pin bar, you can see the boom in prices and a successful buy trade.

![Bullish pin bar trade example on XAU/USD daily — price tests strong support, a bullish pin bar prints with long lower wick, buy entry with stop below the pin bar low](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PIN-BAR-TRADING.png)

While trading a bullish pin bar, your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips below the low of the pin bar or below the support zone, whichever is lower.

For profit target, you may mark the next resistance as your take profit.

**Bearish Pin Bar** is reliable in a bearish trend, where prices tend to fall making lower highs and lower lows.

Mark the resistance zones in a bearish trend and wait for price to test the resistance.

When price tests the resistance zone, wait until the close of the candlestick and the formation of a bearish pin bar.

Formation of a pin bar at the resistance zone represents strong rejection and a continuation of the selling trend.

You can execute a sell trade immediately after the formation of the bearish pin bar — or wait for price to retrace at least 50% of the pin bar to execute the sell trade.

In the picture below you can see a XAG/USD daily-timeframe chart where price approached the resistance and made a bearish pin bar signaling a drop in price. You can see the results — a successful sell trade.

![Bearish pin bar trade example on XAG/USD daily — price tests strong resistance, a bearish pin bar prints with long upper wick, sell entry with stop above the pin bar high](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/bearish-pin-bar-trading-strategy.png)

In this case, your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips above the high of the bearish pin bar or above the resistance zone, whichever is higher.

For take profit, you may target the next support level.

#### Pin Bar Quality — How to Pick the Best Ones

Not every pin bar is worth trading. The highest-quality pin bars share these characteristics.

**(1) Long wick relative to body.** The wick should be at least 2x the body, ideally 3x or more. The longer the rejection, the stronger the signal.

**(2) Forms at a higher-timeframe key level.** Daily and 4-hour support / resistance — not 5-minute noise.

**(3) Aligned with the higher-timeframe trend.** Bullish pin bar in an uptrend; bearish pin bar in a downtrend.

**(4) Closes near the opposite end of the wick.** A bullish pin bar should close near its high, with the wick stretching down. A bearish pin bar should close near its low, with the wick stretching up.

**(5) Volume confirmation.** Higher volume on the pin bar candle adds conviction.

#### Pin Bar in the ICT Framework

The pin bar maps onto the ICT framework as the candle-level signature of an [inducement sweep](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) at a higher-timeframe PD array.

The long wick is the sweep — price grabs the liquidity below support (bullish pin bar) or above resistance (bearish pin bar). The small body and close near the opposite end is the candle-level [change in state of delivery (CISD)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-change-in-the-state-of-delivery/>).

In ICT terms, the pin bar is a single-candle CISD at the inducement sweep — which makes it one of the cleanest reversal triggers on the chart.

#### Step-by-Step Pin Bar Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run for a pin bar trade.

  1. **Identify the trend.** Bullish (HH/HL) for bullish pin bar setups, bearish (LH/LL) for bearish pin bar setups.
  2. **Mark the bias-aligned level.** Strong support for a bullish pin bar; strong resistance for a bearish pin bar.
  3. **Wait for price to reach the level.** Patience is mandatory.
  4. **Watch for the pin bar to form.** Long wick (≥ 2x the body) on the rejection side, small body on the opposite end.
  5. **Wait for the candle to close.** Mid-candle action is meaningless — only the closed candle counts.
  6. **Verify quality.** Wick-to-body ratio, alignment with trend, close near opposite end, volume.
  7. **Enter the trade.** Immediately on the pin bar close — or wait for a 50% retracement into the pin bar for a tighter entry.
  8. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips below the pin bar low (buy) or above the pin bar high (sell) — whichever is the more extreme level.
  9. **Set the target.** Next resistance (buy) or next support (sell). For extended targets, project to the next higher-timeframe PD array.
  10. **Manage the trade.** Book partials at 1:1, trail the runner to the full target.

#### Best Pairs for Pin Bar Trading

Pin bars work on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the pin bar framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for pin bar reversals.

#### Common Mistakes Around Pin Bar Trading

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading pin bars.

  1. **Trading every pin bar.** Pin bars are common — but only the ones at higher-timeframe key levels are tradeable. The rest are noise.
  2. **Pin bars on the wrong timeframe.** Daily and 4-hour pin bars are reliable. 5-minute pin bars produce many fakes.
  3. **Trading pin bars against the trend.** Counter-trend pin bars have a much lower hit rate. Stick to bias-aligned pin bars in uptrends and downtrends respectively.
  4. **Tight stops at the pin bar extreme.** The 10–20 pip buffer is mandatory — wicks often spike just past the level on the second test.
  5. **Entering mid-candle.** Pin bars are only valid AFTER the close. Mid-candle reads change before the close completes.
  6. **Ignoring the wick-to-body ratio.** A pin bar with a 1:1 wick-to-body is not a pin bar. Require at least 2:1.

#### FAQs about Pin Bar Trading Strategy

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about pin bar trading.

**What is a pin bar?**

A single candlestick with a long wick (shadow) and a small real body. The long wick shows strong price rejection from a level, and the small body indicates the close occurred near the opposite end of the wick.

**What is the difference between a bullish and bearish pin bar?**

Bullish pin bar — long lower wick with small body near the top (looks like a hammer). Bearish pin bar — long upper wick with small body near the bottom (also called a shooting star).

**What ratio should the wick have to the body?**

The wick should be at least 2x the body, ideally 3x or more. The longer the wick, the stronger the rejection signal.

**What timeframes work best for pin bars?**

Higher timeframes — daily, weekly, and 4-hour — produce the most reliable pin bars. Lower timeframes generate many false pin bars.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

Two options — (1) immediately on the pin bar candle close, or (2) wait for price to retrace 50% into the pin bar for a tighter entry. Option 2 has a lower hit rate but higher reward.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

10 to 20 pips below the low of a bullish pin bar (or below support) for buy trades — 10 to 20 pips above the high of a bearish pin bar (or above resistance) for sell trades, whichever is the more extreme level.

**Where do I take profit?**

Next resistance for a buy trade, next support for a sell trade. For extended targets, the next higher-timeframe PD array.

**Can I trade pin bars without support and resistance?**

The hit rate drops significantly. Pin bars are reliable BECAUSE they form at S&R — without that context, they are just random rejection candles.

**How does the pin bar fit in the ICT framework?**

It is a single-candle signature of an inducement sweep plus CISD at a higher-timeframe PD array. The long wick is the sweep, the small body and close are the CISD.

**What is the difference between a pin bar and a doji?**

A doji has a very small body with wicks of similar length on both sides — signaling indecision. A pin bar has ONE long wick (the rejection side) and a small body on the OPPOSITE end — signaling directional rejection.

**Does the pin bar work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook pin bars at higher-timeframe support and resistance, especially during the New York AM session.

**What is the success rate of pin bar trading?**

Pin bars at higher-timeframe key levels in a confirmed trend historically deliver a 60–70% hit rate. Random pin bars without level context have a much lower success rate.
