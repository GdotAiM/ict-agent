# QML (Quasimodo) Chart Pattern — Bullish/Bearish Reversal Setup, QML Zone & MSS Confirmation

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/qml-chart-pattern/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/qml-chart-pattern/)

![QML Quasimodo chart pattern — reversal pattern with higher high and lower low at the end of a bullish trend, marked as the QML zone between the two highs](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/ICT-Liquidity-Sweep-and-Liquidity-Run_20240811_150951_0000.png)

QML — Quasimodo — is a reversal chart pattern that, when formed at a higher-timeframe support or resistance level, signals the change of trend. It is the structural cousin of the Change of Character (CHOCH) and one of the cleanest pre-CHOCH reversal signals on the chart.

In this guide I walk you through the QML pattern — the meaning, the bullish and bearish variants, the QML zone identification, the step-by-step trade flow, the QML vs CHOCH comparison, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is the QML (Quasimodo) Chart Pattern?

QML (Quasimodo) is a reversal chart pattern widely used by day traders to anticipate price reversals from a key level — a higher-timeframe supply or demand zone, or a support or resistance level.

It is indicated by the **higher high and lower low** at the end of a bullish trend, or **lower low and higher high** at the end of a bearish trend.

On the basis of the trend, the QML pattern has two types — bullish and bearish.

#### (I) Bullish QML (Quasimodo) Pattern

Bullish QML is formed at the end of a bearish trend at a key level.

In a bearish trend price makes lower lows and lower highs. But when price reaches a key support level and makes a higher high that violates the previous lower high, a structure with a lower low and a higher high is formed — that is the bullish QML pattern.

![Bullish QML pattern example — bearish trend reaches a key support level, prints a new lower low, and then breaks the previous lower high to confirm a higher high reversal](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/bullish-QM.png)

It indicates the reversal of price from bearish trend to bullish.

**How to Trade a Bullish QML Pattern**

**(I)** Identify the second-to-last low and the last low before the formation of QML.

**(II)** Mark the area between these two lows (the QML area).

**(III)** Look for imbalances, demand zones, or FTR (failed-to-return) levels in this area.

**(IV)** Execute a bullish trade when price taps this area, or wait for a confirmation like a [Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) on a lower timeframe.

**(V)** Place the stop-loss below the last low (QML low) and target the last high (QML high) for take profit.

![Bullish QML trade example — QML zone between the second-to-last and last low, demand zone refinement, MSS confirmation, buy entry with stop below QML low and target at QML high](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Untitled1.png)

#### (II) Bearish QML (Quasimodo) Pattern

In a bullish trend price makes higher highs and higher lows. However, when price reaches a key resistance level and then makes a lower low, breaking the previous higher low, it forms a structure known as a bearish QML pattern.

![Bearish QML pattern example — bullish trend reaches a key resistance level, prints a new higher high, and then breaks the previous higher low to confirm a lower low reversal](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/bearsih-QM-pattern.png)

This pattern indicates a potential reversal from a bullish trend to a bearish trend.

**How to Trade a Bearish QML Pattern**

**(I)** Identify the second-to-last high and the last high before the formation of QML.

**(II)** Mark the area between these two highs (the QML area).

**(III)** Look for imbalances, supply zones, or FTR levels in this area.

**(IV)** Execute a bearish trade when price taps this area, or wait for a confirmation like a Market Structure Shift on a lower timeframe.

**(V)** Place the stop-loss above the last high (QML high) and target the last low (QML low) for take profit.

![Bearish QML trade example — QML zone between the second-to-last and last high, supply zone refinement, MSS confirmation, sell entry with stop above QML high and target at QML low](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Untitled3.png)

#### Basis of the QML Pattern

QML is basically based on the concept of [Change of Character (CHOCH)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/change-of-character-choch-in-trading/>) — when price changes its trend and breaks the lower high or higher low in a bullish or bearish trend, that is called a change of character, which is a smart money concept.

Sometimes the QML may indicate the reversal of price BEFORE the CHOCH happens — because every high or low is not a structural high or low, and the QML may form without breaking the structural high or low.

#### QML vs CHOCH — How They Relate

QML and CHOCH are closely related but they are not the same level.

CHOCH is the break of the most recent structural higher low (in a bullish trend) or lower high (in a bearish trend) — the structural reversal confirmation.

QML can form earlier — at any non-structural higher low or lower high — and gives an earlier reversal signal that may or may not become a full CHOCH.

In practice, every CHOCH is a QML, but not every QML is a CHOCH. The QML zone is the entry zone; the CHOCH is the bias confirmation.

#### Step-by-Step QML Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading a QML pattern.

  1. **Read the higher-timeframe context.** 1-day and 4-hour charts — identify the trend and the key support / resistance level price is approaching.
  2. **Wait for price to reach the key level.** Bullish QML — at higher-timeframe support or demand. Bearish QML — at higher-timeframe resistance or supply.
  3. **Watch for the QML structure.** Bullish — lower low followed by a higher high that violates the previous lower high. Bearish — higher high followed by a lower low that breaks the previous higher low.
  4. **Mark the QML zone.** Bullish — between the second-to-last low and the last low. Bearish — between the second-to-last high and the last high.
  5. **Refine inside the QML zone.** Look for an imbalance, demand / supply zone, or FTR level inside the zone for the entry.
  6. **Drop to the lower timeframe (5-minute or 15-minute).** For the entry trigger.
  7. **Wait for the lower-timeframe MSS.** A clean break of the most recent counter-trend swing point in the new direction.
  8. **Enter on the post-MSS retest.** At the refinement level inside the QML zone.
  9. **Set the stop.** Below the QML low (bullish) or above the QML high (bearish), with a small buffer.
  10. **Take profit at the QML opposite extreme.** Bullish — target the last high (QML high). Bearish — target the last low (QML low).

#### Best Pairs for QML Trading

QML works on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD. The cleanest QML prints come from instruments that produce strong reversals at key higher-timeframe levels.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the QML framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for QML reversals.

#### Common Mistakes Around the QML Pattern

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading the QML pattern.

  1. **Marking QML without a key level.** A QML structure in the middle of a range is meaningless. The QML only works when it forms at a higher-timeframe support or resistance level.
  2. **Entering blindly at the QML zone.** The QML is the zone — wait for the lower-timeframe MSS confirmation before entering. Blind entries at the zone get hunted regularly.
  3. **Confusing QML with a regular pullback.** A pullback continues the trend. A QML reverses it. The structural difference is the violated higher low / lower high.
  4. **Tight stops at the QML extreme.** Stops parked exactly at the QML low or high get hunted on the second test. Place the stop with a buffer.
  5. **Ignoring the higher-timeframe trend at the entry.** A bullish QML against the daily bearish trend has a much lower hit rate than one aligned with a daily bullish bias.
  6. **Trading every higher-high or lower-low as a QML.** The structure must include both the violated swing AND the new reversal extreme. One leg alone is not a QML.

#### FAQs about the QML Pattern

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the QML pattern.

**What is the QML chart pattern?**

QML (Quasimodo) is a reversal chart pattern indicated by a higher high and lower low (at the end of a bullish trend) or a lower low and higher high (at the end of a bearish trend) at a higher-timeframe support or resistance level.

**Why is the QML pattern significant in trading?**

The QML pattern is significant because it helps traders identify potential trend reversals at key levels, allowing them to enter trades with a higher probability of success.

**Can the QML pattern be used on any timeframe?**

Yes — the QML pattern can be used on any timeframe. However, it is most effective when identified on higher-timeframe support or resistance levels.

**How is the QML pattern used by traders?**

Day traders use the QML pattern to anticipate price reversals from key levels such as higher-timeframe supply or demand zones, and support or resistance levels. It helps in identifying potential turning points in the market.

**What is the difference between QML and CHOCH?**

CHOCH is the break of the most recent structural higher low (bullish trend) or lower high (bearish trend). QML can form earlier — at any non-structural higher low or lower high — giving an earlier reversal signal. Every CHOCH is a QML, but not every QML is a CHOCH.

**Where do I place the stop loss on a QML trade?**

Below the QML low (bullish QML) or above the QML high (bearish QML), with a small buffer. Not at the level itself.

**Where do I take profit?**

The QML opposite extreme — bullish QML targets the last high (QML high); bearish QML targets the last low (QML low). For an extended target, use the next higher-timeframe PD array.

**What confirmation do I wait for before entering?**

A lower-timeframe MSS, fair value gap or order block formed inside the QML zone after price taps it. Do not enter on the touch alone.

**What is the FTR level inside the QML zone?**

FTR (failed-to-return) is a smart-money concept zone where price failed to return after the structural break. It is one of the refinement levels inside the QML zone for the entry.

**Does the QML pattern work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook QML reversals at higher-timeframe key levels, especially during the New York AM session.

**How is QML different from a head-and-shoulders pattern?**

A QML is structurally the inverse of a head-and-shoulders — same three-extreme structure but the entry rules and zone definitions are different. Both signal reversals; QML emphasizes the violation of the previous lower high or higher low as the trigger.
