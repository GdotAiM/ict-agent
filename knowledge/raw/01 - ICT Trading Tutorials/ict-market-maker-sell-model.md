#### What is the ICT Market Maker Sell Model?

The ICT Market Maker Sell Model is the behaviour of price delivery from a bearish PD array to a bullish PD array.

You have to look for the following things before an ICT Market Maker Sell Model:

**(I)** The higher-timeframe market structure should be bearish.

**(II)** The next draw-on-liquidity (or daily bias) should be pointing lower.

**(III)** There should be a buy program on the lower timeframe prior to the higher-timeframe PD array.

#### Components of the ICT Market Maker Sell Model

The Market Maker Sell Model is made up of four components, explained below.

**(I) Original Consolidation** refers to the consolidated range of price between two limits — the area where price built up before the model began.

**(II) Engineering Liquidity** refers to the buy-side move of price making higher lows that act as liquidity when price moves to the sell side.

**(III) Smart Money Reversal** refers to the reversal from the buy side to the sell side, when price reaches the higher-timeframe PD array.

**(IV) Liquidity Hunt** refers to the sweep of the old lows that were made previously during engineering liquidity, and finally the original consolidation area.

#### How to Trade the ICT Market Maker Sell Model

As mentioned above, to trade the ICT Market Maker Sell Model you first look for a bearish market structure and a next draw-on-liquidity to the downside on the higher timeframe.

After the confirmation of these steps you wait for a buy program on the lower timeframe so that price reaches the bearish PD array of the higher timeframe.

When price reaches the higher-timeframe bearish PD array, wait for bearish confirmations like a **Market Structure Shift** and **SMT divergence**.

After these confirmations of sell-side intent, you can execute a sell trade in any fair value gap when price retraces above the market structure shift level.

Your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips above the last swing high (the high made before the market structure shift).

#### Step-by-Step MMSM Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when setting up a Market Maker Sell Model trade.

  1. **Confirm bearish higher-timeframe structure.** Daily and H4 making lower highs and lower lows.
  2. **Confirm the next draw-on-liquidity is below.** The daily bias must point to the sell side.
  3. **Identify the higher-timeframe bearish PD array.** Order block, fair value gap or breaker block in the premium zone of the swing.
  4. **Wait for the buy program on the lower timeframe.** Price climbs into the bearish PD array, building engineering liquidity (higher lows) along the way.
  5. **Watch for the Smart Money Reversal.** Price taps the bearish PD array and stalls.
  6. **Wait for the bearish MSS.** A clean break of the most recent short-term low confirms the reversal.
  7. **Look for SMT divergence.** A correlated pair or instrument should fail to make a new high — confirming the reversal across two charts.
  8. **Enter on the retest.** Sell into a fair value gap or order block when price retraces above the MSS level.
  9. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips above the swing high made before the MSS.
  10. **Take profit at the staged targets.** Old lows from the engineering-liquidity leg first, then the low of the original consolidation, then the higher-timeframe draw-on-liquidity.

#### Best Markets for the MMSM

The Market Maker Sell Model works on every market that the ICT methodology is applied to.

  * **NQ (NASDAQ futures)** and **ES (S &P 500 futures)** — bearish daily and H4 swings produce textbook MMSM setups, especially on news-day reversals.
  * **GBP/USD** and **EUR/USD** — bearish weekly bias paired with a London-killzone buy-program setup is one of the cleanest MMSM patterns on forex.
  * **XAU/USD (Gold)** — bearish MMSM after a bullish run into a higher-timeframe order block delivers some of the largest moves on the chart.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the MMSM is most natural on NQ and ES futures (CME Group). #### Common Mistakes Trading the MMSM

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading the Market Maker Sell Model.

  1. **Trading without a bearish higher-timeframe.** The MMSM only works inside a bearish daily or H4 bias. A bullish HTF pattern that looks like an MMSM is usually a continuation, not a reversal.
  2. **Entering the SMR without an MSS.** The smart-money reversal point is not the trade. The entry only triggers after the lower-timeframe Market Structure Shift confirms intent.
  3. **Skipping SMT divergence.** SMT across a correlated pair (or NQ vs ES) is a powerful filter. Without it, the model produces more false signals.
  4. **Stop too tight.** A stop parked exactly at the MSS swing often gets hunted on the second test. The 10 to 20 pip buffer above the last swing high is non-negotiable.
  5. **One-shot profit-taking.** The MMSM has staged targets — old lows, original consolidation, HTF draw-on-liquidity. Closing the whole position at the first target leaves the largest part of the move on the table.
  6. **Trading the MMSM on a Friday.** Friday-afternoon liquidity is poor and the model frequently fails to deliver to the original-consolidation target before the weekend close. I prefer Monday through Wednesday.

#### FAQs about the ICT Market Maker Sell Model

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the MMSM.

**What is the ICT Market Maker Sell Model?**

The MMSM is a structural model that maps how price moves from a bearish PD array to a bullish PD array. It comprises four components — original consolidation, engineering liquidity, smart money reversal and liquidity hunt — and is used to time precise sell entries.

**What does MMSM stand for?**

MMSM stands for Market Maker Sell Model. The mirror version is the MMBM (Market Maker Buy Model), which maps the move from a bullish PD array to a bearish PD array.

**What are the four components of the MMSM?**

(1) Original Consolidation — the prior range. (2) Engineering Liquidity — higher lows built during the buy program. (3) Smart Money Reversal — the reversal at the higher-timeframe bearish PD array. (4) Liquidity Hunt — the sweep of the engineering-liquidity lows and back into the original consolidation.

**What confirmation do I need before entering the MMSM?**

A Market Structure Shift on the lower timeframe at the higher-timeframe bearish PD array, ideally combined with SMT divergence on a correlated pair.

**Where do I place the stop loss on the MMSM?**

10 to 20 pips above the last swing high — the high made before the Market Structure Shift.

**What are the profit targets for the MMSM?**

Use the Fibonacci tool drawn from the high of the smart money reversal to the low of the MSS to stage the targets. Target the engineering-liquidity old lows first, then the low of the original consolidation, with the final target being the higher-timeframe draw-on-liquidity.

**Does the MMSM work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD produce textbook MMSM patterns, especially when the daily bias is bearish and the smart-money reversal lands inside the New York AM session.

**What is the difference between the MMSM and the MMBM?**

The MMSM is the bearish version (price moves from a bearish PD array to a bullish PD array, sweeping lows). The MMBM is the bullish version (price moves from a bullish PD array to a bearish PD array, sweeping highs). The components mirror each other.

**What is the ICT 2023 model?**

The ICT 2023 model is closely related to the MMSM and MMBM frameworks. The 2023 model formalises a daily template using PD arrays, killzones and the smart-money reversal sequence — the MMSM is the bearish template inside that broader framework.

**Can beginners trade the MMSM?**

Beginners should first master PD arrays, daily bias, market structure shift and fair value gaps. Once those four pillars are clear, the MMSM becomes a high-probability model that turns the components into a single repeatable trade.
