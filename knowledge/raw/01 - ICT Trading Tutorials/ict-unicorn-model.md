# ICT Unicorn Model — Breaker Block & Fair Value Gap Overlap Setup + Free PDF

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-unicorn-model/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-unicorn-model/)

The ICT Unicorn Model is a high-precision trade-execution tool used to maximise the probability of a clean entry. It is the area of overlap between an ICT Breaker Block and an ICT Fair Value Gap — two reliable PD Arrays that, when stacked at the same price level, produce one of the strongest reversal triggers in the ICT toolkit.

The Unicorn is one of the institutional [PD Arrays](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/>) in the ICT method, and it earns the “unicorn” name because the precise overlap between a Breaker Block and a Fair Value Gap is rare. When you spot one, the win rate on the entry is meaningfully higher than either tool used alone.

The fastest way to identify a Unicorn on the chart: mark every Breaker Block first, then check whether any of those Breaker Blocks have a Fair Value Gap overlapping them. The shaded overlap zone is the Unicorn entry area.

This guide is the full breakdown of the ICT Unicorn Model — what it is, how to identify it, the bullish and bearish variants, the step-by-step trade flow, the common mistakes I see traders make, and the answers to the questions I get most often.

#### What is the ICT Unicorn Model?

The ICT Unicorn Model is the area of overlap between an ICT Fair Value Gap and an ICT Breaker Block. By stacking two independent PD Arrays at the same price level, the Unicorn produces a uniquely reliable trade-entry signal that neither tool delivers on its own.

The Breaker Block contributes the directional confirmation — a failed order block flipping into reversal support or resistance after a liquidity sweep. The Fair Value Gap contributes the price-imbalance magnet — a three-candle gap that price returns to rebalance.

Together, the Unicorn marks a future area of support or resistance with both structural and imbalance backing. The model signals an early shift in the direction of price delivery and often precedes a meaningful trend reversal.

#### How to Identify the ICT Unicorn Model

The Unicorn is built from two underlying ICT concepts. Before working with it, make sure both the Breaker Block and the Fair Value Gap are familiar.

**Breaker Block**

A Breaker Block is a failed order block formed after a shift in price structure and a liquidity sweep. The original order block is broken, and that broken zone now acts as support or resistance from the opposite side.

![ICT Breaker Block example showing a failed bearish order block flipping into a bullish breaker after liquidity sweep](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ICT-breaker-Block-1.png)

For the full breakdown of the Breaker Block — bullish, bearish, and the four-condition validation — see my dedicated [ICT Breaker Block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-breaker-block-trading/>) guide.

**Fair Value Gap (FVG)**

A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle price imbalance — the gap between the high of candle 1 and the low of candle 3 (or inverse for bearish), where buy-side and sell-side delivery were not balanced. Price tends to return to rebalance the gap before continuing in the original direction.

![Bullish Fair Value Gap example — three-candle pattern with unfilled gap between candle 1 high and candle 3 low](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Bullish-fair-value-gap.png)

For the full FVG breakdown — strength tiers, identification rules, and trade flow — see my [ICT Fair Value Gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy/>) guide.

**Unicorn Model — Validation Checklist**

To validate an ICT Unicorn Model, three conditions must be true:

  1. A break of a swing high or swing low (the structural shift).
  2. A Breaker Block forming at the broken swing.
  3. A Fair Value Gap overlapping that Breaker Block.

The shaded overlap area between the Breaker Block and the Fair Value Gap is the Unicorn entry zone.

#### Types of ICT Unicorn Model

By market direction, the Unicorn splits into two clean types — bullish and bearish — each with its own entry criteria and stop loss placement.

#### Bullish ICT Unicorn Model

A bullish Unicorn forms at the end of a bearish price move, signalling a potential reversal to the upside. The validation is:

  1. A lower low followed by a higher high (structural shift to the upside).
  2. A bullish Breaker Block overlapping a bullish Fair Value Gap.
  3. A successful retest of the overlap zone — that retest confirms the Unicorn.

The trade is buy on the retest. The stop loss sits 10–20 pips below the low of the candle that created the Fair Value Gap. Take profit targets the next draw on liquidity above — equal highs, the previous swing high, or the higher-timeframe PD Array.

![Bullish ICT Unicorn Model example — bullish breaker overlapping bullish FVG at swing low after structural shift to upside](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Bullish-ICT-unicorn-model-1.png)

#### Bearish ICT Unicorn Model

A bearish Unicorn forms at the end of a bullish price move, signalling a potential reversal to the downside. The validation is:

  1. A higher high followed by a lower low (structural shift to the downside).
  2. A bearish Breaker Block overlapping a bearish Fair Value Gap.
  3. A successful retest of the overlap zone — that retest confirms the Unicorn.

The trade is sell on the retest. The stop loss sits 10–20 pips above the high of the candle that created the Fair Value Gap. Take profit targets the next draw on liquidity below — equal lows, the previous swing low, or the higher-timeframe PD Array.

![Bearish ICT Unicorn Model example — bearish breaker overlapping bearish FVG at swing high after structural shift to downside](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Bearish-ICT-unicorn-model.png)

#### Step-by-Step Unicorn Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run on every Unicorn setup. Save it, print it, do not skip a step.

  1. **Set the daily bias** using [ICT Daily Bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-explained/>). Unicorn trades work best in the direction of the higher-timeframe bias.
  2. **Mark the higher-timeframe[PD Array](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/>)** on the daily, 4-hour, or 1-hour chart — the level where institutions are likely to be positioning.
  3. **Wait for price to approach** the higher-timeframe PD Array in the premium or discount zone.
  4. **Drop to the 15-minute or 5-minute chart** and watch for a Market Structure Shift in the direction of your bias.
  5. **Mark the Breaker Block** created by the structural shift — the failed order block at the swing extreme.
  6. **Check for a Fair Value Gap** overlapping the Breaker Block. No FVG = no Unicorn. Pass on the trade and wait for a different setup.
  7. **Confirm the overlap zone** — the area where the Breaker Block body and the Fair Value Gap intersect. That is the Unicorn entry zone.
  8. **Wait for price to retrace** back to the Unicorn zone. Do not chase the displacement leg.
  9. **Execute the trade** on the retest, with stop loss 10–20 pips beyond the candle that created the FVG.
  10. **Take profit** at the next draw on liquidity in the trade direction.

#### ICT Unicorn Model Trading Strategy

To trade the Unicorn Model effectively, the daily bias has to be correct first. With the bias set, I wait for price to approach a premium or discount higher-timeframe [PD Array](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-pd-array-key-to-trade-execution/>).

When price approaches the PD Array, I watch for the Market Structure Shift. As the structure shifts, I identify the Unicorn — the Breaker Block plus overlapping Fair Value Gap — and wait for the retracement back into that overlap zone. The trade is executed on the retracement.

![ICT Unicorn Model live chart example — daily bias direction, PD Array tap, MSS, retest of breaker-FVG overlap zone for entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/ict-unicorn-model.png)

**Stop Loss** on a bullish Unicorn sits 10–20 pips below the low of the candle creating the Fair Value Gap that overlaps the Breaker Block. On a bearish Unicorn, the stop sits 10–20 pips above the high of that same candle.

**Profit Target** on either variant is the next draw on liquidity — equal highs, equal lows, or any higher-timeframe PD Array sitting in the trade direction.

#### Best Time Frame for the ICT Unicorn Model

The Unicorn is an entry-execution tool, so the best timeframes to spot it are the lower timeframes. The 15-minute chart is the highest timeframe I would use for Unicorn identification; the 5-minute and 3-minute are the sharpest for entry timing.

I do not recommend the 1-minute for Unicorn execution until you have a hundred logged 5-minute setups. The 1-minute amplifies noise and produces too many false overlap zones.

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make on the Unicorn Model

Five mistakes account for the majority of failed Unicorn trades I see in the comments. Avoid these and the model converts at a much higher rate.

  1. **Trading the Breaker Block alone without the Fair Value Gap overlap.** The whole point of the Unicorn is the overlap of two PD Arrays. A Breaker Block by itself is a valid trade — but it is not a Unicorn. If there is no FVG overlapping the Breaker, do not call it a Unicorn and do not size up as if it were.
  2. **Using a stale Fair Value Gap.** The FVG must be the one created during the recent structural shift, not a random older FVG that happens to sit at the same price. The Unicorn is built on a fresh, in-context overlap.
  3. **Trading Unicorns against the daily bias.** Counter-bias Unicorns fail far more often than aligned Unicorns. Filter every setup through the daily bias before acting.
  4. **Stop loss too tight on the FVG candle.** Place the stop 10–20 pips beyond the wick of the FVG candle, with a small buffer. Stops one pip past the FVG are routinely tagged before the retracement plays out.
  5. **Confusing the Unicorn with a regular order block retest.** If price retraces to a normal order block and there is no Breaker-FVG overlap, that is an OB trade, not a Unicorn. The two have different probability profiles and should not be sized identically.

#### Final Thoughts

No strategy is foolproof, the Unicorn included. Do not risk all your capital on a single setup, and always trade with a stop loss in place to keep your equity safe. The Unicorn produces some of the cleanest entries in the ICT method when used inside a clear daily bias, at a higher-timeframe PD Array tap, with a confirming Market Structure Shift. Used outside those conditions, it is just two coincidental shapes on the chart.
