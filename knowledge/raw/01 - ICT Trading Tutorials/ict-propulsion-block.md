#### What is an ICT Propulsion Block?

The word “propulsion” means to push something. In trading it is a single candlestick that pushes price away from itself.

The ICT propulsion block is the single candlestick which traded into an [order block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-order-block/> "Order Block") — and price then moved away from it.

When price tests the propulsion candle, it delivers a quick reaction in the form of a sudden price move. That is why the propulsion candle is used as an entry zone — the reaction is fast, the stop is small, and the reward-to-risk is excellent when it works.

#### Bullish ICT Propulsion Block

A bullish ICT propulsion block is the last bearish candlestick that traded into a bullish order block — and from which price then moved up.

![ICT Bullish Propulsion Block diagram — last bearish candle that traded into the bullish order block and propelled price upward](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/ICT-bullish-propulsion-block.png)

The next time price retraces it will take support at the propulsion candlestick and move quickly to the upside.

**How to Trade a Bullish ICT Propulsion Block**

To trade a bullish ICT propulsion block you have to identify the mean threshold (50% retracement level) of the propulsion candlestick by applying the Fibonacci from the high to the low of the propulsion candlestick.

A good propulsion block will not let price go down below the mean threshold.

Now wait for price to retrace back and retest the propulsion candlestick.

You can execute the buy trade when price approaches the body of the propulsion candlestick above the mean threshold level.

![Bullish ICT Propulsion Block trade example — price retraces into the propulsion candle, holds above the mean threshold and propels back up](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/bullish-ICT-propulsion-block.png)

In this case your stop loss will be 10 pips below the low of the propulsion candlestick.

For take profit you can target the next buy-side liquidity level or you can use the [ICT fib levels](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-fibonacci-levels/>) to set your take profit.

#### Step-by-Step ICT Propulsion Block Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading a propulsion block.

  1. **Identify a valid order block.** Bullish OB on a higher-timeframe bullish trend, or bearish OB on a higher-timeframe bearish trend.
  2. **Spot the propulsion candle.** The single candle whose body or wick traded into the order block — and from which price then moved sharply away.
  3. **Mark the mean threshold.** 50% of the propulsion candle’s range. Use the Fibonacci tool: high-to-low for bullish, low-to-high for bearish.
  4. **Wait for the retest.** Price must trade back to the propulsion candle on a later leg.
  5. **Confirm the mean-threshold rule.** A valid propulsion block does NOT let price close beyond the mean threshold on the retest. If it does, the propulsion has failed and the trade is invalid.
  6. **Enter on the body retest.** Buy at the body of the propulsion candle above the mean threshold (bullish), or sell at the body below the mean threshold (bearish).
  7. **Set the stop.** 10 pips below the low of the propulsion candle (bullish) or 10 pips above the high (bearish).
  8. **Take profit at the next liquidity.** Buy-side liquidity for longs, sell-side liquidity for shorts. Alternative: stage exits using the ICT fib extension levels.

#### Is the ICT Propulsion Block Reliable for Trading?

Yes — the ICT propulsion block is reliable for trading because of its accuracy and a small stop loss, resulting in a high reward-to-risk ratio.

The reliability is highest when the propulsion candle sits inside a clearly defined order block and the higher-timeframe bias agrees with the propulsion direction.

#### Can I Trade Using a Propulsion Block Alone?

Yes — you can trade using a propulsion block alone. The propulsion block helps you execute a precise entry, but you should have a correct daily bias to trade accurately.

In practice I prefer the propulsion block as a continuation entry inside an existing higher-timeframe bias, rather than as a standalone signal in choppy conditions.

#### Can I Use the Propulsion Block for Scalping?

Yes — you may use it for scalping, day trading or swing trading as you wish.

The only difference is the timeframe used for identifying the propulsion block.

  * **Scalping** — M1, M3 or M5 propulsion candles inside lower-timeframe order blocks.
  * **Day trading** — M30, 1H or 4H propulsion candles inside intraday order blocks.
  * **Swing trading** — daily and weekly propulsion candles inside higher-timeframe order blocks.

#### Common Mistakes Trading the Propulsion Block

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading propulsion blocks.

  1. **Trading propulsion blocks outside an order block.** The propulsion block is defined as a candle that traded into an order block. Without the OB context, the candle is just a single bar — not a propulsion block.
  2. **Ignoring the mean-threshold rule.** A valid propulsion block does NOT close beyond the mean threshold on the retest. If price closes through the 50% level, the trade is invalid.
  3. **Stop too tight.** The 10-pip buffer below the low (bullish) or above the high (bearish) is the minimum. On gold or indices, scale this to ticks of equivalent dollar value.
  4. **Trading against the daily bias.** A bullish propulsion block in a bearish daily bias often fails on the retest. Always reference the higher-timeframe bias first.
  5. **Wrong Fibonacci direction.** For bullish, draw fib from high to low (so 50% sits at the bullish midpoint). For bearish, draw from low to high. Reversing the direction inverts the mean threshold.
  6. **Confusing propulsion with the order block.** The order block is the broader zone (the bearish candle before a bullish impulse, or the bullish candle before a bearish impulse). The propulsion block is the single candle that traded into the OB after it formed and gave the second push.

#### FAQs about the ICT Propulsion Block

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the propulsion block.

**What is an ICT propulsion block?**

A single candlestick that traded into an order block and pushed price away from it. When price retests the propulsion candle on a later leg, the algorithm typically delivers a sharp reaction — making it a high-probability entry zone.

**How is the propulsion block different from an order block?**

The order block is the broader zone (the last opposite-coloured candle before a strong impulse). The propulsion block is the single candle that traded into the order block after it formed and produced the second push.

**What is the mean threshold of a propulsion block?**

The 50% retracement level of the propulsion candle’s range. Found by applying the Fibonacci tool from the high to the low (for bullish) or low to the high (for bearish) of the propulsion candle.

**Where do I enter a bullish propulsion block trade?**

At the body of the propulsion candlestick above the mean threshold (50%) on the retest, with bias-aligned higher-timeframe context.

**Where do I enter a bearish propulsion block trade?**

At the body of the propulsion candlestick below the mean threshold (50%) on the retest, with bias-aligned higher-timeframe context.

**Where is the stop loss?**

10 pips below the low of the propulsion candle (bullish) or 10 pips above the high (bearish).

**Where is the take profit?**

The next buy-side liquidity level for longs and the next sell-side liquidity level for shorts. Alternatively, stage exits using the ICT fib extensions (-1, -2, -2.5).

**Is the propulsion block reliable?**

Yes — it is one of the more reliable ICT entries because the stop is small and the reward-to-risk is high. Reliability improves further when the propulsion candle sits inside a higher-timeframe order block and the daily bias agrees.

**Can I use the propulsion block for scalping?**

Yes — on M1, M3 or M5. The setup mechanic is timeframe-agnostic; only the timeframe of identification changes.

**What if price closes through the mean threshold?**

The propulsion block has failed. The trade is invalid. Look for the next setup — do not chase price below the mean threshold on a bullish propulsion (or above it on a bearish propulsion).

**Does the propulsion block work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD produce textbook propulsion-block retests, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.
