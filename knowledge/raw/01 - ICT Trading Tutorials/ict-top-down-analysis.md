# ICT Top Down Analysis for High-Probability Trade Setups

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-top-down-analysis/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-top-down-analysis/)

![ICT Top Down Analysis diagram showing the Daily, H4, H1 and M15 timeframes stacked from bias down to entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ICT-TOP-DOWN-ANALYSIS.png)

One of the biggest mistakes I see traders make is opening a chart on a lower timeframe and taking trades without understanding the bigger picture. That usually ends with buying straight into higher timeframe premium, or selling into higher timeframe discount, which is the worst price to enter at.

The whole point of ICT top down analysis is to understand what the higher timeframes are doing first, and only then drop to the lower timeframes to find a precise entry. You stop guessing and start trading with a roadmap.

This guide walks through the full process, what top down analysis actually is, the step by step bullish read from the Daily down to the M15, the bearish version, the common mistakes that wreck the approach, and the questions I get asked most often. If you are new to the building blocks, read the [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) and [premium and discount](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-premium-and-discount-zone-identification/>) guides first, then come back here to tie them together.

#### What is ICT Top Down Analysis?

ICT top down analysis is a trading approach that starts from the higher timeframes and gradually works down to the lower timeframes to build a complete market picture. Instead of making decisions from a single chart, you read the Daily, H4, H1 and M15 together to find the overall direction, the key premium and discount zones, and the high probability entries.

Done properly, top down analysis keeps you aligned with higher timeframe market structure, stops you trading against the dominant trend, and sharpens your entries by using the lower timeframes only for confirmation. Think of each timeframe as having a job:

  * **Daily chart** gives the overall market direction and bias
  * **H4 chart** gives the medium term direction
  * **H1 chart** gives the short term direction
  * **M15 chart** is the entry timeframe where trades are executed

The rule never changes. You always start from the highest timeframe and work your way down, never the other way around.

#### Bullish Top-Down Analysis

Let us walk through a complete bullish scenario from top to bottom.

#### Step 1: Analyze the Daily Chart

The Daily chart is responsible for your overall bias. The first thing to look for is the most recent [Market Structure Shift (MSS)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>). If the Daily has recently shifted to the upside, your overall bias becomes bullish. That does not mean you buy immediately. You first have to know whether price is currently trading in premium or in discount.

**What if price is already in premium?**

If price is already in a premium area, buying is risky because the market has already moved higher. Instead of chasing, you wait patiently for a retracement.

**What if price is in discount?**

This is where you become interested in buying. A discount area gives you a far better risk to reward because you are buying at relatively lower prices. At this point your job becomes finding lower timeframe confirmation to buy. The Daily chart example is shown below:

![ICT top down analysis Daily chart example, recent bullish MSS sets the bias and price trading into the discount area below 50 percent of the range](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Top-Down-analysis-1D.png)

#### Step 2: Move to the H4 Chart

Now look at what the H4 chart is doing.

**Scenario A: H4 is also bullish**

This is the ideal situation. The Daily is bullish, the H4 is bullish, and both timeframes agree. The market is moving the same way on both higher timeframes, so the plan is simple:

  * Focus only on buying
  * Ignore sell setups
  * Keep looking for buying opportunities

The Daily and H4 bullish alignment is shown below:

![ICT top down analysis with Daily and H4 both bullish, the ideal aligned scenario where only buy setups are taken](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/top-down-analysis-d1-h4-same.png)

**Scenario B: Daily is bullish but H4 is bearish**

This means the H4 is currently pulling back against the Daily trend. Treat it as a temporary correction. In this situation:

  * Short term selling opportunities may exist
  * The sell is not meant to start a new downtrend
  * The only objective is for price to reach the Daily discount area

Once price reaches Daily discount, you stop looking for sells, begin searching for buy setups in the Daily discount, and prepare to trade in the direction of the Daily bullish trend. The contradicting Daily and H4 example is shown below:

![ICT top down analysis with bullish Daily and bearish H4, the H4 pullback used as a sell only until price reaches Daily discount](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/top-down-analysis-d1-h4-contradict.png)

#### Step 3: Compare H1 with H4

Now drop to the H1 chart. The purpose of the H1 is to give you more detail about what the H4 is doing.

**H4 bullish and H1 bullish**

This is a strong bullish alignment. The Daily is bullish, the H4 is bullish, and the H1 is bullish, so all the major timeframes agree. This is where many of the highest probability trades happen. Your plan:

  * Look for buy entries
  * Hold buys toward higher timeframe objectives
  * Ignore bearish setups

The H1 and H4 bullish alignment is shown below:

![ICT top down analysis with H4 and H1 both bullish, strong alignment for buy entries toward higher timeframe targets](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/h1-h4-bullish.png)

**H4 bullish and H1 bearish**

This tells you the H1 is making a temporary pullback against the H4 uptrend. In this case a small sell may be possible, and the target of that sell is the H4 discount area. Once price reaches H4 discount, you stop selling, wait for a bullish confirmation, and look to buy with the H4 trend. The bullish H4 and bearish H1 example is shown below:

![ICT top down analysis with bullish H4 and bearish H1, the H1 pullback sold only down to the H4 discount before buying resumes](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/TOP-DOWN-ANALYSIS-H1-H4-Contradict.png)

#### Step 4: Compare M15 with H1

The M15 chart is used for execution. This is where trades are actually entered.

**H1 bullish and M15 bullish**

This is exactly what you want to see. The H1 and M15 are moving in the same direction. Your plan:

  * Look for an M15 bullish confirmation, such as an MSS or a [fair value gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy/>)
  * Enter the buy
  * Target H4 premium or Daily premium levels

**H1 bullish and M15 bearish**

This tells you the M15 is pulling back inside an H1 uptrend. A very short term sell may be possible, with the target being the H1 discount area. That move is often around 100 to 200 points depending on the market. Once H1 discount is reached, you stop looking for sells, wait for an M15 bullish confirmation, and enter buys in the direction of the H1 trend.

#### Bearish Top-Down Analysis

The bearish read is the exact mirror of the bullish one, so once you understand the steps above you already know this. Start on the Daily and look for a recent bearish Market Structure Shift to set a bearish bias. Then check whether price is in premium or discount. You only want to sell from premium, never from discount, because selling into discount is chasing.

Work down the same way. If the H4 agrees and is bearish, focus only on selling and ignore buys. If the H4 is pulling back bullish against a bearish Daily, treat that bullish move as a temporary buy that runs only up to the Daily premium, then look to sell again. Repeat the same logic between H1 and H4, and finally between M15 and H1. Your sells get executed on the M15 once it aligns bearish with the H1, targeting H4 discount or Daily discount. Same cascade, opposite direction.

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make with Top-Down Analysis

A handful of repeatable errors quietly ruin top down analysis for most traders. Avoid these and the approach gets a lot more reliable.

  1. **Starting on the M15 instead of the Daily.** If you open the entry timeframe first and form an opinion, you will force the higher timeframes to fit that bias. Always start at the top and let the Daily decide the direction.
  2. **Buying in premium or selling in discount.** This is the single most common way traders enter at the worst possible price. Buy from [discount](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-premium-and-discount-zone-identification/>), sell from premium, on every timeframe you read.
  3. **Trading the pullback as if it were the new trend.** A lower timeframe move against the higher timeframe is a correction, not a reversal. Trade it only to the higher timeframe premium or discount, then get back in line with the dominant trend.
  4. **Skipping the premium and discount check.** Bias alone is not enough. A bullish Daily still does not mean buy now if price is sitting in premium. The zone tells you whether the entry is at a good price.
  5. **Forcing alignment that is not there.** When the timeframes genuinely disagree and there is no clean read, the correct move is to wait. Not every session offers a clean top down setup.

#### The Golden Rule

Always trade in the direction of the highest timeframe available. The Daily controls the H4, the H4 controls the H1, and the H1 controls the M15. Lower timeframes can move against the higher ones for a while, but they eventually fall back in line with the larger trend.

This is exactly why I start from the Daily and work downward instead of starting on the M15 and guessing where price will go. When you combine market structure shifts with premium and discount zones across several timeframes, you end up with a clear roadmap of where price is likely to move and where the high probability trades actually sit.

#### USA Trading Note

The same Daily to M15 cascade works on the US index futures, the NASDAQ 100 (NQ) and the E-mini S&P 500 (ES). Index futures tend to respect higher timeframe premium and discount very cleanly, so the top down read on ES and NQ is often sharper than on low volume forex pairs. The major USD pairs and Gold (XAU/USD) follow the same logic.

#### Final Thoughts

Top down analysis is the framework that holds every other ICT concept together. Market structure shifts, premium and discount, order blocks and fair value gaps all become far more useful once you know which timeframe is in charge. Get into the habit of reading the Daily first and only entering on the M15, and most of the random, against the trend losses simply stop happening. #### Frequently Asked Questions

**What is ICT top down analysis?**

It is a method of reading the market from the higher timeframes down to the lower ones. You use the Daily for bias, the H4 and H1 for direction, and the M15 for the actual entry, so every trade aligns with what the larger timeframes are doing.

**Which timeframes are used in ICT top down analysis?**

The common set is the Daily, H4, H1 and M15. The Daily sets the overall direction and bias, the H4 and H1 refine that direction, and the M15 is the execution timeframe where you enter the trade.

**Which timeframe should I start with?**

Always start with the highest timeframe, the Daily, and work downward. Starting on the M15 leads you to force a bias and trade against the larger trend, which is the most common mistake in the whole approach.

**What do I do when the timeframes disagree?**

A lower timeframe moving against a higher one is a pullback, not a reversal. Trade that pullback only as far as the higher timeframe premium or discount, then get back in the direction of the higher timeframe trend. If there is no clean read at all, wait.

**How does premium and discount fit into top down analysis?**

Bias tells you the direction, premium and discount tell you the price. In a bullish bias you only buy from discount, and in a bearish bias you only sell from premium. Checking the zone on each timeframe keeps you from entering at a poor price.

**Is top down analysis only for forex?**

No. The same cascade works on the US index futures like ES and NQ, on Gold, and on the major USD pairs. The timeframes and the premium and discount logic stay identical across markets.
