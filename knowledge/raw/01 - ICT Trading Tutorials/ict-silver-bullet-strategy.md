# Master ICT Silver Bullet Strategy — Times, Setup & Free PDF (2026)

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-silver-bullet-strategy/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-silver-bullet-strategy/)

Like most ICT concepts, the Silver Bullet strategy is a time-based algorithmic trading model built on liquidity and fair value gaps. It is designed to capture short-term liquidity in a single one-hour window, which makes it the cleanest scalping setup in the ICT toolkit.

The Silver Bullet happens three times every trading day:

London Session Open Silver Bullet — 03:00 AM to 04:00 AM (New York local time).

New York AM Session — 10:00 AM to 11:00 AM (New York local time).

New York PM Session — 02:00 PM to 03:00 PM (New York local time).

You do not need to sit at the screen all day to trade this model. You only need to be at the chart during the one-hour window of whichever session fits your schedule. Each window is designed to deliver a 20–30 pip move on a clean setup.

This guide is the full breakdown of the Silver Bullet — the setup, the times in EST and GMT, the lower-timeframe execution, the win-rate expectations, the common mistakes, and a fully worked example on XAU/USD.

#### What is the ICT Silver Bullet Strategy?

The ICT Silver Bullet strategy is a time-based algorithmic trading model built on [Liquidity](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/liquidity-in-forex-trading/>) and the [Fair Value Gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy/>). It runs three times a day in a one-hour window each session.

As Michael Huddleston, the founder of ICT, put it: “To quit your job, you need something that repeats every day and yields five handles.” The Silver Bullet is what he was talking about — it repeats every session, every day, and a single clean setup can deliver 20–30 pips.

**How the ICT Silver Bullet Strategy Works**

Price moves for two reasons: to balance an imbalance in delivery, or to take liquidity. The Silver Bullet is built on both of those mechanics.

Before the Silver Bullet window opens, I mark the nearest buy-side and sell-side liquidity on the 15-minute chart. Those two levels become the reference for the session.

If price has already [swept the liquidity](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) on one side, it tends to rotate to the other side to grab the opposite liquidity. If price has run beyond a level instead of sweeping it, it tends to continue in that direction toward the next pool.

When the Silver Bullet window opens, I drop to a 1-minute or 3-minute chart and watch for an [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) in the direction of the next draw on liquidity.

After the MSS, I mark the fair value gap left behind during the displacement leg. I use the [premium and discount](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-premium-and-discount-zone-identification/>) framework to confirm the FVG is in the right zone relative to the recent swing.

When price retraces back to that fair value gap, I execute the scalp.

This is how a clean Silver Bullet looks on the chart.

#### Step-by-Step Silver Bullet Checklist

This is the exact checklist I run before every Silver Bullet session. Save it, print it, do not skip a step.

  1. **Mark the buy-side and sell-side liquidity** on the 15-minute chart before the window opens.
  2. **Wait for the Silver Bullet window to open** — 03:00 AM, 10:00 AM, or 02:00 PM New York local time. Do not pre-position.
  3. **Drop to 1-minute or 3-minute** for execution. The 5-minute can be used for the parent read.
  4. **Watch for a Market Structure Shift** in the direction of the next draw on liquidity.
  5. **Confirm displacement** on the MSS leg — the breaking candle should close decisively and leave a fair value gap behind.
  6. **Mark the fair value gap** created during the displacement leg.
  7. **Validate the FVG sits in the correct premium or discount zone** relative to the most recent swing.
  8. **Wait for price to retrace into the FVG**. Do not chase the displacement leg.
  9. **Execute the trade** on the FVG tap, with stop loss beyond the candle that created the FVG and a take profit at the next liquidity pool — typically a 1:3 risk-reward target.
  10. **Close the trade or trail the stop** within the same session. The Silver Bullet is a scalp, not a swing.

#### Types of Liquidity for the ICT Silver Bullet Strategy

There are seven liquidity types I work with for the Silver Bullet model. These are the references I check on the 15-minute chart before the window opens, in priority order:

**(I)** Previous day high / low draw on liquidity.

**(II)** Previous session high / low draw on liquidity.

**(III)** Established high / low on the 15-minute chart.

**(IV)** Previous week high / low draw on liquidity.

**(V)** Return to the current or old week opening gap.

**(VI)** Expansion away from the current or old week opening gap.

**(VII)** Relative equal highs or lows.

The first three are the strongest references because they cluster the most resting orders. The lower four are intraday refinements.

#### Best Time Frame for the ICT Silver Bullet Strategy

The Silver Bullet is a scalping setup that runs inside a one-hour window, so the execution timeframe should be a lower timeframe — 5-minute, 3-minute, or 1-minute.

I use the 15-minute chart as the parent chart to identify liquidity and bias. For the actual entry, I drop to the 1-minute or 3-minute. The 1-minute gives the sharpest entry but it also magnifies noise — I do not recommend it until you have logged at least a hundred 5-minute setups.

#### Best Pair for the ICT Silver Bullet Strategy

The Silver Bullet was originally designed and tested on US index futures — **NASDAQ 100 (NQ Futures)** and the **E-mini S &P 500 (ES Futures)**. These remain the cleanest instruments for the model because the time-of-day delivery on US index futures is the most predictable.

It also works very well on forex majors and metals: **GBP/USD** , **EUR/USD** , and **XAU/USD** all respect the same kill-zone logic and the same liquidity pools.

For US-based futures traders, ES and NQ are CFTC-regulated futures and execute through a US futures broker (NinjaTrader, AMP, Tradovate, or a prop firm such as Topstep). TradingView is for chart analysis only — order placement happens at the broker.

#### Best ICT Silver Bullet Session

All three Silver Bullet windows produce setups, but the best of the three is the New York AM session (10:00 AM – 11:00 AM EST).

This window is the strongest because the volatility is highest at the New York open and the session overlaps the tail of London. For traders on US index futures, this window also lines up with the equity open at 09:30 AM EST, which adds order flow.

If you can only trade one Silver Bullet session a day, make it the New York AM.

On the 3-minute chart, after the buy-side liquidity raid, price shifts the structure to the downside. The MSS leg leaves behind a fair value gap and an order block — both valid reference points.

#### Win Rate and Realistic Expectations

The Silver Bullet is not a magic strategy. The win rate I see on a strict execution of the checklist is in the 55–65% range, with a 1:3 average risk-reward target — which is enough to be profitable as long as the rules are honoured.

Two factors degrade the win rate fast: (a) trading outside the one-hour window, and (b) entering before the market structure shift confirms. Both account for the majority of losing setups in the comments I get on this strategy.

Beginners should expect six to twelve months of demo work before risking real capital on the Silver Bullet. A hundred logged setups on the 5-minute chart is a reasonable benchmark before going live.

#### How Daylight Saving Time Affects the ICT Silver Bullet Times

Daylight Saving Time (DST) is the US practice of moving clocks forward one hour in spring and back one hour in autumn. The clocks “spring forward” on the second Sunday of March and “fall back” on the first Sunday of November, both at 02:00 AM local.

If your country does not follow DST, the Silver Bullet times in your local time zone shift by one hour twice a year — once in March and once in November.

If your country does follow DST, no adjustment is needed. The session times in NY local stay the same.

The cleanest fix is the one Huddleston himself recommends: regardless of where you live, follow New York local time as the base time. The Silver Bullet windows do not move in NY local time — only the time-zone label changes from EST (Eastern Standard Time) to EDT (Eastern Daylight Time) twice a year.

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make on the Silver Bullet

Five mistakes show up in nearly every Silver Bullet comment thread on the site. Avoid these and the model converts at a much higher rate.

  1. **Trading outside the one-hour window.** The Silver Bullet is a time-based model. Setups outside the window are not Silver Bullet setups, even when the chart pattern looks identical. The clock matters more than the pattern.
  2. **Entering before the market structure shift confirms.** A liquidity raid alone is not a signal. Wait for the lower-timeframe MSS and the displacement candle close before acting. A wick poke into the FVG without an MSS first is a setup that fails far more often than it works.
  3. **Stop loss too tight on the FVG candle.** Place the stop beyond the wick of the candle that created the fair value gap, with a small buffer. Stops sitting one pip beyond the FVG are routinely tagged before the trade resolves.
  4. **Trading the wrong session for your instrument.** US index futures trade cleanest on the New York AM Silver Bullet. Forex majors trade well on London open and NY AM. Picking the wrong session for the instrument shows up as a low-quality setup.
  5. **Exiting before the trade plays out.** The Silver Bullet setup forms inside the window but can take longer than the window to deliver. Honour the original stop and target — do not close the trade early just because the clock has rolled past the window.
