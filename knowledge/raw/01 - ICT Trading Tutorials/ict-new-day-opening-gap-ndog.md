# ICT New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) — 5 PM–6 PM NY Gap, Consequent Encroachment & Trade Setup

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-new-day-opening-gap-ndog/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-new-day-opening-gap-ndog/)

![ICT New Day Opening Gap \(NDOG\) — gap between 5:00 PM close and 6:00 PM open New York time, marked across multiple days for fair value support and resistance](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/New-Day-Opening-Gap_20240827_091132_0000.png)

ICT New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) is the daily fair-value gap that prints when the market closes for one hour each evening — the gap between the 5:00 PM close and the 6:00 PM open in New York local time. Mark several of these gaps on your chart and they become some of the cleanest support, resistance and draw-on-liquidity levels in the ICT framework.

In this guide I walk you through the NDOG concept — the meaning, the identification rule, the consequent encroachment, the bullish and bearish trade scenarios, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is the ICT New Day Opening Gap?

ICT New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) is the gap between the closing price at 5:00 PM New York local time and the opening price at 6:00 PM New York local time — because trading stops for an hour every day from Monday to Thursday.

On Friday, the market closes for the week-end, and the gap on Monday’s opening is called the [ICT New Week Opening Gap (NWOG)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-new-week-opening-gap-nwog/>).

#### Identification of ICT New Day Opening Gap

To identify an ICT New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) you have to mark the closing price at 5:00 PM and the opening price at 6:00 PM.

ICT new day opening gaps act as a magnet for price — price often retests and fills the gaps for fair value because these gaps are also real fair value gaps.

![ICT New Day Opening Gap example — daily NDOG marked between 5 PM close and 6 PM open New York time, with consequent encroachment at 50 percent of the gap](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/ICT-New-Day-opening-Gap.png)

After marking the New Day Opening Gap on the daily chart, always drop into lower timeframes like 15-minute to trade the ICT NDOG.

#### Consequent Encroachment of NDOG

Consequent encroachment is the 50% retracement level (the middle) of the new day opening gap, and it can be the most reactive level of price. You can use the Fibonacci tool to measure the consequent encroachment of the NDOG.

By applying the Fibonacci tool with the inputs 0, 0.5 and 1 from the low to the high of the NDOG, you can find and mark the 50% retracement level of the NDOG.

#### How to Use ICT NDOG

According to ICT you should have at least 5 new day opening gaps — for Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday — annotated on your chart, which will give you the reference to the fair value of price.

These NDOGs will act as support and resistance levels for price as well as draw-on-liquidity levels, and you will see price rejection and accumulation around these areas.

To trade the ICT new day opening gap you should have a [Bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-trick/>) for the next price move.

#### (I) Bullish Scenario

If your bias is bullish and price is above the ICT NDOG, you wait for price to retrace and test the NDOG. After the confirmation of reversal — for example a [Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) on the lower timeframe (5-minute or 15-minute) — you can execute a buy trade targeting the next draw on liquidity.

But if price is below the NDOG and your bias is bullish, then this NDOG will be a draw on liquidity and price will test and close above it. After price closes above the NDOG, it will act as a support for price.

#### (II) Bearish Scenario

If your bias is bearish and price is below the ICT New Day Opening Gap, you wait for price to retrace and test the NDOG. After the bearish confirmation — for example a [Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) on the lower timeframe (5-minute or 15-minute) — you can execute a sell trade targeting the next draw on liquidity.

But if price is above the NDOG and your bias is bearish, then this NDOG will be a draw on liquidity and price will test and close below it. After price closes below the NDOG, it will act as a resistance for price.

#### Step-by-Step NDOG Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading around the New Day Opening Gap.

  1. **Mark the last 5 NDOGs on your daily chart.** Closing price at 5:00 PM NY to opening price at 6:00 PM NY for each weekday.
  2. **Mark the consequent encroachment for each.** Fibonacci 0 / 0.5 / 1 from low to high of every NDOG.
  3. **Set the daily bias.** 1-day and 4-hour structure plus the higher-timeframe PD array.
  4. **Locate price relative to the nearest NDOG.** If price is above the NDOG and bias is bullish, the NDOG is the support / re-entry. If price is below and bias is bullish, the NDOG is a draw on liquidity to be claimed first.
  5. **Drop to the 15-minute (or 5-minute) for the trigger.** Wait for price to tap the NDOG (or the consequent encroachment).
  6. **Wait for confirmation.** Lower-timeframe MSS, fair value gap or order block formed at the NDOG or its consequent encroachment.
  7. **Enter on the retest.** Buy at the bullish NDOG (bias up) or sell at the bearish NDOG (bias down) on the post-MSS PD array.
  8. **Set the stop.** Beyond the NDOG extreme, with a small buffer.
  9. **Take profit at the next draw on liquidity.** Old high or low, relative equal level, or the next NDOG / NWOG above (bullish) or below (bearish).

#### Best Pairs for ICT NDOG Trading

NDOG works best on instruments that observe the 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM NY pause — major currency pairs (GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, USD/JPY) and metals (XAU/USD, XAG/USD).

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the NDOG framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for NDOG retests because the algorithmic delivery is most concentrated there.

#### Common Mistakes Around the NDOG

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading the NDOG concept.

  1. **Marking only one NDOG.** ICT recommends at least 5 — Monday through Friday. A single NDOG out of context is much less useful than a stack of recent NDOGs.
  2. **Using the wrong timezone.** The NDOG is defined in New York local time (5:00 PM to 6:00 PM). Using your broker’s server time or your local time produces a different gap.
  3. **Treating every retest as an entry.** The NDOG is the zone — wait for the lower-timeframe MSS or order block confirmation INSIDE the zone before entering.
  4. **Confusing the NDOG with the NWOG.** NDOG is the daily 5 PM–6 PM gap. NWOG is the Friday-close to Monday-open weekly gap. Different time scales, different draw strength.
  5. **Closing the trade after the NDOG fills.** An NDOG can keep working as support / resistance for several days after it has been filled — do not write it off the moment price touches it.
  6. **Trading the NDOG against the higher-timeframe bias.** NDOGs work best in the direction of the daily bias. Counter-trend NDOG entries have a much lower hit rate.

#### FAQs about ICT NDOG

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the New Day Opening Gap.

**Why is the NDOG significant?**

ICT New Day Opening Gap is considered a liquidity void because no trading activity occurs during that hour, which results in a gap. This gap often acts as a magnet for price movements, with price frequently retesting and filling the gap to achieve fair value.

**Why is it recommended to track multiple NDOGs?**

Tracking multiple NDOGs on your chart helps you understand the market’s fair value over time and provides key support and resistance levels. This information is crucial for making informed trading decisions based on price behavior around these gaps.

**Is the NDOG over after price fills it?**

No — an ICT NDOG can work for several days, which is why it is recommended to keep at least a week of NDOGs on the chart. A filled NDOG can still act as support or resistance on subsequent retests.

**What is the difference between an NDOG and an NWOG?**

NDOG is the daily 5:00 PM–6:00 PM New York gap. NWOG is the weekly Friday-close to Monday-open gap. NWOG generally has a stronger draw on liquidity because it spans the entire weekend close, but both are real fair value gaps.

**What timeframe is best to trade NDOG?**

Mark the NDOG on the daily chart. Drop to 15-minute or 5-minute for the trade trigger and execution.

**What is the consequent encroachment of an NDOG?**

Consequent encroachment is the 50% retracement level (the middle) of the NDOG. Use Fibonacci with inputs 0, 0.5 and 1 from low to high. The 50% level is often the most reactive point inside the gap.

**How many NDOGs should I keep on my chart?**

At least 5 — Monday through Friday — so you have a full week of fair-value reference points. Some ICT traders keep two weeks (10 NDOGs) for a cleaner directional read.

**Where do I place the stop loss on an NDOG trade?**

Beyond the NDOG extreme — below the bullish NDOG low or above the bearish NDOG high, with a small buffer.

**Where do I take profit?**

The next draw on liquidity — old high or low, relative equal level, or the next NDOG / NWOG above (bullish) or below (bearish).

**Does NDOG work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook NDOG retests, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**What if there is no clear NDOG on a given day?**

If the 5:00 PM close and 6:00 PM open are essentially equal, there is no usable NDOG for that day. Move to the next day’s NDOG and the prior NDOGs as your draw-on-liquidity references.
