# Master ICT Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) — Fibonacci Setup with Examples + Free PDF

ICT OTE — Optimal Trade Entry — is the fibonacci retracement zone between an established high and low from which I expect price to reverse. It is one of the cleanest entry tools in the ICT method because the risk at the OTE level is neither too small nor too large, and the risk-to-reward potential is consistently strong.

I use OTE for scalping, intraday trading, and swing trading alike. The same fibonacci levels work across timeframes — what changes is the size of the dealing range and the size of the move that follows.

This guide is the full breakdown of ICT OTE — what it is, the exact fibonacci levels Huddleston uses, how to identify the dealing range, how to trade it in bullish and bearish markets, the common mistakes I see, and the answers to the questions I get most often.

#### What is ICT OTE?

ICT OTE is a trading model built on the retracement of price inside a trending market. The fibonacci retracement levels defined by the Inner Circle Trader are the key to identifying the OTE zone — they are explained below.

When price moves in a trend — bullish or bearish — it does not travel in a straight line. It retraces, gathers liquidity, and continues. OTE is the framework I use to enter on those retracements with controlled risk.

OTE has two clean uses: **joining a continuation** at the retracement of an established trend, and **fading a reversal** at the tap of a higher-timeframe PD Array.

**ICT Fibonacci Levels for the OTE Setup**

To use the ICT optimal trade entry pattern, set your fibonacci retracement levels to the values Huddleston specifies, listed in the table below.

To identify an OTE pattern, set the fibonacci retracement levels first.

The precise OTE level is **0.705**. Any value between **0.62 and 0.79** still sits inside the OTE range and is tradable.

#### Step-by-Step OTE Trade Flow

This is the ten-step checklist I run before every OTE trade. Save it, print it, do not skip a step.

  1. **Establish the daily bias** on the daily and 4-hour charts before the session opens.
  2. **Identify the dealing range** — the most recent established high and low on your trade timeframe.
  3. **Confirm the trend direction** matches the daily bias. Do not take counter-trend OTE without a higher-timeframe PD Array reason.
  4. **Apply the fibonacci retracement** from low to high (bullish) or high to low (bearish) of the dealing range.
  5. **Mark the OTE zone** — the area between 0.62 and 0.79, with 0.705 as the precise level.
  6. **Wait for price to retrace** into the OTE zone. Do not chase.
  7. **Watch for confirmation** at the OTE tap — price rejection, an ICT Market Structure Shift on the lower timeframe, or a clean candle pin.
  8. **Execute the trade** at the OTE level after confirmation, with stop loss beyond the swept extreme of the dealing range.
  9. **Set the take profit** at the opposing extreme of the dealing range or the next significant liquidity pool.
  10. **Manage the trade** — trail the stop to break-even after the first 1:1 of risk-to-reward is captured.

**(II) ICT OTE in a bearish market.**

#### Common Mistakes I See Traders Make on OTE

Five mistakes account for the majority of failed OTE trades I see in the comments. Avoid these and the model converts at a much higher rate.

  1. **Trading OTE against the daily bias.** OTE is a continuation tool first. Counter-trend OTE without a higher-timeframe PD Array reason fails far more often than aligned OTE. Filter every setup through the daily bias.
  2. **Using the wrong dealing range.** The dealing range must use the most recent established high and low — not arbitrary swing points. A bad range produces OTE levels that price ignores.
  3. **Entering at 0.705 without confirmation.** The 0.705 level is the precise OTE, but price often wicks past it before reversing. Wait for a rejection candle or a lower-timeframe MSS at the OTE before pulling the trigger.
  4. **Stop loss inside the dealing range.** Place the stop beyond the extreme of the dealing range that the OTE is retracing from, with a small buffer. Stops inside the range get tagged routinely.
  5. **Ignoring the time of day.** OTE setups taken during low-volatility windows (Asia lunch, NY lunch) deliver weaker results. Pair OTE with kill-zone times for the cleanest entries.

#### Final Thoughts

No strategy is foolproof, OTE included. Do not risk all your capital on a single setup, and always trade with a stop loss in place to keep your equity safe. The 0.705 level is the precise reference, but the real edge in OTE comes from combining it with the daily bias, a higher-timeframe PD Array, and confirmation on the lower timeframe.

#### FAQs About ICT OTE / Optimal Trade Entry

**What is OTE / Optimal Trade Entry in trading?**

ICT OTE stands for Optimal Trade Entry. It is the fibonacci retracement zone between an established high and low from which price is expected to reverse. The precise OTE level is 0.705, with the broader zone running from 0.62 to 0.79.

**Is ICT Optimal Trade Entry a reliable model?**

Yes. OTE is one of the most reliable models in the ICT toolkit because it is a trend-continuation method. As long as the trend is intact, OTE produces clean entries. The model loses reliability when traded against the higher-timeframe bias.

**Is ICT OTE a day-trading strategy?**

OTE is a flexible model. It works for day trading, swing trading, and scalping because the same fibonacci levels apply across timeframes. For swing setups, use the daily timeframe trend and OTE levels for entry. For intraday, use a 1-hour or 15-minute trend and OTE on the lower timeframe.

**Is there a specific time for the ICT OTE setup?**

There is no specific time of day for OTE itself. That said, kill-zone times deliver the cleanest setups because volatility is concentrated there. Look for OTE during the [ICT Kill Zone](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/master-ict-kill-zones/> "ICT Kill Zones") windows for the highest-probability entries.

**What is the precise OTE level?**

The precise ICT OTE level is 0.705 on the fibonacci retracement. Any value between 0.62 and 0.79 sits inside the broader OTE range and is tradable when paired with confirmation.

**Can OTE be used with other ICT concepts?**

Yes — OTE is at its strongest when combined with a higher-timeframe PD Array, the daily bias, and a lower-timeframe Market Structure Shift or CISD as the entry trigger. The fibonacci zone identifies the price; the other tools confirm the timing.
