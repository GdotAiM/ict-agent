#### What is an ICT Bearish Order Block?

The ICT bearish order block is the zone on a price chart where a large number of sell orders are placed by smart money, and the market shows a sudden, strong move from that area.

Retail traders follow smart-money footprints, so they wait for these order-block zones to sell in the market and profit alongside the big institutions like banks.

A bearish order block is a bullish candle followed by a strong bearish engulfing candle.

![ICT Bearish Order Block diagram — last bullish candle followed by a strong bearish engulfing candle that breaks below the bullish candle's low](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Bearish-Order-Block.png)

#### How to Identify a Bearish Order Block

A bearish order block is the last bullish candle before the bearish impulse move. It typically consists of two candles — the first candlestick is bullish and the second candlestick is bearish.

To identify a valid bearish order block you need to check the following four conditions.

**(I)** The second candle (bearish) grabs the high of the previous bullish candle. Price should go above the high of the previous bullish candle.

**(II)** The second candle (bearish) closes below the low of the previous bullish candle.

**(III)** Imbalance on the lower timeframe inside the order-block zone.

**(IV)** Structure shift on the lower timeframe.

To sum it up: the second candle should completely engulf the first candle — body-to-body and wick-to-wick.

#### How to Trade a Bearish Order Block

To initiate a trade based on a bearish order block, it is essential to first identify the prevailing market order flow or trend.

Bearish order blocks typically hold greater reliability within a bearish trend. During a bullish trend they might offer only a brief retracement.

It is advisable to execute trades on bearish order blocks within a bearish market structure.

Once the market trend is determined, the next step is to identify a valid bearish order block as outlined previously.

Upon identifying a bearish order block, it is important to wait for price to retest it.

When price approaches the bearish order block, consider executing a sell trade at around the 50% retracement level of the bearish order block.

For additional confirmation, look for signals such as the [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/> "ICT Market Structure Shift"), especially on lower timeframes like 15-minute or 5-minute.

#### Step-by-Step Bearish Order Block Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run when trading a bearish order block.

  1. **Confirm bearish trend.** Lower highs and lower lows on the daily and H4. The bearish OB works best in a bearish higher-timeframe context.
  2. **Spot the candle pair.** A bullish candle followed by a strong bearish candle that fully engulfs it body-to-body and wick-to-wick.
  3. **Verify the four conditions.** The bearish candle must (a) grab the high of the bullish candle, (b) close below its low, (c) leave an imbalance on the LTF, and (d) print a structure shift on the LTF.
  4. **Mark the order block zone.** The body of the bullish candle defines the zone.
  5. **Mark the next draw on liquidity.** Old low or relative equal low below the OB — this is the profit target.
  6. **Wait for the retest.** Price must come back to the bearish order block.
  7. **Drop to the lower timeframe.** 15-minute or 5-minute for the entry trigger.
  8. **Wait for the LTF MSS.** A clean Market Structure Shift to the downside confirms intent at the OB retest.
  9. **Enter at the 50% mark.** Take the sell at the 50% retracement of the bearish order block.
  10. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips above the high of the order block.
  11. **Take profit at the next draw on liquidity.** The level identified in step 5.

#### Stop Loss and Take Profit

When executing a trade based on a bearish order block, consider placing your stop loss approximately 10 to 20 pips above the high of the order block.

For setting a take profit, you can aim for the next significant liquidity draw — typically a relative equal low or the prior session low.

A typical 1:2 to 1:4 reward-to-risk ratio is achievable on most bearish OB setups when the higher-timeframe trend is aligned.

#### Is the ICT Bearish Order Block Reliable?

Yes — a bearish order block is reliable for trading, mostly in a bearish trend as it offers a continuation of the bearish move.

In a bullish trend, the bearish order block may act as liquidity and price may go higher, breaking the bearish order block. So always reference the higher-timeframe bias before taking a bearish OB trade.

#### Can I Trade Using a Bearish Order Block Alone?

Yes — you can trade solely on the basis of a bearish order block, provided the OB sits in the [ICT Optimal Trade Entry](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-optimal-trade-entry-ote-pattern/> "ICT Optimal Trade Entry") zone (0.62 to 0.79 of the prior swing) inside a bearish trend, and confirmation comes from an [ICT Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/> "ICT Market Structure Shift") on the lower timeframe at the OB.

#### Common Mistakes Trading the Bearish Order Block

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading bearish OB setups.

  1. **Trading bearish OB in a bullish trend.** The bearish OB is a continuation pattern. Against the higher-timeframe bias, it usually fails to hold and price runs through to the next liquidity level.
  2. **Skipping the engulfing rule.** The bearish candle must engulf the bullish candle body-to-body AND wick-to-wick. Partial engulfing is not a valid OB.
  3. **No LTF MSS at the retest.** The retest alone is not the entry. The lower-timeframe Market Structure Shift is the trigger.
  4. **Stop too tight.** Stops parked at the body high of the OB get hunted on the wick. The 10 to 20 pip buffer above the OB high is non-negotiable.
  5. **Trading every two-candle engulfing.** A bullish candle followed by a bearish engulfing in the middle of a range is rarely a valid OB. The setup requires a strong impulse move away from the OB and a lower-timeframe imbalance left behind.
  6. **Mistaking continuation for reversal.** A bearish OB inside an ongoing uptrend is liquidity for the algorithm — not a reversal signal. Price often takes out the OB high to continue up.

#### FAQs about the ICT Bearish Order Block

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the bearish order block.

**What is a bearish order block?**

A bearish order block is the last bullish candle before a strong bearish impulse move. It marks the zone where institutional sell orders were filled and acts as a high-probability sell zone on the retest.

**How do I identify a bearish order block?**

Look for a bullish candle immediately followed by a bearish candle that (a) grabs the high of the bullish candle, (b) closes below its low, (c) leaves a lower-timeframe imbalance, and (d) prints a lower-timeframe structure shift. The bearish candle must engulf the bullish candle body-to-body and wick-to-wick.

**Where do I enter a bearish order block trade?**

At the 50% retracement of the bearish order block on the retest, ideally with confirmation from a lower-timeframe Market Structure Shift on the 15-minute or 5-minute chart.

**Where is the stop loss on a bearish order block trade?**

10 to 20 pips above the high of the bearish order block.

**Where is the take profit on a bearish order block trade?**

The next draw-on-liquidity in the trend direction — typically a relative equal low or the prior session low.

**Is the bearish order block reliable?**

Yes — but mostly in a bearish trend. In a bullish trend, a bearish OB often acts as liquidity for the algorithm rather than as a reversal trigger.

**Can I trade a bearish order block alone without other confluences?**

Yes — provided the OB sits in the OTE zone (0.62 to 0.79 retracement of the prior swing) inside a bearish trend and is confirmed by a lower-timeframe MSS at the retest.

**What is the difference between a bearish order block and a bullish order block?**

A bearish OB is the last up-close candle before a strong down-move (used as a sell zone). A bullish OB is the last down-close candle before a strong up-move (used as a buy zone). The two are mirror opposites — see the [ICT Bullish Order Block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-bullish-order-block/>) guide for the bullish variant.

**What is a valid bearish order block?**

A bearish order block is valid only when the bearish candle fully engulfs the prior bullish candle (body-to-body and wick-to-wick), leaves a lower-timeframe imbalance, and prints a lower-timeframe structure shift. Without all four conditions, the pattern is unreliable.

**What timeframe is best for the bearish order block?**

The 4-hour and daily produce the highest-conviction OBs. The 15-minute and 5-minute are used for the entry trigger (the LTF MSS) at the retest.

**Does the bearish order block work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES, XAU/USD and XAG/USD all produce textbook bearish OB setups, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.
