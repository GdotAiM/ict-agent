# Shooting Star Candlestick — Bearish Reversal at Resistance with Long Upper Tail

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/shooting-star-candlestick/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/shooting-star-candlestick/)

![Shooting Star Candlestick — bearish reversal pattern with long upper wick and small body at resistance, marking the top of price before a bearish reversal](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/shooting-star-candlestick.png)

Shooting Star Candlestick is one of the cleanest bearish reversal candles in price action — a single candle with a long upper “tail” (wick) and a small body that closes near the open, signaling that buyers pushed price up but were overwhelmed by sellers who drove it right back down. When it prints at strong resistance after an uptrend, it is one of the highest-conviction tops in candlestick analysis.

In this guide I walk you through the shooting star — the definition, what it tells us, the confirmation rule, how to trade it, timeframe selection, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ. For the doji variants see [Gravestone Doji](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/gravestone-doji-candlestick/>) (no body) and [Dragonfly Doji](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/dragonfly-doji-candlestick/>) (bullish mirror).

#### What is Shooting Star Candlestick?

It is a single candlestick pattern, and it resembles a shooting star because of its long tail and small body.

Shooting star candlestick is a bearish candlestick pattern which marks the top of price before a reversal.

It is formed when a candlestick opens and moves up — but after that price moves down, coming back to the opening price and closing near the opening price, leaving a long wick to the upside called the tail.

![Shooting star candlestick anatomy — small body near the open with a long upper wick \(tail\) showing buyer rejection from the highs and a return to the open](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/shooting-star-candlestick-pattern.png)

Due to its closing near the opening price, it has a small body — either bullish or bearish.

There can be a little wick at the bottom of the shooting star, but a shooting star without a wick at the bottom is more reliable.

#### What does Shooting Star Candlestick Tell?

A shooting star candlestick shows that at opening, the buyers were strong, leading price to move up significantly.

But the buyers failed to hold price higher, and price came back to the opening price — which shows that the sellers got control and they look strong.

It also indicates that the first bullish move was a trap for retailers, and the real trend is going to be bearish.

#### Confirmation of Shooting Star Candlestick

A shooting star formed at a strong resistance level can be reliable — but if it is forming anywhere else, then you need to confirm the shooting star before trading it.

The follow-up candlestick formed after the shooting star will act as a confirmation of the shooting star candlestick.

If the next candle remains below the high of the shooting star candlestick and closes below the low of the shooting star candlestick, then it is a confirmation of decline in price.

But if the next candle crosses the high of the shooting star or closes above the high of the shooting star, it indicates that the shooting star formed was a false signal and is not reliable.

#### How to Trade Using Shooting Star Candlestick

As discussed earlier, the shooting star is a bearish candlestick — so it acts as a confirmation of decline in price at strong resistance levels.

When a shooting star forms at a resistance level, wait for the follow-up candlestick for the confirmation of the shooting star.

After the confirmation of the shooting star, you can place a sell order when price retraces and tests the body of the shooting star candlestick.

In this scenario, your stop loss will be 10 to 20 pips above the high of the shooting star candlestick.

![Shooting star trade example — bullish trend tests resistance, shooting star prints, confirmation candle closes below the low, retest of body triggers the sell entry](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/shooting-star-candlestick-trading.png)

For take profit, you may target 1:2 risk-reward — or you can target the next support level as your take profit.

#### Timeframe to Trade Shooting Star Candlestick

The shooting star candlestick pattern can be used on any timeframe, but its effectiveness varies.

On shorter timeframes like 5-minute, it might only lead to small moves of 20, 30 or 50 pips.

However, on higher timeframes like 4-hour or daily, it tends to be more reliable and can result in larger trade setups of 100 pips or more.

It is better to use the shooting star pattern on higher timeframes such as 1-hour, 4-hour or daily.

#### Shooting Star vs Gravestone Doji

Both are bearish reversal candles with a long upper wick at resistance. The structural difference is the body size.

**Shooting Star** — has a small real body (either bullish or bearish) near the open.

**[Gravestone Doji](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/gravestone-doji-candlestick/>)** — has virtually no real body (open and close at the same level).

Both signal bearish reversal — the gravestone is the stronger, no-body version; the shooting star is the more common, small-body version. The trade rules are identical: wait for the confirmation candle and enter on the retest of the body.

#### Shooting Star in the ICT Framework

In ICT terms, the shooting star is the single-candle signature of an [inducement sweep](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) above resistance followed by strong rejection.

The long upper wick is the sweep — buyers grab the liquidity above resistance. The close back near the open is the candle-level [change in state of delivery (CISD)](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-change-in-the-state-of-delivery/>) — buyers exhausted, sellers in control.

For the highest-conviction setup, look for shooting star at a higher-timeframe supply zone or bearish PD array, then drop to the 15-minute for the lower-timeframe MSS confirmation before entering.

#### Step-by-Step Shooting Star Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run for a shooting star trade.

  1. **Identify the trend.** Bullish — making higher highs and higher lows.
  2. **Mark the resistance zone.** Strong horizontal resistance, ideally aligned with a higher-timeframe PD array.
  3. **Wait for price to test resistance.** Patience is mandatory.
  4. **Watch for the shooting star to print.** Long upper wick, small body near the open. No or minimal lower wick is more reliable.
  5. **Wait for the candle to close.** Only the closed candle counts.
  6. **Wait for the confirmation candle.** Next candle must remain below the shooting star’s high and close below its low.
  7. **Wait for the retest.** Price retraces back to the body of the shooting star.
  8. **Enter the sell.** On the retest, with bearish confirmation or lower-timeframe MSS.
  9. **Set the stop.** 10 to 20 pips above the high of the shooting star.
  10. **Set the target.** 1:2 risk-reward minimum or the next support level.

#### Best Pairs for Shooting Star

Shooting star works on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the shooting star framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for shooting star reversals.

#### Common Mistakes Around Shooting Star

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading shooting stars.

  1. **Trading every shooting star.** Without a resistance level above the wick, the shooting star is just noise. The level is mandatory.
  2. **Skipping the confirmation candle.** The follow-up candle must close below the shooting star’s low. Without it, the pattern may fail.
  3. **Trading shooting stars on low timeframes.** 5-minute produces many fakes. 1-hour, 4-hour and daily are far more reliable.
  4. **Stops too tight.** The 10-20 pip buffer above the high is mandatory — wicks often spike just past the shooting star high on the second test.
  5. **Confusing shooting star with gravestone doji.** Shooting star has a small body; gravestone has none. Treat them as related but distinct — the no-body version is structurally stronger.
  6. **Entering before retest.** The clean entry is on the retest of the body, not on the confirmation candle close.

#### FAQs about Shooting Star Candlestick

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about the shooting star candlestick.

**What is a shooting star candlestick?**

A single bearish reversal candlestick with a long upper wick (tail) and a small body that closes near the open. It marks the top of price before a reversal — buyers pushed price up but sellers overwhelmed them and drove price back.

**What does shooting star candlestick tell us?**

That buyers were strong at the open and pushed price up, but sellers regained control and forced price back to the open — signaling exhaustion of buyers and the start of a bearish reversal.

**Where does a shooting star form?**

Most reliably at strong horizontal resistance after a bullish trend. Shooting stars in the middle of a range or against the trend have a much lower hit rate.

**How do I confirm a shooting star?**

The follow-up candle must remain below the shooting star’s high AND close below its low. Without that close, the shooting star is unconfirmed.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

After the confirmation candle, wait for price to retrace back to the body of the shooting star. Enter on the retest with bearish confirmation or lower-timeframe MSS.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

10 to 20 pips above the high of the shooting star candlestick.

**Where do I take profit?**

1:2 risk-reward minimum, or the next support level. For extended targets, use the next higher-timeframe PD array.

**What timeframe works best for shooting star?**

1-hour, 4-hour and daily produce the most reliable shooting stars. 5-minute and 15-minute generate many false signals.

**What is the difference between a shooting star and gravestone doji?**

Both have a long upper wick at resistance. Shooting star has a small real body; gravestone doji has virtually no body (open and close at the same level). Gravestone is the stronger no-body version of the same pattern.

**What is the difference between a shooting star and an inverted hammer?**

Same shape — but context differs. Shooting star forms at the top of an uptrend (bearish reversal). Inverted hammer forms at the bottom of a downtrend (bullish reversal hint). The level it forms at decides the meaning.

**Can a shooting star fail?**

Yes — if the next candle crosses or closes above the shooting star’s high, the pattern is invalidated. Always wait for the confirmation candle before committing.

**How does shooting star fit in the ICT framework?**

It is a single-candle signature of an inducement sweep above resistance with a candle-level CISD. The strongest setup is shooting star at a higher-timeframe supply zone with lower-timeframe MSS confirmation.

**Does shooting star work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all produce textbook shooting stars at higher-timeframe resistance and supply zones, especially during the New York AM session.
