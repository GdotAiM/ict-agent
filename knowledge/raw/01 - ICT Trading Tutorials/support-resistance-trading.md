# Support and Resistance Trading Strategy — Zones, Four Pillars & Bullish/Bearish Setups

**Source:** [https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/support-resistance-trading/](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/support-resistance-trading/)

![Support and Resistance Trading Strategy — horizontal support and resistance zones, four pillars \(history, timeframe, freshness, psychology\), bullish and bearish trade execution](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/support-and-resistance-trading-strategy.png)

Support and Resistance Trading Strategy is the foundational price-action framework — support is the historical zone where price has bounced higher, resistance is the zone where price has been rejected lower, and trading at these levels with proper confirmation is one of the most reliable approaches in technical analysis.

Support and resistance are the key elements of price action trading strategy — these are the key areas for price to react (reverse or retrace).

In this guide I walk you through the support and resistance strategy from basic to advanced — the definitions, the four pillars (history, timeframe, freshness, psychology), the identification rules, the bullish and bearish trade execution, the step-by-step trade flow, common mistakes and the FAQ.

#### What is Support and Resistance Trading Strategy?

It is one of the best price action trading strategies, in which traders use support levels to execute a buy trade and resistance levels to execute a sell trade — with other confirmations too, which are discussed later in this guide.

To have a grip on this strategy, you must know about support and resistance levels first.

#### What is a Support Zone?

A support is a horizontal price zone from where traders expect a retracement or reversal of a bearish trend.

It can also be a single price level, but marking a zone of price as support is better.

It is based on **historical price movement** — in the past price had respected this level and bounced back. There can be a single or multiple bounces of price from a single support zone.

**Timeframe** matters — the higher the timeframe, the stronger the support zone.

**Fresh** support levels are more reliable for price reaction.

**Psychology** plays a great role while identifying a support zone — you may prefer a complete number instead of a half / odd number. Price in hundreds and thousands has a psychological connection with support zones (for example, instead of 1999 you may mark 2000 as a support).

**How to Identify a Support Zone**

To identify a support zone, go to a price chart and mark the levels from where price had bounced toward the buy side in the past.

If it is not a single price level, you can mark the complete area as a support zone.

Next time when price tests that zone, you can look for a price reversal or retracement from this area.

In the picture below you can see XAU/USD daily-timeframe chart with a support zone marked from $1805–1815. Price has tested this zone two times and made a support here. When price touches this zone for the third time you can see the result — a boom in price.

![Support zone example on XAU/USD daily — $1805-1815 zone tested twice with bullish reactions, third test produces a strong bullish move higher](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Support-zone.png)

#### What is a Resistance Zone?

Resistance is a horizontal price area where traders anticipate a potential reversal or retracement of a bullish trend.

It can manifest as a single price level but is often identified as a zone.

This identification is grounded in **historical price behavior** — past instances where price respected this level by reversing or retracing.

Within a resistance zone, there may be multiple instances of price bouncing off.

The significance of a resistance zone can vary with the **timeframe** , typically stronger on higher timeframes.

**New** resistance levels are typically considered more reliable for price reactions.

**Psychology** also plays a crucial role in identifying resistance zones, with traders often favoring whole numbers over half or odd numbers. Round numbers — especially in hundreds and thousands — tend to be associated with resistance zones (preferring to mark 2010 instead of 2008 as a resistance level).

**How to Identify a Resistance Zone**

To identify a resistance zone, examine a price chart and identify levels where price has previously fallen impulsively after touching — either once or multiple times.

If the resistance is not confined to a single price point, you can mark the entire area as a resistance zone.

When price approaches this zone again in the future, you can anticipate a potential reversal or retracement from this area.

In the picture below you can see XAU/USD 4H chart where $1975–1955 is marked as a resistance zone. Price has fallen impulsively from this zone two times, making it a strong resistance zone. When price tested this resistance zone for the third time, you can see the result — a crash.

![Resistance zone example on XAU/USD 4H — $1955-1975 zone tested twice with bearish reactions, third test produces a strong bearish crash lower](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/resistance-zone.png)

#### How to Trade Using Support and Resistance Strategy

To trade using this strategy, first identify the trend of the market.

**In a bullish trend** , price makes higher highs and higher lows, resulting in higher prices.

In a bullish trend, support zones tend to be highly regarded as price consistently rises. Price reacts from these support zones because they signify levels where buyers have historically stepped in to drive price higher. In this context, bullish momentum will eventually lead to the breaking of resistance levels.

Mark the support zones and wait for price to test these levels. When price reaches a support zone, wait for bullish confirmations like a candlestick confirmation or chart-pattern confirmation to execute a buy trade.

![Bullish trend support trade example — support zone tested with bullish engulfing candle confirmation, buy entry with stop below support and target at next resistance](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/SUPPORT-AND-RESISTANCE-TRADING.png)

In this case your stop loss will be below the support zone, and for profit you may target the next resistance level.

**In a bearish trend** , price makes lower highs and lower lows, resulting in lower prices.

In a bearish trend, mark the resistance areas and wait for price to test these levels. When price touches the resistance zone, you can execute a sell trade after confirmation — like a bearish candlestick or chart-pattern confirmation.

![Bearish trend resistance trade example — resistance zone tested with bearish rejection candle confirmation, sell entry with stop above resistance and target at next support](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/support-and-resistance-tyrading-strategy.png)

In this case your stop loss will be above the resistance level, and for profit target you can mark the next support level.

#### Support and Resistance vs Supply and Demand

Support and resistance are related to but not identical to [supply and demand](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/supply-and-demand-in-forex-trading/>) zones.

Support and resistance are HORIZONTAL price lines or zones drawn at historical reaction points. Supply and demand are DIRECTIONAL zones identified by the candle pattern that produced the reaction (a sharp bullish move from a demand zone, a sharp bearish move from a supply zone).

Every supply zone acts as resistance, and every demand zone acts as support — but not every resistance is a supply zone, and not every support is a demand zone. S&D adds the structural-context layer (BOS or CHOCH) on top of the horizontal-level S&R.

#### Support and Resistance in the ICT Framework

ICT traders treat support and resistance as a sanity check on top of the structural framework. The cleanest setups occur when an S&R level aligns with a higher-timeframe PD array — for example, a daily support zone that overlaps a daily [fair value gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy/>), a daily [order block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-order-block/>), or a 4-hour bullish breaker.

Use S&R for the higher-timeframe price-target zone, then drop to the 15-minute or 5-minute for the lower-timeframe MSS trigger. The combination filters out random S&R touches that have no institutional structure behind them.

#### Step-by-Step Support and Resistance Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence I run for an S&R trade.

  1. **Identify the trend.** Higher highs and higher lows = bullish. Lower highs and lower lows = bearish.
  2. **Mark the bias-aligned levels.** Bullish — mark support zones below price. Bearish — mark resistance zones above price.
  3. **Filter by the four pillars.** History (multiple bounces), timeframe (higher = stronger), freshness (newer = more reliable), psychology (round numbers).
  4. **Wait for price to reach the level.** Be patient — do not chase.
  5. **Drop to the lower timeframe.** 15-minute or 5-minute for the trigger.
  6. **Wait for confirmation.** Bullish candlestick at support / bearish at resistance, or lower-timeframe MSS in the bias direction.
  7. **Enter on the confirmation candle close.**
  8. **Set the stop.** Below the support zone (buy) or above the resistance zone (sell), with a small buffer.
  9. **Set the target.** Next bias-opposite S&R level — the next resistance for a buy, the next support for a sell.
  10. **Manage the trade.** Book partials at 1:1, trail to the full target.

#### Best Pairs for Support and Resistance Trading

S&R works on every major instrument — GBP/USD, EUR/USD, USD/CAD, plus metals such as XAU/USD and XAG/USD, and indices like NQ and ES.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, the S&R framework maps cleanly onto NQ and ES futures (CME Group) plus regulated forex pairs through US-based brokers. The 09:50 NY-AM [macro window](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-macro-time-based-strategy/>) is a particularly strong place to watch for S&R reactions.

#### Common Mistakes Around Support and Resistance

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first start trading S&R.

  1. **Marking too many levels.** Every wick is not a level. Only the most-tested zones with clear reactions qualify.
  2. **Trading S &R against the trend.** Buy at support in bullish trend, sell at resistance in bearish trend — counter-trend S&R has a much lower hit rate.
  3. **Entering without confirmation.** S&R is the zone — the entry trigger is the candlestick confirmation or lower-timeframe MSS. Touching the zone alone is not enough.
  4. **Stops parked exactly at the zone.** S&R zones get hunted with wicks. Place the stop with a buffer below support / above resistance.
  5. **Ignoring the timeframe rule.** A daily support is stronger than a 5-minute support. Trade the higher-timeframe zone first.
  6. **Treating broken levels as still valid.** Once price closes through a level, it flips polarity — old support becomes resistance and vice versa. Treat the broken level as opposite-direction support / resistance now.

#### FAQs about Support and Resistance Trading

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about support and resistance.

**What is support in trading?**

A horizontal price zone where price has historically bounced upward. Traders expect the bearish trend to retrace or reverse from a support zone.

**What is resistance in trading?**

A horizontal price zone where price has historically been rejected downward. Traders expect the bullish trend to retrace or reverse from a resistance zone.

**What are the four pillars of S &R identification?**

(1) Historical price reaction — multiple bounces strengthen the zone. (2) Timeframe — higher timeframes produce stronger zones. (3) Freshness — newer levels are more reliable than older ones. (4) Psychology — round numbers (100s, 1000s) draw more order flow.

**Should I mark S &R as a line or a zone?**

A zone is generally better than a single line. Price rarely reacts at the exact pip — the zone gives room for the reaction to set up.

**How is support and resistance different from supply and demand?**

S&R are horizontal historical-reaction levels. Supply and demand are directional zones identified by the candle pattern that produced the move. Every S&D zone acts as S&R, but not every S&R is a S&D zone.

**Where do I enter the trade?**

After price reaches the S&R zone and a confirmation candle prints (bullish at support, bearish at resistance) — or after a lower-timeframe MSS triggers in the bias direction.

**Where do I place the stop loss?**

Below the support zone for buy trades, above the resistance zone for sell trades, with a small buffer for wick hunts.

**Where do I take profit?**

The next bias-opposite S&R level — the next resistance for a buy trade, the next support for a sell trade.

**What timeframe is best for S &R?**

Higher timeframes (daily, 4-hour) for marking the strongest zones. Lower timeframes (15-minute, 5-minute) for the entry trigger.

**How does S &R fit in the ICT framework?**

S&R is the price-target layer; ICT structure (BOS, CHOCH, MSS) is the entry layer. Use higher-timeframe S&R aligned with a PD array, then take the lower-timeframe MSS trigger.

**What happens when a level breaks?**

It flips polarity — broken support becomes resistance, broken resistance becomes support. Retests of the flipped level are often very tradeable.

**Does S &R work on indices and gold?**

Yes — NQ, ES and XAU/USD all respect horizontal S&R, especially during the New York AM session and around US economic releases.

**Can I trade with S &R alone?**

Yes, but the hit rate improves significantly when paired with trend alignment, candlestick confirmation, or lower-timeframe MSS at the zone.
