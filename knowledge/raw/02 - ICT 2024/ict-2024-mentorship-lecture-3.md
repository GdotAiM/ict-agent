In this third lecture of the ICT 2024 Mentorship Program, we look into the characteristics of the ICT New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) and the New Week Opening Gap (NWOG).

These gaps often act as powerful magnets for price action, either serving as a draw-on-liquidity or aligning with a PD array.

Understanding how these gaps influence market behaviour is crucial for traders aiming to refine their strategies.

This session specifically emphasises the significance of observing market movements during the afternoon and post-New York lunch hour — periods when these gaps often play a pivotal role in price dynamics.

#### Lecture 3 Quick Summary

ICT 2024 Mentorship Lecture 3 introduces the NDOG and NWOG as the strongest inefficiencies on the chart — stronger than relative equal highs and lows. Keep at least four NDOGs annotated. Trade the AM-session at 07:00 AM, 08:00 AM and 09:00 AM (NY local) when price approaches an NDOG. If there is no market-driver news in the morning, expect a rangy day with false break-outs and trade after the New York lunch hour — between 01:30 PM and 02:30 PM NY local — when an OTE setup typically prints.

#### Key Elements (Definitions)

**(I) New Day Opening Gap :** abbreviated as [NDOG](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-new-day-opening-gap-ndog/>) — it is the gap between the closing price at 05:00 PM (New York local time) and the opening price at 06:00 PM (New York local time).

**(II) New Week Opening Gap :** abbreviated as [NWOG](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-new-week-opening-gap-nwog/>) — it is the gap between the closing price on Friday at 05:00 PM (New York local time) and the opening price on Monday at 06:00 PM (New York local time).

**(III)[Market Structure Shift](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-market-structure-shift/>) :** is the initial change in the delivery of price which indicates a short-term change in trend.

**(IV)[Fair Value Gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy/>) :** is a three-candle formation having a gap between the 1st and 3rd candle’s wicks.

**(V)[Inverse Fair Value Gap](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-inversion-fair-value-gap/>) :** is a failed fair value gap which works inversely.

**(VI)[Breaker Block](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-breaker-block-trading/>) :** is a failed order block.

**(VII)[Liquidity Sweep](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-liquidity-sweep-vs-liquidity-run/>) :** is the phenomenon of hunting liquidity above highs and below lows.

**(VIII) Relative Equal Highs :** is when a high has a lower swing high on the right side of it formed due to price swing failure.

**(IX) Relative Equal Lows :** is when a swing low has a higher swing low on the right side of it formed due to price swing failure.

**(X)[Consequent Encroachment](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-consequent-encroachment/>) :** is the 50% retracement level of a PD array.

#### Application in the Market

New Day Opening Gaps and New Week Opening Gaps are the strongest inefficiencies and draw-on-liquidity levels — stronger than relative equal highs and lows.

You need to have at least four NDOGs on your chart and they act as a magnet for price by drawing price to themselves.

An NDOG can tell you where price is likely to draw even before the formation of relative equal highs and lows, and you can even predict relative equal highs/lows by using these gaps.

The morning session demands more expertise and the afternoon session is relatively easier. If there is no market-driver news in the AM-session, expect a rangy day with false breakouts and price trading back to range.

#### (I) Bearish Bias

If you have a single or multiple New Day Opening Gaps above price and your [bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-explained/>) is bearish, then wait for price to get to those NDOGs at 07:00 AM, 08:00 AM or 09:00 AM (New York local time).

When price approaches these NDOGs, look for an ICT MSS to the sell side. After the MSS, when price retraces back, look for a sell trade at the breaker block or the IFVG.

Your stop loss in this case will be above the high formed post-07:00 AM liquidity hunt. For take profit you can target the next draw-on-liquidity, such as relative equal lows or the previous session low.

If the New Day Opening Gap is below price and your bias is bearish, then this NDOG will act as the draw-on-liquidity and price will draw toward it.

![ICT 2024 Mentorship Lecture 3 bearish setup — NDOG above price acts as the draw, MSS to the sell side after the 07:00 AM tap and short entry at the breaker or IFVG](https://innercircletrader.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/ict-2024-mentorshiplecture-3-1.png)

#### (II) Bullish Bias

If you have a single or multiple New Day Opening Gaps below price and your [bias](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-daily-bias-explained/>) is bullish, then wait for price to get to those NDOGs at 07:00 AM, 08:00 AM or 09:00 AM (New York local time).

When price approaches these NDOGs, look for an ICT MSS to the buy side. After the MSS, when price retraces back, look for a buy trade at the breaker block or the IFVG.

Your stop loss in this case will be below the low formed post-07:00 AM liquidity hunt. For take profit you can target the next draw-on-liquidity, such as relative equal highs or the previous session high.

If the New Day Opening Gap is above price and your bias is bullish, then this NDOG will act as the draw-on-liquidity and price will draw toward it.

#### Note — When Morning is Quiet, Trade the Afternoon

If there is no market-driver news in the morning then look for the trading opportunity in the afternoon session — preferably after the New York lunch hour at 01:30 PM (New York local time).

Between 01:30 PM and 02:30 PM (NY local time) you can get an [ICT Optimal Trade Entry](<https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-optimal-trade-entry-ote-pattern/>).

#### Step-by-Step Lecture 3 Trade Flow

This is the exact sequence Michael Huddleston walks through in Lecture 3, condensed into a checklist.

  1. **Annotate at least four NDOGs.** Mark the 05:00 PM close to 06:00 PM open gap on the daily chart for the last four sessions.
  2. **Annotate the active NWOG.** Friday 05:00 PM close to Monday 06:00 PM open.
  3. **Set the daily bias.** Bullish, bearish or neutral on the daily and H4.
  4. **Identify the relevant NDOG for the day.** NDOGs above price are draws-on-liquidity in a bearish bias; NDOGs below price are draws in a bullish bias.
  5. **Wait for the AM-session tap.** Price must approach the NDOG at 07:00 AM, 08:00 AM or 09:00 AM NY local time.
  6. **Wait for the MSS.** A clean Market Structure Shift in the direction of the bias confirms intent.
  7. **Mark the IFVG and breaker.** The Inverse Fair Value Gap and the breaker block formed after the MSS become the entry zones.
  8. **Enter on the retracement.** Take the trade at the breaker block or the IFVG (the original lecture lists IFVG and breaker block — pick the one closest to the MSS).
  9. **Set the stop.** Above the high formed post-07:00 AM liquidity hunt (shorts) or below the low formed post-07:00 AM liquidity hunt (longs).
  10. **Take profit at the next draw on liquidity.** Relative equal levels or prior session high/low.
  11. **If the AM is quiet, switch to PM.** Look for the OTE setup between 01:30 PM and 02:30 PM NY local time.

#### Best Markets for the Lecture 3 Model

The model is built around the New York open and afternoon, so it works best on instruments active during those windows.

  * **NQ (NASDAQ futures)** and **ES (S &P 500 futures)** — daily NDOGs are highly visible on US index charts and the AM/PM session structure is the most natural fit.
  * **GBP/USD** and **EUR/USD** — the AM-session NDOG sweep often provides the cleanest forex setup of the day.
  * **XAU/USD (Gold)** — afternoon-session OTE setups around 01:30 to 02:30 PM ET deliver clean reactions, especially on news-driven days.

**For traders in the United States** who follow the CFTC FIFO and no-hedge rules, NQ and ES on the 1-minute chart are the most natural fit for the Lecture 3 NDOG model. The post-lunch OTE window from 01:30 to 02:30 PM ET aligns directly with the NYSE-open afternoon flow on US futures and CME Group instruments.

#### Common Mistakes on the Lecture 3 Model

These are the recurring mistakes I see when traders first try to apply the Lecture 3 framework.

  1. **Annotating only one NDOG.** Lecture 3 explicitly calls for at least four NDOGs on the chart. Older NDOGs continue to react.
  2. **Wrong NDOG-to-bias pairing.** NDOGs above price draw bearish setups; NDOGs below price draw bullish setups. Reversing the pairing inverts the entire trade.
  3. **Forcing AM trades on a quiet morning.** If there is no market-driver news in the morning, the lecture explicitly tells you to wait for the afternoon. Trading a rangy AM produces chop entries.
  4. **Skipping the MSS.** Without the lower-timeframe Market Structure Shift, the NDOG tap is just a wick — not a tradeable reversal.
  5. **Stop too tight.** The stop must sit beyond the entire post-07:00 AM swing — not just at the MSS pivot.
  6. **Missing the 01:30 PM window.** The OTE setup between 01:30 and 02:30 PM is one of the most reliable in the framework. Ignoring it means missing the best second-chance entry of the day.

#### FAQs about ICT 2024 Mentorship Lecture 3

Brief answers to the questions readers ask most often about Lecture 3.

**What does ICT 2024 Mentorship Lecture 3 cover?**

Lecture 3 introduces the New Day Opening Gap (NDOG) and the New Week Opening Gap (NWOG) as the strongest inefficiencies on the chart, walks through the AM-session model at 07:00 AM, 08:00 AM and 09:00 AM, and covers the post-lunch OTE setup between 01:30 PM and 02:30 PM NY local time.

**What is an NDOG?**

NDOG stands for New Day Opening Gap — the gap between the closing price at 05:00 PM and the opening price at 06:00 PM, both in New York local time.

**What is an NWOG according to Lecture 3?**

NWOG stands for New Week Opening Gap — Lecture 3 defines it as the gap between the closing price on Friday at 05:00 PM and the opening price on Monday at 06:00 PM, both in New York local time.

**How many NDOGs should I annotate?**

Lecture 3 calls for at least four NDOGs on the chart at all times. Older NDOGs continue to react when price returns to them.

**How do I trade a bearish NDOG setup?**

With a bearish bias and the NDOG above price, wait for price to tap the NDOG at 07:00, 08:00 or 09:00 AM NY local. Wait for an MSS to the sell side, then enter on a retest of the breaker block or the IFVG. Stop above the post-07:00 AM swing high; target the next draw-on-liquidity.

**How do I trade a bullish NDOG setup?**

With a bullish bias and the NDOG below price, wait for price to tap the NDOG at 07:00, 08:00 or 09:00 AM NY local. Wait for an MSS to the buy side, then enter on a retest of the breaker block or the IFVG. Stop below the post-07:00 AM swing low; target the next draw-on-liquidity.

**What is the post-lunch OTE setup?**

If the morning is quiet (no market-driver news), the lecture instructs traders to wait for the afternoon session. Between 01:30 PM and 02:30 PM NY local time an Optimal Trade Entry typically prints — that is the second-chance setup for the day.

**What if the NDOG is below price and bias is bearish?**

The NDOG below price acts as a draw-on-liquidity in a bearish bias — price will draw toward it. The trade plan is then to wait for price to reach the NDOG before considering a new entry on the next day’s tap.

**What is the difference between NDOG and NWOG?**

NDOG is the daily gap (05:00 PM close to 06:00 PM open). NWOG is the weekly gap (Friday 05:00 PM close to Monday 06:00 PM open per Lecture 3). Both act as magnets for price, but the NWOG is the higher-tier reference.
