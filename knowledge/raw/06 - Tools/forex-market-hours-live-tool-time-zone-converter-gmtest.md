## What Are Forex Market Hours?

Forex market hours are the windows when the global currency market is open and you can place trades. The market is decentralized — there is no single exchange that opens and closes — so it runs as a relay between four major financial centers around the world: **Sydney, Tokyo, London, and New York**.

Each of these centers takes a turn as the lead market for several hours, and together they keep forex open continuously from Sunday evening to Friday evening (Eastern Time). The weekend is the only multi-hour break in a normal trading week.

Here is the simplest way to think about it:

  * **One global market** , traded electronically through brokers and banks.
  * **Four sessions** — Sydney, Tokyo, London, New York — that overlap and hand off to each other.
  * **5 days a week** (Monday to Friday in most parts of the world).
  * **24 hours a day** while the week is in progress.

## What Time Does the Forex Market Open?

The forex market opens once a week, then stays continuously open until Friday evening. The single weekly open is what most traders are asking about when they search for “what time does the forex market open.”

**Weekly Open:** Sunday 5:00 PM EST (10:00 PM UTC) — Sydney session starts.

**Weekly Close:** Friday 5:00 PM EST (10:00 PM UTC) — New York session ends.

After the Sunday open, each session takes over from the previous one in this order:

Sequence| Session| Opens at (EST)| Opens at (UTC)  
---|---|---|---  
1| Sydney| Sunday 5:00 PM| Sunday 10:00 PM  
2| Tokyo| Sunday 7:00 PM| Monday 12:00 AM  
3| London| Monday 3:00 AM| Monday 8:00 AM  
4| New York| Monday 8:00 AM| Monday 1:00 PM  
  
The same four-session pattern repeats every weekday until Friday evening, when the market closes for the weekend.

## Forex Market Hours on Sunday — How the Weekly Open Works

A lot of new traders get confused by the Sunday open, because the same moment looks like a different weekday depending on where you live. Here is what is actually happening.

The forex market opens at a single global instant — **Sunday 5:00 PM Eastern Time** (which is the same as 10:00 PM UTC). At that moment:

  * It is still **Sunday evening** in New York and most of the Americas.
  * It is already **Monday morning** in Sydney.
  * It is **Monday 1:00 AM** in London (GMT).
  * It is **Monday 2:30 AM** in Karachi (Pakistan).
  * It is **Monday 3:00 AM** in New Delhi (India).

So when you read that “forex opens on Sunday,” that refers to the New York calendar. In your part of the world, the market may technically open on Monday morning. The tool at the top of this page handles this automatically — it shows the local-time equivalent for your time zone.

## Forex Market Hours in GMT and UTC

GMT (Greenwich Mean Time) and UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) are practically the same time for forex traders. Both are fixed year-round — they do not change with daylight saving — which makes UTC the simplest reference for international traders.

### UTC / GMT Session Times

---|---|---|---  
Sydney| 22:00 (10:00 PM)| 07:00 next day| 9 hours

If you anchor your trading routine to UTC, you can ignore daylight saving entirely. The session open and close times above stay the same all year. Only the local-time labels on your broker platform shift twice a year when DST starts and ends.

## How Daylight Saving Time (DST) Affects Forex Market Hours

Daylight Saving Time (DST) is the practice of moving clocks one hour forward during the warmer months to extend evening daylight. The United States and parts of Europe observe DST roughly from **March to November**.

DST affects forex market timings by shifting the local-time labels of the London and New York sessions:

Session| Standard Time (Nov – Mar)| Daylight Saving Time (Mar – Nov)  
---|---|---  
New York opens| 8:00 AM EST| 9:00 AM EDT  
London opens| 3:00 AM EST (8:00 AM GMT)| 4:00 AM EDT (9:00 AM BST)  
  
Sydney and Tokyo do not observe DST the same way, so their UTC-based timings are mostly stable year-round. The tool at the top of this page automatically detects DST and shows the correct local times — no manual adjustment needed.

## The Four Forex Sessions Explained

Each of the four sessions has its own character — different pairs move, different news drives the action, and trading volume looks very different. Here is a beginner-friendly summary of what to expect from each one.

### Sydney Session

Opens| 22:00 UTC / 5:00 PM EST  
---|---  
Closes| 07:00 UTC next day / 2:00 AM EST  
Most active pairs| AUD/USD, NZD/USD, AUD/JPY  
Volatility| Generally the lowest of the four sessions  
  
Sydney is the first session of the trading week. It usually produces tighter ranges than London or New York, which makes it less popular for short-term traders. Australian and New Zealand economic data releases (RBA, RBNZ) happen during this window.

### Tokyo Session

Opens| 00:00 UTC / 7:00 PM EST  
---|---  
Closes| 09:00 UTC / 4:00 AM EST  
Most active pairs| USD/JPY, EUR/JPY, AUD/JPY  
Volatility| Low-to-moderate  
  
Tokyo is sometimes called the “Asian session.” Japanese economic data and BoJ-related flow concentrate here. Volatility is usually higher than Sydney but lower than London or New York.

### London Session

Opens| 08:00 UTC / 3:00 AM EST  
---|---  
Closes| 17:00 UTC / 12:00 PM EST  
Most active pairs| EUR/USD, GBP/USD, EUR/GBP  
Volatility| High  
  
London is the largest forex trading center in the world by daily volume. Major European data releases and central-bank decisions happen here, and the session covers the entire European business day. This is where most of the daily range on EUR/USD and GBP/USD is typically set.

### New York Session

Opens| 13:00 UTC / 8:00 AM EST  
---|---  
Closes| 22:00 UTC / 5:00 PM EST  
Most active pairs| EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CAD, XAU/USD  
Volatility| High — peaks during the London overlap  
  
New York is the second-largest forex center after London. US economic data releases (NFP, CPI, FOMC) drive the heaviest volatility of the week here. The first four hours of the New York session overlap with the last four hours of the London session — and that overlap is the busiest window in global forex.

## Session Overlaps and the Best Times to Trade

The interesting thing about a 24-hour relay market is that two sessions are sometimes open at the same time. Those overlap windows usually produce the biggest moves of the day because more market participants are active simultaneously.

Overlap| EST Time| UTC Time| Why It Matters  
---|---|---|---  
Sydney – Tokyo| 7:00 PM – 2:00 AM| 12:00 AM – 7:00 AM| Asian pairs (AUD, NZD, JPY) most active  
Tokyo – London| 3:00 AM – 4:00 AM| 8:00 AM – 9:00 AM| Short one-hour transition window  
**London – New York**| **8:00 AM – 12:00 PM**| **13:00 – 17:00**| **Highest volatility globally — most popular window**  
  
The **London–New York overlap** is the four-hour window where the world’s two largest financial centers are open at the same time. It is the most popular execution window for short-term and intraday traders because EUR/USD, GBP/USD, gold (XAU/USD), and the major US indices typically produce their biggest range moves here.

If you can only trade one window per day, this is the highest-leverage four hours globally.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is forex market timing?

The forex market operates around the clock globally, but for retail traders, trading is available from Sunday at 5:00 PM Eastern Time (ET) until Friday at 5:00 PM ET — a five-day window each week with a break over the weekend.

### What time does the forex market open?

The forex market opens every Sunday at 5:00 PM Eastern Time (ET), which is 10:00 PM UTC. That is the start of the Sydney session and the start of the weekly trading cycle. The market then stays continuously open until Friday at 5:00 PM ET.

### What time does the forex market open on Sunday?

5:00 PM Eastern Time on Sunday (10:00 PM UTC). In your local time: 1:00 AM Monday in London (GMT), 3:00 AM Monday in Pakistan, 3:30 AM Monday in India, 8:00 AM Monday in Sydney. Use the tool at the top of this page for an exact local-time conversion.

### Is the forex market open right now?

If it is currently Monday through Friday, yes — the market is almost certainly open. The only exceptions are the weekend window (Friday 5:00 PM EST to Sunday 5:00 PM EST), Christmas Day, and New Year’s Day. The status badges on the live tool at the top of this page give you the precise answer for the current moment.

### What time does the forex market close?

Friday at 5:00 PM Eastern Time (ET), which is 10:00 PM UTC. The New York session closes at that moment, and no major session opens again until Sunday 5:00 PM ET. The market is closed for approximately 48 hours every weekend.

### Is the forex market open 24 hours?

Yes — while a single financial center’s business day is shorter, the overlap between Sydney, Tokyo, London, and New York keeps the market continuously open from Sunday evening to Friday evening (ET). The market is closed only on weekends and two public holidays per year (Christmas and New Year’s Day).

### What are the forex market hours in GMT?

Sydney 22:00–07:00, Tokyo 00:00–09:00, London 08:00–17:00, New York 13:00–22:00 — all in UTC / GMT (year-round, unaffected by DST). The full GMT table is in the “Forex Market Hours in GMT and UTC” section above.

### What time does the Sydney session open in GMT?

22:00 UTC / GMT (10:00 PM). The Sydney session is the first session of the weekly trading cycle and opens every Sunday at 22:00 UTC.

### What time does the Tokyo session open?

The Tokyo session opens at 00:00 UTC, which is 7:00 PM EST or 9:00 AM Tokyo local time. It is the second session of the trading cycle, opening two hours after Sydney.

### What time does the London session open in EST?

3:00 AM EST during standard time (November to March) and 4:00 AM EDT during daylight saving (March to November). In UTC it is always 08:00.

### What time does the New York session open?

8:00 AM EST during standard time and 9:00 AM EDT during daylight saving. In UTC it is always 13:00. The New York session overlaps with the London session for the first four hours, which is the highest-volume period of the trading day.

### Which forex session is best for trading?

The London–New York overlap (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM ET) usually brings the highest volatility and trading volume, making it the most popular window for short-term traders. For Asian pairs (AUD, NZD, JPY), the Tokyo session is most active.

### Which session is best for trading EUR/USD?

The London session (3:00 AM – 12:00 PM EST) and the London–New York overlap. EUR/USD sees its largest range moves when London is open, because London is the world’s largest forex trading center and European data releases drive the pair.

### Which session is best for trading USD/JPY?

Tokyo and New York. Tokyo because Japanese economic activity and BoJ-related flow concentrate there; New York because the USD leg is most active during US business hours. USD/JPY is one of the few major pairs with strong activity in both Asian and Western sessions.

### Which session is best for trading AUD/USD and NZD/USD?

The Sydney and Tokyo sessions. Australian and New Zealand economic data releases happen during these hours, which produces the largest range moves on the Aussie and Kiwi pairs.

### Why does the forex market close on weekends?

Because the major banks and financial institutions that provide liquidity in the institutional market close their dealing desks for the weekend. Retail brokers depend on that wholesale liquidity, so they also close. The market reopens on Sunday evening (EST) when Sydney comes online again.

### Does daylight saving time change forex market hours?

Local-time labels shift, but UTC times stay constant year-round. For example, the New York session opens at 13:00 UTC year-round, but the local label changes from 8:00 AM EST (winter) to 9:00 AM EDT (summer). The tool at the top of this page adjusts automatically when DST is active.

### What is the London–New York overlap?

The four-hour window from 8:00 AM to 12:00 PM EST (13:00 to 17:00 UTC) when the London and New York sessions are both open. It is the highest-volatility and highest-volume period of the global trading day, especially for EUR/USD, GBP/USD, XAU/USD and major US indices.
